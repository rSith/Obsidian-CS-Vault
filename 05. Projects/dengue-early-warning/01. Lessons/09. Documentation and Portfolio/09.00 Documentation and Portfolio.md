## What this lesson is about
A project that nobody can understand has, for practical purposes, not been done. The goal of this phase is that **a lecturer, a recruiter or a health official can understand what you built, why it works and where it stops working, within five minutes.**

Four things to produce:
1. a **README** with the problem, a screenshot, an architecture diagram, how to run it, the results and the limitations;
2. a **technical report** of 8–15 pages, built like a paper;
3. a **demo video** of 3–5 minutes;
4. a **public post and a talk**, and a request for feedback from a domain expert.

And one thing to think through carefully: what it means to build an AI tool for **health**.

## What a strong finish looks like
| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "The model achieved good accuracy." | "The model's error was 25 % lower than the persistence baseline at 4 weeks, and 6 % of that came from weather features." |
| Only the districts where it worked | Every district, with the missed outbreaks named and explained |
| "Future work: improve the model." | "Future work: add serotype data, which would address the 2026 miss in Kandy." |
| No limitations section | Reporting delay, under-reporting, district-centre weather, unusual years |

Every number above is a placeholder for your own results.
