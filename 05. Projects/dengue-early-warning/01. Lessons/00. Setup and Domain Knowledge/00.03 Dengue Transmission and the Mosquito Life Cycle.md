# 00.03 · Dengue Transmission and the Mosquito Life Cycle

> [!info] [[00.00 Setup and Domain Knowledge|Lesson 0]] · Roadmap Phase 0 · Priority 🔴 Core
> **Used for:** deciding which weather features to build and how many weeks to lag them. This note is the reason the whole project can work.

## 1. Definition
> [!note] Dengue
> **Dengue** is a viral infection caused by the **dengue virus (DENV)**. It passes to people through the bite of an infected female ***Aedes*** mosquito, mainly *Aedes aegypti* and also *Aedes albopictus*.

- The virus has **four serotypes**: DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3 and DENV-4.
- A mosquito that carries a disease from one person to another is called a **vector**.
- Dengue does **not** spread directly from person to person. The mosquito is always in the middle.

## 2. Why this matters for the project
The model predicts cases from weather. That only makes sense if weather really drives mosquito numbers, and if there is a delay between the weather and the cases. Both are true, and the delay is the **early-warning window**.

## 3. How transmission works
```text
Infected person ──bite──▶ Mosquito ──(virus multiplies)──▶ infectious mosquito ──bite──▶ New person ──▶ symptoms
                              extrinsic incubation                                intrinsic incubation
                                 about 8–12 days                                    about 4–10 days
```

| Step | What happens | Typical time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A mosquito bites a person who has the virus in their blood | |
| 2 | **Extrinsic incubation period (EIP):** the virus multiplies inside the mosquito until the mosquito can pass it on | About 8–12 days at 25–28 °C; shorter when it is warmer |
| 3 | The mosquito bites other people. It stays infectious for the rest of its life | |
| 4 | **Intrinsic incubation period:** time from the bite to the first symptoms in the person | About 4–10 days |

## 4. The mosquito life cycle
```text
Egg ──▶ Larva ──▶ Pupa ──▶ Adult
(needs water to hatch)   (all three live in water)   (flies and bites)
```
- *Aedes* lays eggs on the inside walls of **containers that hold water**: tyres, flower pots, blocked gutters, coconut shells, water tanks.
- From egg to adult takes roughly **8–10 days** in warm conditions.
- Eggs can survive **dry for months** and hatch when rain fills the container again.
- *Aedes* bites mostly in the **daytime**, and lives close to houses.

## 5. Why weather comes first
| Weather | Effect on the mosquito and virus |
|---|---|
| **Rainfall** | Fills containers, so there are more places to breed. Very heavy rain can also wash larvae away, so more rain does not always mean more mosquitoes. |
| **Temperature** | Warmth speeds up the mosquito's growth, shortens the EIP and makes mosquitoes bite more often. Extreme heat kills them. |
| **Humidity** | Adult mosquitoes live longer in humid air, so more of them survive long enough to become infectious. |

## 6. Why cases come weeks after the weather
The delays add up:

| Delay | Rough length |
|---|---|
| Rain collects, eggs hatch, larvae grow into adults | 1–2 weeks |
| Adults bite infected people; the virus multiplies in the mosquito (EIP) | 1–2 weeks |
| A newly bitten person develops symptoms | about 1 week |
| The person visits a doctor, the case is notified and counted | 1 week or more |
| Several rounds of this chain before case numbers are clearly high | more weeks |

Studies usually find that cases follow rainfall by about **one to three months**, and the exact number differs between places. You do not assume the lag: you **measure it per district** in Phase 2 with [[02.04 Cross-Correlation and Lag Analysis]].

## 7. What weather cannot explain
> [!warning] Limits of a weather model
> - **Serotype changes.** After infection a person is immune to that serotype for life, but not to the others. A second infection with a different serotype has a higher risk of severe dengue. When a serotype that few people are immune to starts spreading, cases can jump with ordinary weather. Sri Lanka's very large 2017 epidemic was linked to DENV-2.
> - **Population immunity**, **people travelling** between districts, **mosquito-control campaigns** and **changes in reporting**.
>
> None of these is in the weather data, so the model will miss some outbreaks. That is a limitation to state, not to hide.

## 8. Common mistakes
- **"More rain always means more dengue."** The link is not a straight line, and very heavy rain can reduce breeding.
- **Using the same week's weather to predict the same week's cases.** Biology needs weeks. Use lagged weather.
- **Treating correlation as proof of cause.** Rain and cases both follow the seasons, so they correlate even without a direct link.
- **Mixing up the two incubation periods.** Extrinsic is in the mosquito; intrinsic is in the human.

## 9. Exam and viva focus
- **Vector:** female *Aedes aegypti* (main) and *Aedes albopictus*.
- **Four serotypes**; lifelong immunity to one, not to the others.
- **EIP (in the mosquito, about 8–12 days) vs intrinsic incubation (in the human, about 4–10 days).**
- **Why do cases lag weather?** Breeding time + EIP + human incubation + reporting.
- **Why is weather alone not enough?** Serotypes, immunity, movement, control, reporting.
