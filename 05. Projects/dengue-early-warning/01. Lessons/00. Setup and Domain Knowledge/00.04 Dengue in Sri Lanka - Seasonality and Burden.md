# 00.04 · Dengue in Sri Lanka: Seasonality and Burden

> [!info] [[00.00 Setup and Domain Knowledge|Lesson 0]] · Roadmap Phase 0 · Priority 🔴 Core
> **Used for:** knowing what "normal" looks like in each district before you ask a model to find what is unusual.

## 1. Definition
> [!note] Seasonality
> **Seasonality** is a pattern that repeats at the same time every year. Dengue in Sri Lanka is seasonal because the rains are seasonal.

> [!note] Burden
> **Burden** means how much disease a place carries: the number of cases, or cases per 100,000 people.

## 2. The two monsoons
| Period | Months | Where it rains most |
|---|---|---|
| First inter-monsoon | March – April | Scattered afternoon thunderstorms |
| **South-west monsoon** | May – September | South-west: Colombo, Gampaha, Kalutara, Galle, Ratnapura |
| Second inter-monsoon | October – November | Most of the island |
| **North-east monsoon** | December – February | North and east: Jaffna, Trincomalee, Batticaloa |

## 3. The two dengue peaks
Because cases follow rain by weeks ([[00.03 Dengue Transmission and the Mosquito Life Cycle]]), the country usually sees **two peaks a year**:
1. a larger peak around **June – July**, after the south-west monsoon begins;
2. a second peak around **December – January**, after the second inter-monsoon and north-east monsoon rains.

> [!warning] Districts do not share one calendar
> A wet-zone district such as Colombo peaks mid-year. A dry-zone district in the north or east gets most of its rain at the end of the year and tends to peak then. This is why lags and seasonal patterns are worked out **per district**, not once for the whole country.

## 4. Where the burden is
- The **Western Province** (Colombo, Gampaha, Kalutara) carries about half of all cases in most years. It is the most crowded and most urban part of the country, and *Aedes aegypti* thrives in cities.
- News reports of National Dengue Control Unit figures put the Western Province at about **53 %** of 2026 cases by 26 August 2026, with Gampaha (about 20,200) and Colombo (about 18,800) the two highest districts.
- Kandy, Kalutara, Galle, Matara and Ratnapura are usually among the next highest.

## 5. Years to know
| Year | Reported cases (approximate) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | about 186,000 | Largest epidemic on record, linked to DENV-2. An extreme value that can distort averages. |
| 2019 | about 105,000 | Another very high year. |
| 2020 – 2021 | far below 2019 | COVID-19 restrictions reduced movement and disrupted reporting. Low for reasons unrelated to weather. |
| 2026 | 101,744 by 6 October | A large epidemic year in progress (National Dengue Control Unit daily update). |

These numbers are for orientation. Use your own downloaded data for anything you report.

## 6. How cases are reported
- Dengue is a **notifiable disease**: doctors must report suspected cases.
- The **Epidemiology Unit** publishes the Weekly Epidemiological Report (WER). The **National Dengue Control Unit** publishes daily and weekly updates.
- Reports list **26 reporting units**, not 25 districts, because Kalmunai is reported separately from Ampara.
- Counts are of **notified** cases. Mild cases that never see a doctor are not counted, so true infections are higher.

## 7. What this means for the model
| Fact | Design choice |
|---|---|
| Two peaks a year | Add week-of-year features: [[03.04 Cyclical and One-Hot Encoding]] |
| Districts peak at different times | Compute lags and "normal" levels per district |
| 2017 and 2020–21 are unusual | Flag them with a column; do not delete them: [[02.02 Data Quality Checks and Missing Data]] |
| Colombo has far more cases than small districts | Compare cases per 100,000 people, or use scaled errors: [[05.01 Forecast Error Metrics - MAE and RMSE]] |
| 26 units vs 25 districts | Decide the mapping in Phase 1 and write it in the data dictionary |

## 8. Common mistakes
- **One seasonal pattern for the whole country.** It hides the north and east.
- **Comparing raw counts between districts.** Use cases per 100,000 people.
- **Calling 2020–21 "normal".** A baseline built on those years is too low and makes later years look like constant outbreaks.
- **Treating notified cases as all infections.**

## 9. Exam and viva focus
- **Two monsoons** (south-west May–September, north-east December–February) and **two dengue peaks** (about June–July and December–January).
- **Highest burden:** Western Province.
- **Why flag 2017 and 2020–21?** Epidemic year and disrupted years; both distort averages.
- **Notifiable disease** and **under-reporting**: define both.
