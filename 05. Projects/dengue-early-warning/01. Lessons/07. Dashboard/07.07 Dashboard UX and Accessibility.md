# 07.07 · Dashboard UX and Accessibility

> [!info] [[07.00 Dashboard|Lesson 7]] · Roadmap Phase 7 · Priority 🔴 Core
> **The theory is in your course notes and is not repeated here:** [[05.00 HCI in Web Design]] · [[09.00 Web Accessibility]] · [[04.00 Web Design Principles]]. This note **applies** it to one page with one job.
> **Used for:** the Phase 7 goal: a non-technical health officer understands the page in 30 seconds.

## 1. Definitions
> [!note] Dashboard
> A **dashboard** is a single screen that shows the most important information for a decision, arranged so it can be read at a glance.

For UI, UX, usability and accessibility themselves, see [[05.01 UI vs UX]] and [[09.01 Why Web Accessibility Matters]].

## 2. Why it matters here
The reader is a busy official, not a data scientist. If they cannot tell within half a minute **which districts need attention**, the forecasting behind the page has no effect. And a public-health tool that some people cannot read (colour-blind users, phone users, screen-reader users) fails the very people it is for.

## 3. How to design it: three questions in order
The page must answer these, in this order, without scrolling:

| # | Question | Element |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where is the risk? | The map, and a count: "3 districts at Warning" |
| 2 | How bad, and how sure? | The detail chart with its uncertainty band |
| 3 | Can I trust this, and how fresh is it? | "Last updated", sources, and the note that this is decision support |

Put the most important thing where the eye lands first (top left), and make it the largest. This is **visual hierarchy**: [[04.01 Aesthetic Layout and CARP Principles]].

Show less. Every extra number makes the important one harder to find: [[05.09 Cognitive Load Theory]].

## 4. Apply: colour
> [!warning] Never use colour alone
> About 1 in 12 men cannot tell red from green reliably. A red–amber–green map with no other cue is unreadable to them.

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Write the level as **text** in the tooltip, the table and the panel title | Works without colour |
| Add a **legend** with the words Normal, Watch, Warning | Explains the colours |
| Choose colours that differ in **lightness**, not only in hue | Still distinct in greyscale and for colour-blind users |
| Dashed line for the threshold, solid for data | A second cue besides colour |
| Text contrast of at least **4.5 : 1** against its background | The WCAG AA level for normal text |

Check the page with a colour-blindness simulator and a contrast checker. Colour meaning is covered in [[04.02 Colour Psychology and Colour Schemes]].

## 5. Apply: words
- **Plain labels.** "Expected cases in 4 weeks", not "ŷ (h=4)".
- **Say what each level means and what to do**, in one line each:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Normal | Forecast is within the usual range for this time of year |
| Watch | Forecast is above the usual range. Monitor closely. |
| Warning | Forecast is well above the usual range. Consider early action. |

- **State uncertainty in words:** "Most likely 262 cases (range 180–390)".
- **A "How to read this" box** of three or four lines.
- **Sources and date** of the data.
- **The limits, stated plainly:** "A decision-support tool. Not an official forecast. Based on reported cases, which are published late."

## 6. Apply: accessibility
| Need | What to do | Course note |
|---|---|---|
| Screen readers cannot read a map or a canvas | Keep the **table** of districts as the text version; give the map and chart an `aria-label` | [[09.03 Applying Accessible Design]] |
| Keyboard users | Use real `<button>`, `<a>` and `<select>` elements; a visible focus outline | [[09.04 Introduction to ARIA and the Five Rules]] |
| Structure | `<header>`, `<main>`, `<footer>`, one `<h1>`, headings in order | [[00.06 Semantic HTML]] |
| Small screens | One column on a phone; tap targets big enough for a finger | [[06.04 Making Pages Responsive]] |
| Slow connections | A small boundary file; a loading message | [[06.07 Web Performance Optimisation]] |
| Updates | Announce panel changes with `aria-live="polite"` | [[09.05 ARIA Roles, Properties and States]] |

## 7. Example: the test with one real person
The roadmap asks for one non-technical tester. Run it as a **think-aloud test** ([[05.10 Usability Testing Methods]]):

1. Show the page. Say nothing about how it works.
2. Give three tasks:
   - "Which districts should worry most right now?"
   - "What is expected in Kandy next month, and how sure is the system?"
   - "How recent is this information?"
3. Ask them to say what they are thinking. **Do not help.**
4. Write down every hesitation and every wrong answer.
5. Fix the top three problems, and record what you changed.

The moments where they get stuck are the findings. A tester who says "it's nice" has told you nothing.

## 8. Checklist before calling Phase 7 done
- [ ] The three questions are answered without scrolling on a laptop
- [ ] Every alert level appears as text, not only as colour
- [ ] Legend, "How to read this", sources, last-updated date and the decision-support note are visible
- [ ] The table gives the same information as the map
- [ ] Everything can be reached and operated with the keyboard
- [ ] Text contrast is at least 4.5 : 1
- [ ] The page works on a phone
- [ ] One non-technical person has tested it and their feedback is addressed

## 9. Common mistakes
- **Designing for yourself.** You know what every number means; the reader does not.
- **Red, amber and green with no text.**
- **Too much on the screen:** every metric, every model, every horizon.
- **Jargon:** MAE, quantile, horizon, endemic channel.
- **Hiding uncertainty** to look confident.
- **No date.** Old data shown as if it were current is dangerous.
- **A map that only responds to hover.** There is no hover on a phone.
- **Testing only with classmates** who already understand the project.
- **Treating accessibility as an extra** to add at the end.
