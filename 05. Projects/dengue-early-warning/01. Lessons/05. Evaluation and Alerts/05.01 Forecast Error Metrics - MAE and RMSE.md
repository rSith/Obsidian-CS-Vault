# 05.01 · Forecast Error Metrics: MAE and RMSE

> [!info] [[05.00 Evaluation and Alerts|Lesson 5]] · Roadmap Phase 5 · Priority 🔴 Core
> **Parent concept:** [[Model Evaluation]]. This note covers only the error measures used for the case forecasts.

## 1. Definitions
> [!note] Error
> The **error** of one forecast is the difference between what happened and what was predicted: $e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$.

| Metric | In words | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **MAE**, mean absolute error | The average size of the error, ignoring its direction | $\dfrac{1}{n}\sum \lvert y_i - \hat{y}_i \rvert$ |
| **RMSE**, root mean squared error | Square the errors, average them, take the square root | $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n}\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$ |
| **Bias**, mean error | The average error *with* its direction | $\dfrac{1}{n}\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)$ |

Here $y_i$ is the actual value, $\hat{y}_i$ the forecast and $n$ the number of forecasts.

## 2. Why they are used
- Both MAE and RMSE are in **cases per week**, the same unit as the data, so anyone can understand them.
- They answer different questions, and bias answers a third.

| Question | Metric |
|---|---|
| How far off is a typical forecast? | MAE |
| Are there a few very large misses? | RMSE (compare it with MAE) |
| Does the model lean too high or too low? | Bias |

## 3. How they work: a worked example
| Week | Actual $y$ | Forecast $\hat{y}$ | Error | Absolute | Squared |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 110 | −10 | 10 | 100 |
| 2 | 120 | 100 | +20 | 20 | 400 |
| 3 | 80 | 90 | −10 | 10 | 100 |
| 4 | 300 | 200 | +100 | 100 | 10,000 |
| **Sum** | | | +100 | 140 | 10,600 |

$$\text{MAE} = \frac{140}{4} = 35 \qquad \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{10{,}600}{4}} = \sqrt{2650} \approx 51.5 \qquad \text{Bias} = \frac{100}{4} = +25$$

- Three forecasts were close and one missed an outbreak by 100.
- **RMSE (51.5) is much larger than MAE (35)** because squaring makes the one big miss count far more.
- **Bias is +25:** on average the truth was 25 higher than the forecast. The model under-predicts.

> [!tip] Reading the pair
> RMSE is always at least as large as MAE. When they are close, errors are all of similar size. When RMSE is much larger, a few forecasts are badly wrong, and in this project those are probably the outbreak weeks, the ones that matter most.

## 4. Syntax
```python
import numpy as np
from sklearn.metrics import mean_absolute_error, mean_squared_error

mae = mean_absolute_error(y_val, pred)
rmse = np.sqrt(mean_squared_error(y_val, pred))
bias = np.mean(y_val - pred)

# Per district
per_district = val.groupby("district").apply(
    lambda g: mean_absolute_error(g["target"], g["pred"]))
```

## 5. Comparing districts of very different size
An MAE of 30 is excellent for Colombo and terrible for a district that sees 10 cases a week. An overall MAE is dominated by the big districts. Three fixes:

| Fix | How | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-district table** | MAE for each district separately | Always report this |
| **Skill score** | $1 - \dfrac{\text{MAE}_{\text{model}}}{\text{MAE}_{\text{baseline}}}$ | Share of the baseline's error removed; has no unit |
| **MASE** | $\dfrac{\text{MAE}_{\text{model}}}{\text{MAE}_{\text{naive}}}$ | Below 1 means better than the naive forecast |
| **Rate** | Errors in cases per 100,000 people | Comparable across districts |

Baselines are in [[04.02 Forecasting Baselines - Persistence and Seasonal]].

## 6. Why not percentage error?
**MAPE**, mean absolute percentage error, is $\dfrac{100}{n}\sum \left\lvert \dfrac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{y_i} \right\rvert$. It divides by the actual value, so:
- it is **undefined when the actual value is zero**, which happens in small districts;
- a miss of 5 when the truth is 2 counts as 250 %, swamping everything else.

Avoid it for this data.

## 7. Common mistakes
- **Reporting a metric with no baseline beside it.**
- **One overall number.** It hides the small districts.
- **MAPE with zeros in the data.**
- **Comparing errors on different scales.** If the model was trained on $\log(1 + y)$, convert predictions back before computing MAE.
- **Reporting training error.** Only validation or test error says anything about new data.
- **Using R² alone.** A high $R^2$ can come from simply following the seasons.
- **Ignoring bias.** A model that is always 25 too low will always alert late.
- **Averaging the errors of different horizons together.** Report 4, 6 and 8 weeks separately.

## 8. Exam and viva focus
- **Write the formulas** for MAE, MSE and RMSE.
- **MAE vs RMSE:** RMSE penalises large errors more because of the squaring; MAE is less affected by outliers.
- **Why take the root in RMSE?** To return to the original unit.
- **Work a small example by hand,** like the table above.
- **What does bias tell you that MAE does not?** The direction of the error.
- **Why is MAPE a poor choice when values can be zero?**
- **R² (coefficient of determination):** the share of variation explained; 1 is perfect, 0 is no better than predicting the mean.
