# 03.01 · Forecast Origin, Horizon and Target

> [!info] [[03.00 Feature Engineering|Lesson 3]] · Roadmap Phase 3 · Priority 🔴 Core
> **Used for:** turning "predict the future" into an ordinary table of inputs and answers, with every column measured from the same point in time.

## 1. Definitions
| Term | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Forecast origin** | $t$ | The week in which the forecast is made. Nothing after it may be used. |
| **Horizon** | $h$ | How many weeks ahead you predict. Here 4, later 6 and 8. |
| **Target week** | $t + h$ | The week being predicted. |
| **Target** | $y_{t+h}$ | The value to predict: cases in the target week. |
| **Features** | $X_t$ | Everything known at the origin. |

```text
        features come from here            forecast made        week predicted
 ◀───────────── past ─────────────────────────▶│                      │
────────────────────────────────────────────────●──────────────────────●────▶ time
                                             origin t              target t + h
                                                │◀──── horizon h ─────▶│
```

## 2. Why it is used
Forecasting has no ready-made "answer" column. You create one by pairing each week's known information with the case count $h$ weeks later. Once that is done, forecasting becomes ordinary [[04. Concepts/Machine Learning/Supervised Learning|Supervised Learning]] and any regression model can be used.

Fixing the origin also ends a common confusion. "Rain lag 6" means nothing until you say *six weeks before what*. In this project every lag is counted back from the **origin**.

## 3. How it works
One row of the model table = one district at one origin week.

| district | week_start (origin $t$) | rain 4 weeks before $t$ | newest cases known at $t$ | target = cases at $t + 4$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colombo | 6 Jan | 120 | 310 | **402** |
| Colombo | 13 Jan | 85 | 344 | **455** |

The model learns: *"when the situation at the origin looked like this, the count four weeks later was that."*

## 4. Syntax
```python
H = 4                                            # horizon in weeks

df = df.sort_values(["district", "week_start"])
g = df.groupby("district")

df["target"] = g["cases"].shift(-H)              # cases H weeks AFTER this row
df["target_week"] = df["week_start"] + pd.Timedelta(weeks=H)
```
- `shift(-H)` pulls a **future** value up into the row. This is correct **only for the target**.
- The last `H` weeks of each district get `NaN` as target, because their future has not happened. You cannot train on those rows. They are the rows you **predict** when the system is live.

## 5. Example: converting lags
Phase 2 measures lags back from the **case week**. Features are measured back from the **origin**. The origin is $h$ weeks before the case week, so:

$$\text{lag from origin} = \text{lag from case week} - h$$

| Finding from EDA | Horizon $h$ | Feature to build |
|---|---|---|
| Rain 8 weeks before cases | 4 | Rain 4 weeks before the origin |
| Rain 6 weeks before cases | 4 | Rain 2 weeks before the origin |
| Rain 6 weeks before cases | 8 | Rain 2 weeks **after** the origin: impossible |

When the result is negative, that weather has not happened yet. Either accept less information at long horizons, or bring in weather **forecasts**, which is a separate project.

## 6. Predicting several horizons
| Approach | How | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Train one model per horizon: one for $h=4$, one for $h=6$, one for $h=8$ | Simple; each model is tuned for its horizon; no error build-up | Several models to keep |
| **Recursive** | Train a one-week-ahead model; feed each prediction back in to step forward | One model | Errors add up at each step; needs future weather at every step |

This project uses the **direct** approach. Make `H` a parameter of the feature function so the same code builds the table for any horizon.

## 7. Adding the reporting delay
Cases are published late ([[01.06 Reporting Delay and Data Availability]]). If the delay is $d$ weeks, the newest case count known at origin $t$ is from week $t - d$. So:

- newest usable case feature: `cases` shifted by $d$;
- distance from the newest case data to the target: $d + h$ weeks.

## 8. Common mistakes
- **Counting lags from the target week in some columns and from the origin in others.** Pick the origin and stay with it.
- **A feature lag shorter than it should be**, which reaches into the future. This is [[03.03 Data Leakage]].
- **Using `shift(-H)` anywhere except the target.**
- **Deleting the last `H` rows and forgetting them.** They are the live prediction rows.
- **Building the target without `groupby("district")`.** The end of one district then takes its "future" from the start of the next.
- **Reporting a "4-week forecast" that ignores the reporting delay.** State the real distance from the data.

## 9. Exam and viva focus
- **Define** forecast origin, horizon, lead time and target.
- **How is a forecasting problem turned into supervised learning?** Pair features known at $t$ with the value at $t + h$.
- **Direct vs recursive** multi-step forecasting: one advantage and one disadvantage of each.
- **Why do the last $h$ rows have no target?** Their future is not known yet.
- **Why does skill fall as the horizon grows?** Less of the useful information exists yet, and uncertainty builds up.
