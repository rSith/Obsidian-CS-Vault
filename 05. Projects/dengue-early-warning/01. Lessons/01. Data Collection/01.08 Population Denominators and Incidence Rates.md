# 01.08 · Population Denominators and Incidence Rates

> **Part of:** [[Data Preprocessing]]. This note covers only the population side: where the denominator comes from and how to fill the years it is missing.

## 1. Definition
> [!note] Incidence rate
> The **incidence rate** is the number of new cases in a period divided by the number of people at risk, scaled to a round number of people. Here: dengue cases in one week per 100,000 people in the district.

> [!note] Denominator
> The **denominator** is the "people at risk" part: the district's population. The case count is the **numerator**.

> [!note] Mid-year population estimate
> A census counts everyone only once every ten years or so. In between, the statistics office **estimates** the population on 1 July of each year from the last census plus births, deaths and migration. These are the **mid-year estimates**.

> [!note] Census base
> The census an estimate series starts from. When a new census is taken, later estimates use the new **base**, and the series can jump in that year.

## 2. Why it is used in this project
- **Comparing districts.** 100 cases in a week is quiet for Colombo (about 2.4 million people) and a crisis for Mullaitivu (about 0.1 million). As counts the two look the same; as rates they differ 25-fold.
- **Alert thresholds** (Phase 5) are easier to read and to compare across districts as rates.
- **Mapping** (Phase 7): a map coloured by raw counts just shows where people live. A map coloured by rate shows where dengue is high.
- **Population grows.** About 1 % a year. Over 20 years that is 20 %, so the same count means slightly less in 2026 than in 2006.

## 3. How it works
```text
cases_raw.csv (unit, week_start, cases)
        │  Phase 2: Kalmunai added to Ampara → 25 districts
        ▼
weekly cases per district ──join on district and year──▶ population.csv (district, year, population)
        │
        ▼
incidence = cases / population × 100,000
```

**Where the population comes from (Sri Lanka).** The Department of Census and Statistics publishes mid-year estimates by district, from the Registrar General's Department:

| Years | Census base | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 2014–2024 | 2012 census | 2018 onwards provisional |
| 2025–2026 | 2024 census | provisional |
| 2006–2013 | — | no district table found; must be estimated |

**Why not use older official figures for 2006–2013?** They were built on the 2001 census, which could not fully count the North and East during the war. National figures show the break: 20.87 million in 2011, then 20.33 million in 2012 on the new base. Mixing the two series would put a false drop into every district in 2012.

**The chosen fill: back-extrapolation at a constant growth rate.** Take each district's average yearly growth over a calm, final period (2014–2019) and run it backwards from 2014.

## 4. Formulas and code
Incidence:

$$\text{incidence} = \frac{\text{cases}}{\text{population}} \times 100{,}000$$

Average yearly (compound) growth between two years $a$ and $b$:

$$g = \left(\frac{P_b}{P_a}\right)^{1/(b-a)} - 1$$

Backwards from 2014, for a year $y < 2014$:

$$P_y = \frac{P_{2014}}{(1+g)^{\,2014-y}}$$

```python
wide = table.pivot(index="district", columns="year", values="population")
growth = (wide[2019] / wide[2014]) ** (1 / 5) - 1          # one rate per district

for year in range(2006, 2014):
    wide[year] = (wide[2014] / (1 + growth) ** (2014 - year)).round()
```

```python
# Phase 2: weekly incidence. Population is yearly, so join on the year of week_start.
weekly["year"] = weekly["week_start"].dt.year
weekly = weekly.merge(population[["district", "year", "population"]], on=["district", "year"])
weekly["incidence"] = weekly["cases"] / weekly["population"] * 100_000
```

**Compound, not simple, growth.** Population grows on top of last year's population, like interest in a bank. Dividing the total change by the number of years (simple growth) is close for small rates but drifts over many years.

## 5. Worked example
**Rates.** In one week, Colombo and Mullaitivu each report 100 cases. These are example numbers; the populations are the 2024 estimates.

| District | Cases | Population (2024) | Incidence per 100,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colombo | 100 | 2,446,000 | 100 / 2,446,000 × 100,000 = **4.1** |
| Mullaitivu | 100 | 100,000 | 100 / 100,000 × 100,000 = **100** |

The same count is about 25 times more serious in Mullaitivu.

**Back-fill.** Colombo had 2,353,000 people in 2014 and 2,448,000 in 2019.
1. $g = (2{,}448{,}000 / 2{,}353{,}000)^{1/5} - 1 = 0.795\,\%$ a year.
2. 2013: $2{,}353{,}000 / 1.00795^{1} \approx 2{,}334{,}000$.
3. 2006: $2{,}353{,}000 / 1.00795^{8} \approx 2{,}209{,}000$.

Mullaitivu grew 0.418 % a year over 2014–2019, which gives about 91,900 for 2006. That number is the weakest in the table: the last phase of the war (2008–2009) displaced almost the whole district, so its real population in those years was nothing like a smooth curve. Case reporting there was disrupted too. Treat Mullaitivu and Kilinochchi in 2006–2009 with caution in Phase 2.

## 6. Common mistakes
| Mistake | What goes wrong | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Comparing raw counts between districts | Big districts always look worst | Compare rates |
| Leaving population in thousands | Incidence is 1,000 times too big | Multiply by 1,000 once, when the table is built |
| Mixing series from different census bases | A false jump in the base year | Use one base, or mark the base in a column and look at the jump |
| Using one population for all years | Rates drift by about 1 % a year | One value per district per year |
| Joining weekly cases on the week number | Week 53 and week-start changes (see [[01.05 Dates and Epidemiological Weeks]]) break the join | Join on the year of `week_start` |
| Back-filling from provisional or COVID years | 2020–2024 growth includes COVID and emigration, so the rates are off | Use a calm, final window such as 2014–2019 |
| Hiding which rows are estimated | Months later nobody remembers which numbers were made up | An `estimated` column |
| Giving Kalmunai its own population | It is a health region inside Ampara, not a census district | Add Kalmunai cases to Ampara, then divide by Ampara's population |

Also: a small error in the denominator matters far less than it seems. Population is off by a few percent at most; dengue cases change ten-fold between seasons. The rate is driven by the cases.

## 7. Exam and viva focus
- **Define** incidence rate, numerator, denominator, mid-year estimate, census base.
- **Why per 100,000?** It turns small fractions into readable numbers and is the standard unit in Sri Lankan and WHO dengue reports.
- **Why rates and not counts?** Districts differ 25-fold in size; counts mostly measure population.
- **What happens at a census?** The estimate series is re-based and can jump. Name the 2012 example (national figure falls by 540,000) and Mullaitivu's +23 % from 2024 to 2025.
- **How were 2006–2013 filled?** Back-extrapolation at each district's 2014–2019 compound growth rate, marked `estimated=True`. Know the formula and one weakness (wartime displacement in the North).
- **Compound vs simple growth.** Compound applies the rate to each year's new total.
