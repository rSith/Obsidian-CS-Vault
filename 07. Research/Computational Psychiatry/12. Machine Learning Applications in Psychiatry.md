# What Machine Learning Has Achieved

Back to [[00. Computational Psychiatry - Research Home|Home]] · Previous: [[11. Symptom Network Models]] · Source: paper p. 8

## 1. Diagnosis
| Task | Result reported by the paper |
|---|---|
| Schizophrenia vs healthy people | About **80–90% accuracy** |
| Depression | **Less accurate** so far |
| Schizophrenia vs bipolar disorder | Good reliability |

However, diagnosis alone **has limited use in clinics**, because doctors can usually diagnose already.

## 2. Predicting the future (more promising)
ML shows promise in predicting:
- whether a **treatment will work**,
- how well a person will **function in daily life**,
- **suicidal behaviour**,
- who may **develop a disorder** (using brain connection patterns).

## 3. Early detection (already used in practice)
This is where ML is **already used in real clinics**:
- planning resources for people with **first-episode psychosis** (a first experience of losing touch with reality),
- detecting **new cases of depression** early.

Tools are also being built to spot people at risk of psychosis or depression early.

## 4. Summary
| Use | Status |
|---|---|
| Diagnosis | Accurate for some disorders, limited clinical value |
| Predicting outcomes | Promising |
| Early detection | Already used in some places |
