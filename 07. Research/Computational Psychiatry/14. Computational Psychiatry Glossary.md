# Computational Psychiatry Glossary

Back to [[00. Computational Psychiatry - Research Home|Research Home]]

> [!info] How to read this glossary
> Every term here **appears in the paper**. 📄 means the **paper itself defines or explains** the term, and the definition follows it. Terms without 📄 are used in the paper without a definition, so the plain-English definition is **general background I wrote**, not the paper's wording. The last column links to the note where the term matters most.

## Psychiatry and clinical terms

| Term | Plain-English meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychiatry** | The medical specialty that diagnoses and treats mental disorders | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **Psychopathology** | The study of mental disorders; also used to mean the disorder itself ("developing psychopathology") | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Clinical phenomenology** | Careful description of what patients actually experience | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **Symptom** | A sign or experience that is part of a disorder (e.g. low mood, hearing voices) | throughout |
| **Syndrome** | A group of symptoms that tend to occur together | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Diagnosis / prognosis** | Diagnosis = naming the condition; prognosis = predicting how it will develop | [[01. Paper - What is Computational Psychiatry (Antonucci 2026)\|01]] |
| **Etiology (etiological)** | The causes of a disorder | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **Pathophysiology** | The biological processes that go wrong in a disease | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **DSM-5** | The American Psychiatric Association's *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders*, 5th ed. 📄 Criticised as descriptive, defining disorders by co-occurring symptoms | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **ICD-11** | The WHO's *International Classification of Diseases*, 11th revision. 📄 Same criticism as DSM-5 | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **Reliability vs validity** | 📄 Reliability = consistency; validity = capturing real causal mechanisms. Diagnostic manuals "prioritize reliability over validity" | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **RDoC** | 📄 NIMH's Research Domain Criteria: organises mental illness around functional domains and neurobehavioural constructs, not diagnostic categories | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **NIMH** | US National Institute of Mental Health | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **Dimensional framework** | Treats mental health as continuous scales instead of yes/no categories | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **Biomarker** | A measurable biological sign of a condition | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]], [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Precision psychiatry** | 📄 Tailoring interventions to a person's etiological, clinical and pathophysiological profile | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **Precision pathophysiology** | 📄 (in quotes in the paper) Understanding *how* phenomena are generated, not just describing them | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Reductionism** | 📄 (excessive) Explaining a disorder *only* through isolated biological or psychological abnormalities | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **Equifinality** | 📄 Different origins → same observable state (e.g. the same symptom) | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Multifinality** | 📄 Similar starting points → different outcomes or trajectories | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Clinical trajectory** | How a person's illness develops over time | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Depression** | Mood disorder with persistent low mood, loss of interest, pessimism, hopelessness | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **Bipolar disorder** | Mood disorder with depressive episodes and episodes of elevated mood (mania) | [[12. Machine Learning Applications in Psychiatry\|12]] |
| **Mood disorders** | Disorders mainly affecting mood (depression, bipolar disorder) | [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Schizophrenia** | Severe disorder with hallucinations, delusions and disorganised thinking | [[12. Machine Learning Applications in Psychiatry\|12]] |
| **Psychosis / first-episode psychosis** | Losing touch with reality; "first episode" = the first time it happens | [[12. Machine Learning Applications in Psychiatry\|12]] |
| **Psychotic-like experiences** | Milder, psychosis-like experiences in people without a psychotic disorder | [[12. Machine Learning Applications in Psychiatry\|12]] |
| **Hallucination** | Perceiving something that isn't there | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Delusion** | A fixed false belief held despite clear contrary evidence | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Anxiety** | Excessive worry or fear | [[06. Reinforcement Learning and the Rescorla-Wagner Model\|06]] |
| **Panic disorder / panic attack** | Recurrent sudden attacks of intense fear with strong bodily symptoms | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Addiction / substance use disorder** | Continued use of a substance despite harm | [[06. Reinforcement Learning and the Rescorla-Wagner Model\|06]], [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Personality disorders** | Long-lasting, rigid patterns of thinking and relating that cause problems | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Interoceptive disorders** | Problems in sensing the body's internal state (heartbeat, breathing, hunger) | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Anhedonia** | Reduced ability to feel pleasure or be motivated by reward | [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Negativity bias** | Giving more weight to negative than positive information | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **Maladaptive schemata** | Deep, unhelpful mental templates about self and world | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **Pessimism / hopelessness** | Expecting bad outcomes / feeling nothing will improve | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **Agitation, self-harm, aggression** | Restlessness; deliberately injuring oneself; violent behaviour. Risks monitored in hospital | [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
| **Suicidal behaviours** | Suicide attempts and related behaviours (a prediction target in ML studies) | [[12. Machine Learning Applications in Psychiatry\|12]] |
| **Relapse** | Symptoms coming back after improvement | [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
| **Inpatient / outpatient** | Treated while admitted to hospital / while living at home | [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
| **CBT** | Cognitive-behavioural therapy: talking therapy that changes unhelpful thoughts and behaviours | [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
| **Pharmacogenomics** | How a person's genes affect their response to medicines | [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
| **Neuromodulation** | Treatments that directly change brain activity (e.g. magnetic or electrical stimulation). In biology, also chemicals that adjust how neurons respond | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]], [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Healthy controls** | Participants without the disorder, used for comparison | [[12. Machine Learning Applications in Psychiatry\|12]] |
| **Psychometrics** | Designing and scoring psychological tests and questionnaires | [[02. Why Psychiatry Needs Computational Methods\|02]] |
| **Self-report** | Data people give about themselves (questionnaires, interviews) | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Childhood maltreatment** | Abuse or neglect in childhood. The paper's example of a risk factor | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |

## Modelling and maths terms

| Term | Plain-English meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Model** | 📄 A simplified representation of a complex system, not necessarily mathematical | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Statistical model** | 📄 Describes links between observed variables by formulas (e.g. linear regression); shows association, not causation | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Computational model** | 📄 A (generally complex) mathematical representation of a biological or psychological phenomenon | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Generative model** | 📄 Defines latent variables and how they generate observable data; can simulate virtual data | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Latent variable** | 📄 A hidden, not directly measurable quantity, inferred from data | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **Parameter** | 📄 A latent variable summarising the hypothesised process (e.g. slope, learning rate) | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Error (ε)** | 📄 Distance between the model's predictions and observed data | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Model fitting** | 📄 Adjusting parameters to minimise error; comparing competing models | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Overfitting** | 📄 Model too tuned to one dataset, so it fails on new data | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Generalisability** | Working on new, unseen data or populations | [[04. What is a Model\|04]], [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
| **Counterfactual** | 📄 A "what-if" scenario, e.g. predicting behaviour if a latent entity were altered | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |
| **A priori / post-hoc** | Before seeing the data / after collecting it | [[03. Theory-driven vs Data-driven Approaches\|03]] |
| **Non-linear** | Not a straight-line relationship (e.g. inverted U) | [[04. What is a Model\|04]] |
| **Dynamic system; feedback loop** | A system that changes over time; outputs that feed back to amplify (positive) or dampen (negative) themselves | [[04. What is a Model\|04]], [[11. Symptom Network Models\|11]] |
| **Reinforcement learning (RL)** | 📄 Learning from feedback by evaluating outcomes and updating choices | [[06. Reinforcement Learning and the Rescorla-Wagner Model\|06]] |
| **Prediction error (PE)** | 📄 Difference between expected and actual outcome | [[06. Reinforcement Learning and the Rescorla-Wagner Model\|06]] |
| **Rescorla–Wagner model** | 📄 $V_{t+1}=V_t+\alpha(R-V_t)$: update expectation by a fraction of the PE | [[06. Reinforcement Learning and the Rescorla-Wagner Model\|06]] |
| **Learning rate (α)** | 📄 How strongly new outcomes change expectations | [[06. Reinforcement Learning and the Rescorla-Wagner Model\|06]] |
| **Reward sensitivity (β)** | 📄 Responsiveness to the reward bias (PRT models) | [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Bandit task** | A choice task with options of unknown payoff, named after slot machines | [[06. Reinforcement Learning and the Rescorla-Wagner Model\|06]] |
| **Prior / likelihood / posterior** | 📄 Prior expectation / new evidence / updated belief | [[07. Bayesian Brain and Belief Updating\|07]] |
| **Drift diffusion model (DDM)** | 📄 Decisions as noisy evidence accumulation to one of two boundaries | [[08. Drift Diffusion Models\|08]] |
| **Drift rate (v); boundary (a); starting point (z)** | 📄 Strength of evidence; decision threshold; where accumulation starts | [[08. Drift Diffusion Models\|08]] |
| **Reaction time (RT); non-decision time** | 📄 Time to respond = non-decision time (perceptual encoding + motor execution) + decision time | [[08. Drift Diffusion Models\|08]] |
| **Two-alternative forced choice** | A task where you must pick one of two answers | [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Probabilistic Reward Task (PRT)** | 📄 Short/long-mouth face task with hidden asymmetric rewards | [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Response bias** | 📄 Systematic preference for the more-rewarded stimulus | [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Signal detection analysis (model-free)** | Measures discrimination ability separately from response bias, without a learning model | [[09. Probabilistic Reward Task - Anhedonia Case Study\|09]] |
| **Normative model** | A model of how an ideal decision-maker *should* behave | [[05. Theory-driven Computational Models\|05]] |

| Term | Plain-English meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **AI** | 📄 Systems doing tasks that normally need human intelligence | [[10. Data-driven Modelling - AI and Machine Learning\|10]] |
| **Machine learning (ML)** | 📄 Algorithms that learn patterns from data instead of being explicitly programmed | [[10. Data-driven Modelling - AI and Machine Learning\|10]] |

| **Algorithmic bias** | A model treating some groups unfairly or performing worse for them | [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
| **Wearable sensors** | Devices such as smartwatches that record physiological data continuously | [[13. Clinical Use - Promises, Challenges and Explainable AI\|13]] |
