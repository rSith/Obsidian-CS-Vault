## What this lesson is about

A regular expression **describes** a pattern; an **automaton** is the **machine that recognises** it. This lesson gives you:
- the vocabulary (alphabet, string, language, Σ*, Σ⁺),
- the formal machine (the 5-tuple, transition function δ),
- the two kinds of finite automata (**DFA** and **NDFA**),
- the two practical skills that earn most marks: **tracing** a string through a machine, and **designing** a machine from a description.

## Topic structure

```text
Automata
├── What an automaton is ("self-acting"); finite automaton / FSM; limits
├── Terminology: alphabet, string, length, empty string, language
├── Powers of Σ, Kleene star Σ*, Kleene plus Σ⁺ (+ proofs)
├── Formal definition: 5-tuple (Q, Σ, δ, q0, F) and the role of δ
├── DFA: definition, state diagram, transition table, validation rules
├── NDFA: definition, δ: Q × Σ → 2^Q, "every DFA is an NDFA"
├── DFA vs NDFA
├── Classification: FA without output (DFA, NDFA) / with output (Mealy, Moore);
│                   acceptor, classifier, transducer
├── Acceptability of strings: δ*(q0, S) ∈ F
└── Designing DFAs and NDFAs (contains / not contains / parity / starts / ends)
```
