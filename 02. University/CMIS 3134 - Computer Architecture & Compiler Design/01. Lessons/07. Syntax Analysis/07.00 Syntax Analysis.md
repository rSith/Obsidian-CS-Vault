## What this lesson is about

Syntax analysis (**parsing**) is **phase 2** of the compiler. It takes the token stream from the [[06.00 Lexical Analysis|lexical analyzer]] and checks that the tokens are **arranged according to the grammar** of the language, building a **parse tree**. Regular expressions are not powerful enough for this, so it uses **context-free grammars (CFGs)**.

## Topic structure

```text
Syntax Analysis
├── Why REs are not enough → CFG (superset of regular grammar), push-down automata
├── Context-free grammar: V, Σ, P, S; regular grammars (left/right linear)
├── Derivations: left-most and right-most
├── Parse trees
├── Ambiguity (and how to remove it: rewrite, or associativity + precedence)
├── Language generated by a grammar; proofs; "does G generate w?"
├── Associativity and precedence
├── Left recursion (immediate, indirect) and its removal; left factoring
├── FIRST and FOLLOW sets
├── Limitations of syntax analyzers (→ semantic analysis)
└── Parsing strategies: top-down / bottom-up, LL(1), LR(0) (asked in 2019/20 only; not in notes)
```

> Several past-paper grammar questions contain printing problems. For example, 2021/22 Q8c lists `S → aB | bA`, `S → a | aS | bAA`, `S → b | aS | aBB` (the left sides of the A and B rules are printed as S), and the grammar `S → aB | bA, A → aS | bAA | ε, B → b | bS | aBB` cannot generate the strings it is asked about. Each case is handled carefully, with proofs, in [[PP 07.1 - Grammars, Derivations and Ambiguity]].
