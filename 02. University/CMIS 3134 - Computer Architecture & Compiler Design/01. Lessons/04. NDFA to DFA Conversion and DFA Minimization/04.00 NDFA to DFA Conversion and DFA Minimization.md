## What this lesson is about

Three **mechanical algorithms**:
1. **Subset construction:** NDFA → equivalent DFA.
2. **Myhill-Nerode theorem (table-filling):** DFA → minimal DFA by marking distinguishable **pairs**.
3. **Equivalence theorem (partitioning):** DFA → minimal DFA by splitting **groups** until they stop changing.

None of this needs a clever idea. It needs **careful, well-presented tables**. Marks are given for **stating the algorithm** (method marks) and for **every step of the working** (execution marks).

> [!tip] One DFA, two methods
> The lecture uses **the same 6-state DFA** (states a–f, final states {c, d, e}) for both minimization examples. Both methods give the same answer: {a, b}, {c, d, e}, {f}. The minimal DFA is **unique**, so if the two methods disagree, you have made a mistake.
