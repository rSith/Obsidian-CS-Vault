## 2020/21 Q1(d)
> **Question:** "In this information age, the security of data is critical". Describe this sentence considering the security of computer networks

### Expected answer
- Today almost all personal, business and government data (bank accounts, health records, exam results, e-commerce orders) is stored and transmitted over networks, so its **confidentiality, integrity and availability** are critical.
- The lecture notes the main **problem** of networks is **security**: *"it's much easier to protect centralized resources than when they are distributed"*, and *"the network itself"* is a target.
- **Threats:** eavesdropping on wireless links; malware and ransomware spreading through the network; phishing and **identity theft** (thieves collect enough information to obtain credit cards in the victim's name); **cookies** that track users and may leak credit-card numbers; denial-of-service attacks; insider misuse.
- **Consequences:** financial loss, privacy violations, loss of trust, legal penalties, disrupted services.
- **Controls:** encryption (HTTPS, **WPA2 with AES** on WiFi, not WEP); strong authentication and 2FA; **firewalls and proxy servers**; antivirus and patching; access control; backups and redundancy; user awareness training; security policies and laws.
- **Social side:** balance security monitoring with privacy (**employer vs employee rights**, **government vs citizen**).

### Key points
CIA · lecture's "security problem" · 3–4 threats · 3–4 controls · privacy balance.

## 2021/22 Q1(d)
> **Question:** "Misinformation distribution through computer networks must be controlled". Describe this sentence considering the various social aspects.

### Expected answer
- Networks (social networks, messaging apps, newsgroups) let anyone publish to millions **instantly**; false information spreads faster than corrections.
- **Harms:** panic and health risks (fake cures), ethnic/religious tension and violence, election manipulation, fraud and scams, damage to reputations.
- **Social tensions from the lecture:** newsgroups on topics people care about (politics, religion) produce content that is *"deeply offensive"*; **government vs citizen** (control vs freedom of expression); **censorship** (the university that blocked newsgroups); employer monitoring of employees.
- **How to control:** platform moderation, fact-checking and warning labels; reporting/blocking tools; digital-literacy education; legal measures against deliberately harmful falsehoods; verified sources.
- **Risks of control:** over-censorship and abuse of power; who decides what is "false"; privacy concerns from monitoring.
- **Conclusion:** misinformation should be limited mainly through **transparency and education**, with legal action reserved for clearly harmful content, protecting freedom of speech.

## 2022/23 Q1(d) ⚠️
> **Question:** "Immerging Artificial Intelligence trend has a significant impact on computer networks". Describe this sentence considering the various social and technical aspects.

### Expected answer
**Technical aspects**
- AI for **network management**: predicting traffic, automatic configuration, self-healing networks, optimised routing and load balancing.
- AI for **security**: anomaly and intrusion detection, spam/phishing filtering; but attackers also use AI (automated attacks, deepfake phishing).
- AI **increases traffic and infrastructure demand**: cloud AI services, video, large data transfers → need for higher bandwidth, low latency, 5G and edge computing, larger data centres.
- AI in **wireless**: smarter spectrum allocation, IoT analytics.

**Social aspects**
- **Privacy**: massive data collection to train models; tracking (cookies).
- **Misinformation/deepfakes** spread over networks.
- **Jobs**: automation of network administration and support roles; new skills required.
- **Bias, accountability and trust** in automated decisions; digital divide between those with and without access.

**Conclusion:** AI makes networks more efficient and secure but raises privacy, ethical and capacity challenges that need regulation and skilled people.

## 2023/24 Q1(d) ⚠️
> **Question:** "Social Networks and AI have created uncertainty in the minds of their users". Describe this sentence considering the various social and technical aspects.

### Expected answer
**Why users feel uncertain**
- **Authenticity:** AI-generated text, images and deepfake videos make it hard to know what is real; fake accounts and bots.
- **Privacy:** users don't know what data social networks and AI tools collect (cookies, tracking, location), how it is used, or who it is sold to (lecture: violation of privacy, cookies, identity theft).
- **Algorithmic control:** recommendation algorithms decide what users see ("filter bubbles"), influencing opinions and elections.
- **Security:** AI-powered phishing and scams; account takeover; identity theft.
- **Jobs and dependence:** worry about AI replacing work; over-reliance on AI answers.

**Technical aspects**
- Data is stored and processed in distributed cloud systems across countries; encryption and access control determine safety.
- Detection tools (AI content detection, fact-checking, verified accounts, 2FA) and privacy settings help users.

**Conclusion:** the benefits (connection, information, convenience) are real, but **transparency, regulation (data-protection laws), platform accountability and user awareness** are needed to rebuild trust.
