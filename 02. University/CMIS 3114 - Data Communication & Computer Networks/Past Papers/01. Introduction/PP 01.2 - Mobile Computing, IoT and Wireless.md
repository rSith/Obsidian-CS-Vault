## A. Mobile computing (short note / definition + scenario)

> **2019/20 Q1(c)(i), 2020/21 Q1(c)(i), 2023/24 Q1(c)(i):** Write short notes on following topics describing their benefits and issues: Mobile computing.
> **2021/22 Q1(b):** What is mobile computing, describe a usage scenario.

**Related topic:** [[01.05 Mobile Computing and Wireless Networks]]

### Expected answer
**Mobile computing** means using portable computing devices (notebooks, smartphones, tablets, PDAs) that stay **connected to the home base, data and services while the user moves**, through **wireless networks** (WiFi, cellular 3G/4G/5G, Bluetooth). The lecture notes that mobile computers are one of the fastest-growing segments, and wireless is essential because *"having a wired connection is impossible in cars and airplanes."* Users can make calls, send e-mail, surf the Web, access remote files and log on to remote machines on the road.

**Benefits:** anywhere/anytime access · productivity while travelling · real-time communication · location-based services (GPS, maps, ride hailing) · supports field work (health, sales, delivery).
**Issues:** security/privacy (theft, interception of wireless data) · limited battery and small screens · depends on coverage, dropped connections, roaming · lower bandwidth and data cost · health/safety concerns raised in the lecture.

**Usage scenario:** a sales representative uses a tablet with 4G to check stock in the company database, take the customer's order and payment on site, and sync it instantly to head office.

## B. Mobile computing applied

> **2018/19 Q2(d):** Describe how mobile computing would support emergency healthcare using an example.
> **2020/21 Q2(d):** Describe how mobile computing would support an online business using an example.

### Expected answer: emergency healthcare
An accident victim is picked up by an ambulance. Wearable/bedside **sensors (a BAN)** send heart rate, blood pressure and ECG to a paramedic's **tablet**, which transmits them over **4G** to the hospital. The emergency doctor views the data and photos live, guides treatment by **video call**, and prepares the theatre before arrival; **GPS** tracks the ambulance and finds the nearest suitable hospital; the patient's records are retrieved from the hospital database while on the way. Result: faster, better-informed treatment.

### Expected answer: online business
A small business sells through a **mobile app/website**. Customers browse the catalogue on their phones and pay by card or mobile wallet; the owner receives order notifications and updates prices and stock **from her phone anywhere**; delivery riders use **GPS navigation** and confirm delivery on a phone; customers track orders in real time; social-media marketing and push notifications reach mobile users directly.

### Exam approach
Set the scene in one sentence → list 4–5 ways (each naming the device/network/service) → state the benefit.

## C. Wireless networks / Wireless LAN: advantages and disadvantages

> **2018/19 Q2(c):** Discuss the advantages/disadvantages of using wireless networks.
> **2020/21 Q2(c):** List the advantages and disadvantages of using a Wireless LAN.

**Related topic:** [[01.05 Mobile Computing and Wireless Networks]] · [[04.06 IEEE 802.11 WiFi]]

### Expected answer
| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Mobility: connect anywhere within range | Security: signals can be intercepted; needs WPA2 (WEP is easily cracked) |
| No cables: cheaper/faster installation; works in old or temporary buildings | Interference from other devices, walls, weather |
| Easy to add new users/devices | Limited range (tens of metres for WLAN) |
| Supports laptops, phones and IoT devices | Lower and **shared** bandwidth; slows with many users |
| Flexible (ad hoc mode, hotspots) | Battery drain; health/safety concerns; need for a free frequency band |

(The lecture's WLAN design challenges: frequency availability worldwide, finite radio range, user privacy, limited battery, human safety, mobility, enough bandwidth.)

## D. Internet of Things, smart home, smart cars ⚠️

> **2019/20 Q1(c)(ii), 2021/22 Q1(c)(ii), 2023/24 Q1(c)(ii):** Write short notes… describing their benefits and issues: Internet of Things.
> **2022/23 Q1(c)(ii):** Write short notes describing their benefits and issues on the following topics: Smart Home.
> **2020/21 Q8(b)(iii):** Write short notes on any three of the following topics: Use of IoT techniques for Smart Cars.

**Related topic:** [[01.20 IoT, Smart Homes and Emerging Topics]]

### Expected answer: IoT
The **Internet of Things** is a network of everyday physical objects embedded with **sensors, actuators, processors and network connectivity** that collect and exchange data over the Internet and can be monitored or controlled remotely, often automatically. Architecture: sensors/actuators → short-range wireless (Bluetooth, WiFi, Zigbee) to a gateway → Internet → cloud processing → user app.
- **Benefits:** automation and convenience; remote monitoring/control; efficiency (energy, water, fuel); data-driven decisions; applications in health, agriculture, transport, industry.
- **Issues:** security (weak devices hacked, botnets); privacy of continuous data collection; interoperability between vendors; huge numbers of devices need IP addresses (IPv6) and bandwidth; dependence on Internet and power; cost and battery life.

### Expected answer: smart home
Smart lights, thermostats/AC, locks, CCTV, plugs and voice assistants connected via home WiFi/Bluetooth/Zigbee to a hub and the cloud, controlled from a phone. **Benefits:** convenience, energy saving, remote security monitoring, support for elderly/disabled people. **Issues:** hacked cameras/locks, privacy of recordings, Internet/power dependence, cost, compatibility.

### Expected answer: IoT for smart cars
Cars carry sensors (GPS, cameras, radar/LiDAR, engine and tyre sensors) connected over **4G/5G** and **vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) / vehicle-to-infrastructure** links: live-traffic navigation, collision warning and automatic braking, remote diagnostics and over-the-air updates, automatic emergency call after a crash, fleet tracking, driver-assistance/self-driving. Issues: cyber-attacks on safety systems, location privacy, need for low-latency reliable connectivity, cost.

### Exam approach
Definition → mini architecture diagram → 3 benefits → 3 issues → one concrete example.

## E. Wi-Fi vs Li-Fi ⚠️

> **2022/23 Q2(c):** Compare Wi-Fi and Li-Fi communication, discussing the advantages and their disadvantages.

### Expected answer
| | Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11) | Li-Fi |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | Radio waves (2.4/5 GHz ISM bands) | Visible light from LED lamps; photodiode receiver |
| Coverage | Tens of metres, passes through walls | A room; needs line of sight; blocked by walls |
| Speed | Up to hundreds of Mbps (802.11n 600 Mbps) and more | Very high (Gbps in trials) |
| Interference | Crowded radio spectrum, interference | No radio interference: usable in hospitals, aircraft, petrol stations |
| Security | Leaks outside the building; needs WPA2 | More secure: light stays in the room |
| Disadvantages | Interference, security, shared bandwidth | Short range, line of sight, lights must be on, little equipment available, sunlight interference |

Lecture link: Li-Fi is a form of **lightwave/optical wireless transmission** ([[02.02 Wireless Transmission]]).

## F. MANET ⚠️

> **2022/23 Q2(d):** Mobile Ad hoc Networks (MANET) have a highly adaptable nature. Describe a usage scenario of a MANET, listing the advantages and disadvantages.

### Expected answer
A **MANET** is a **self-configuring wireless network of mobile devices with no fixed infrastructure** (no access point or base station): each node is also a **router** that forwards packets for others, and the topology changes as nodes move. (Lecture link: 802.11 **ad hoc mode** and "wireless networking without a base station".)
**Scenario:** after a flood destroys mobile towers, rescue teams' radios/phones form a MANET to share maps, victim locations and messages; similar uses in military operations and vehicle-to-vehicle networks.
**Advantages:** no infrastructure needed; rapid deployment; self-healing/adaptable when nodes move or fail; low cost; works in remote areas.
**Disadvantages:** limited bandwidth and battery; frequent route changes make routing complex; weaker security (any node can relay/eavesdrop); hidden/exposed terminal problems; performance falls as hops increase.
