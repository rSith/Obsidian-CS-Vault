## A. Physical construction of wired media

> **2019/20 Q4(a):** Describe the physical construction characteristics of the following transmission media using a diagram. i. Twisted pair ii. Optical fiber cable
> **2021/22 Q4(a):** Using a named diagram, describe the physical construction characteristics of the following transmission media. i. Twisted pair ii. Coaxial cable iii. Optical fiber cable
> **2023/24 Q4(a):** Describe the physical construction characteristics of the following transmission media using a diagram. Mention a suitable usage for each type. i. Twisted pair ii. Optical fiber cable
> **2020/21 Q4(a):** Describe three types of wired media used in computer networks and list their advantages and disadvantages.

**Related topic:** [[02.01 Guided Transmission Media]]

### Expected answer
**Twisted pair:** two **insulated copper wires (~1 mm)** **twisted together in a helix**; the twisting makes the waves from the two wires **cancel**, reducing radiation and **crosstalk/interference**. Several pairs (usually 4) sit in a plastic **outer jacket**; **UTP** (unshielded, Cat 3/5e/6) or **STP** (with a foil/braid **shield**). RJ-45 connectors.
```text
  ┌─ outer PVC jacket ─────────────────────────────┐
  │  ═╗╔══╗╔══╗╔══╗╔═   pair 1 (insulated copper)  │
  │  ═╝╚══╝╚══╝╚══╝╚═                              │
  │  … pairs 2–4 …                                 │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
*Usage:* telephone local loops, **Ethernet LANs** (labs, offices). ✅ cheap, flexible, easy ❌ ~100 m limit, noise/EMI, lower bandwidth.

**Coaxial cable:** **copper core** → **insulating (dielectric) material** → **braided outer conductor (shield)** → **protective plastic covering**.
```text
   ( jacket ( braided shield ( insulation ( ● copper core ) ) ) )
```
*Usage:* **cable TV / Internet over cable (HFC)**, old Ethernet bus, CCTV. ✅ better shielding, higher bandwidth and distance than twisted pair ❌ bulkier, costlier, being replaced by fibre/UTP.

**Optical fibre:** a very thin **glass core** (8–10 µm single-mode, ~50 µm multimode) surrounded by **glass cladding with a lower refractive index**, then a **plastic jacket**; several fibres are bundled inside an **outer sheath**. Light from an **LED or laser** is kept in the core by **total internal reflection**; a **photodiode** receives it.
```text
  side view:  ┌ jacket ┐┌ cladding ┐┌ core ┐   light bounces ↘↗↘↗ by total internal reflection
  end view of a cable:  sheath around 3 fibres  ((●)) ((●)) ((●))
```
*Usage:* **backbones, undersea cables, telephone trunks, links between buildings, ISP connections**. ✅ huge bandwidth, very low loss (long distance), immune to EMI, secure, thin/light ❌ expensive interfaces, fragile, skilled installation, unidirectional per fibre.

### Exam approach
Labelled diagram (every layer named) → 2–3 lines of description → usage → pros/cons. "Named diagram" = label each part.

## B. Long-distance media

> **2020/21 Q4(b):** Possible choices for data communication infrastructure for long distance data transfer include microwave, optical fiber and radio transmission. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each of these media.

**Related topic:** [[02.02 Wireless Transmission]]

### Expected answer
| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Optical fibre** | Very high bandwidth; very low attenuation → few repeaters over tens of km; immune to EMI; secure; reliable | High installation cost (trenching, undersea laying); cable cuts; skilled splicing; slow to deploy |
| **Microwave** | No cable laying: cheaper over difficult terrain, quick to deploy; high bandwidth; focused beams | **Line of sight** needed: towers and **repeaters about every 50 km**; rain/fog absorption and multipath fading; licensed spectrum |
| **Radio** | Omnidirectional, passes through buildings; very long distances (VLF/LF/MF follow earth's curvature; HF bounces off the ionosphere); cheap receivers | Low bandwidth; interference between users (regulated spectrum); ionospheric conditions vary; easy to intercept |

## C. Wired vs wireless media

> **2022/23 Q4(a):** List the advantages and disadvantages of using wired and wireless transmission media. Provide two examples of each type of media.

### Expected answer
| | Wired (guided) | Wireless (unguided) |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | **Twisted pair, coaxial cable, optical fibre** | **Radio/WiFi, microwave, satellite, infrared** |
| Advantages | Higher and more stable speed; more secure; less interference; reliable; long distances (fibre) | Mobility; no cables; fast/cheap deployment; reaches remote places; easy to add users |
| Disadvantages | Cabling cost and effort; no mobility; physical damage; hard to change layout | Interference and weather effects; less secure; shared, lower bandwidth; limited range; spectrum licensing |

## D. Satellites

> **2022/23 Q4(d):** What are the main three types of Satellites when considering their orbits? Describe the satellite type suitable to provide a global Internet service. Explain why this particular satellite type was selected and what are the technology challenges faced by this type of a network.

**Related topic:** [[02.03 Communication Satellites]]

### Expected answer
| Type | Altitude | Delay | Satellites for global coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GEO** (geostationary) | 35,800 km | ~270 ms round trip | 3 | Fixed in the sky; TV broadcast, VSAT |
| **MEO** (medium-earth) | ~18,000–20,200 km | ~35–85 ms | ~10 | Tracked; navigation (GPS at 20,200 km) |
| **LEO** (low-earth) | ~1,000 km (hundreds–2,000) | ~1–7 ms | ~50+ | Iridium (66 sats, relay in space), Globalstar (relay on ground) |

**Global Internet → LEO constellations** (e.g. Iridium in the lecture; Starlink/OneWeb today).
**Why:** low altitude gives **very low latency** (good for interactive applications); **weaker transmitters and small terminals** are enough; a large constellation gives **coverage of the whole earth including poles and oceans**; each satellite covers a small area so capacity is reused.
**Challenges:** need **hundreds–thousands of satellites** (launch and replacement cost; short lifetime); satellites move fast → **frequent handoffs** and **tracking antennas**; **routing through inter-satellite links** in a constantly changing topology; rain fade; **space debris** and collision risk; spectrum allocation and national regulation; ground-station network.
