## A. Hidden and exposed terminal problems + MACA

> **2019/20 Q7(d):** In wireless LANs explain the Hidden and Exposed terminal problems. Propose a suitable protocol to solve the above problems.
> **2020/21 Q6(c):** In wireless LANs explain the Hidden and Exposed terminal problems and propose a suitable solution.
> **2021/22 Q7(c):** Describe what is Hidden terminal problem and Exposed terminal problem. **Q7(d):** Give and explain a solution to the problems mentioned in part (c) above.
> **2023/24 Q6(c):** Describe using a diagram what is Hidden terminal problem and Exposed terminal problem. **Q6(d):** Provide a solution to the problems mentioned in part (c) above and explain how it works.

### Expected answer
In a wireless LAN what matters is **interference at the receiver, not at the sender**, and each station has a limited radio range.

**Hidden terminal problem:** a station **cannot detect a potential competitor** because the competitor is **too far away**.
```text
  [A] ───► [B] ◄─── [C]          A and C cannot hear each other;
  ( A's range )( C's range )      both sense "idle" and send to B → collision at B
```
A sends to B; C, out of A's range, senses the channel idle and also sends to B; the frames **collide at B** and neither sender knows.

**Exposed terminal problem:** a station **hears a transmission and wrongly refrains** from sending.
```text
  [A] ◄─── [B]      [C] ───► [D]   B sends to A; C hears B and defers,
                                    although C → D would not disturb A
```
C's transmission would not interfere at A (out of C's range), so the waiting **wastes bandwidth**.

**Solution: MACA (Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance)**
1. A sends a short **RTS (Request To Send, ~30 bytes)** to B, containing the **length of the data frame**.
2. B replies with **CTS (Clear To Send)**, also containing the length.
3. A sends the **data frame** (802.11 adds an ACK from B).
4. **Stations that hear the RTS** (near A, e.g. C, E) stay silent long enough for the **CTS** to reach A.
5. **Stations that hear the CTS** (near B, e.g. D, E) stay silent for the **whole data transmission** (length known from the CTS).
- **Hidden terminal solved:** the hidden station hears B's **CTS** and stays quiet.
- **Exposed terminal solved:** a station that hears the **RTS but not the CTS** (near A only) knows it is not near the receiver and is **free to transmit** once the CTS has passed.
- If two RTSs collide, the senders back off randomly and retry (cheap, since RTS is short).

```text
 (a) A sends RTS to B           (b) B replies CTS to A
   C ← [ A ] RTS→ [ B ]   D        C   [ A ] ←CTS [ B ] → D
        E hears RTS                     E hears CTS
```

### Exam approach
Two labelled range diagrams + definitions → MACA steps → explicitly say **how each problem is solved**.

## B. Wi-Fi vs WiMAX vs 4G

> **2020/21 Q8(b)(i):** Write short notes on any three of the following topics: Wi-Fi Vs WIMAX Vs 4G.

### Expected answer
| | Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11) | WiMAX (IEEE 802.16) | 4G (LTE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Wireless **LAN** | Broadband wireless **MAN** / last mile | Cellular **WAN** |
| Range | Tens of metres | Several km; non-line-of-sight | Several km per cell |
| Speed | 11 (b), 54 (a/g), up to 600 Mbps (n) | Tens of Mbps | ~100 Mbps+ |
| Technology | DSSS / OFDM, MIMO | **OFDM, 2–10 GHz** | OFDMA, MIMO |
| Spectrum | Unlicensed ISM 2.4/5 GHz | Licensed (mostly) | Licensed operator bands |
| Mobility | Low (within a building) | Fixed and limited mobility | High, with handoff |
| Use | Homes, offices, hotspots | Rural/last-mile broadband (avoids laying fibre/coax) | Mobile Internet, voice, video |
The lecture notes WiMAX **competes with 3G/4G LTE**, and 4G LTE offers high bandwidth, ubiquity, seamless integration with WiFi and other IP networks, adaptive spectrum management and high QoS. ⚠️ The numeric ranges/speeds beyond the slide are typical values, not lecture content.
