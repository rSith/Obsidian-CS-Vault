# 02.02 · Wireless (Unguided) Transmission

> [!info] [[02.00 Physical Layer|Lesson 2]] · Lecture ref Ch2 slides 14–21 · Priority 🟡 Medium
> **Asked as:** "Possible choices for data communication infrastructure for long distance data transfer include microwave, optical fiber and radio transmission. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each" (2020/21 Q4b) · "List the advantages and disadvantages of using wired and wireless transmission media. Provide two examples of each" (2022/23 Q4a)

## 1. What is it?
**Unguided (wireless) media** send electromagnetic waves through **air or space** with no physical conductor. Lecture list: **electromagnetic spectrum, radio, microwave, infrared and millimetre waves, lightwave transmission.**

## 2. The electromagnetic spectrum
The lecture figure shows frequencies from radio (≈10⁴ Hz) through microwave, infrared, visible light, UV, X-rays and gamma rays, with the bands used for communication: twisted pair and coax (low), AM and FM radio, TV, terrestrial microwave, **satellite**, **fibre optics** (infrared). Key idea: **higher frequency → more bandwidth (more data), but behaves more like light** (straight lines, blocked by obstacles).

## 3. Radio transmission
- Easy to generate, **travels long distances**, **penetrates buildings**, **omnidirectional** (no need to point antennas).
- Behaviour depends on frequency (lecture figure):
  - **VLF, LF, MF bands:** radio waves **follow the curvature of the earth** (ground waves), e.g. AM radio.
  - **HF band:** waves **bounce off the ionosphere**, travelling very long distances (amateur/military radio).
- Problems: **interference** between users (strictly regulated), absorbed by rain at high frequencies.

## 4. Microwave transmission *(slide title only; standard textbook content)*
- Above ~100 MHz waves travel in **nearly straight lines** and can be **narrowly focused** with parabolic dish antennas.
- Needs **line of sight**: towers are placed on hills or tall masts, with **repeaters every ~50 km** because of the earth's curvature.
- Used for long-distance telephone, mobile-phone backhaul, TV distribution.
- **Multipath fading** and rain absorption at high frequencies are problems.

## 5. Infrared and millimetre waves
Short-range, **cannot pass through walls**: used in **TV remote controls**. Advantage: no interference with neighbours and **no licence needed**; secure within a room.

## 6. Lightwave (laser) transmission
Lasers on rooftops connect buildings. The lecture shows a **bidirectional system with two lasers** and notes **convection currents (heat from the roof) can interfere** with the beam; fog and rain also block it. Very high bandwidth, cheap, no licence, but must be aimed precisely.

## 7. Politics of the spectrum
- Governments allocate frequency bands; operators need **licences**.
- **ISM bands** (Industrial, Scientific and Medical): *"internationally reserved for the use of RF energy intended for scientific, medical and industrial requirements rather than for communications"*; they can be used **without a licence** at low power (e.g. **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz** used by WiFi, Bluetooth, microwave ovens).
- In Sri Lanka, spectrum is regulated by the **Telecommunications Regulatory Commission (TRC)**, trc.gov.lk.

## 8. Google Loon
Project **Loon** used **high-altitude balloons** in the stratosphere as floating cell towers to bring Internet to remote areas; Sri Lanka was one of the trial countries. *(The slide is pictures only; Loon ended in 2021.)*

## 9. Comparisons

### Wired (guided) vs wireless (unguided)

| | Wired / guided | Wireless / unguided |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | Copper wire, glass fibre | Air / space (radio, microwave, infrared, light) |
| Examples | **Twisted pair, coaxial cable, optical fibre** | **Radio (WiFi), microwave, satellite, infrared** |
| Advantages | Higher, more stable speeds; **more secure**; less interference; reliable | **Mobility**; no cabling; easy and quick to deploy; reaches remote/difficult places |
| Disadvantages | Installation cost and effort; no mobility; cables can be cut | **Interference**, weather effects; **less secure**; shared bandwidth; needs spectrum licence (except ISM) |

### Long-distance options (2020/21 Q4b)

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Optical fibre** | Huge bandwidth; very low loss and **few repeaters**; immune to EMI; secure | **High installation cost** (digging, undersea laying); cable can be cut; skilled splicing |
| **Microwave** | No cable to lay: **cheaper** over rough terrain; quick to install; **high bandwidth** | Needs **line of sight** and **towers/repeaters every ~50 km**; affected by **rain and fog**, multipath fading; spectrum licence |
| **Radio** | **Omnidirectional**, passes through buildings, very long range (HF via ionosphere), cheap receivers | **Low bandwidth**; **interference** from other users; ionosphere conditions change; easy to intercept |
