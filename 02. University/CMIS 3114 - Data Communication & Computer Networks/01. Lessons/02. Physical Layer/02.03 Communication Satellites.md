# 02.03 · Communication Satellites: GEO, MEO, LEO

> [!info] [[02.00 Physical Layer|Lesson 2]] · Lecture ref Ch2 slides 22–31 · Priority 🟢 Low (1/6 papers)
> **Asked as:** "What are the main three types of Satellites when considering their orbits? Describe the satellite type suitable to provide a global Internet service. Explain why this particular satellite type was selected and what are the technology challenges faced by this type of a network" (2022/23 Q4d)

## 1. What is it?
A communication satellite is a **big microwave repeater in the sky**: it receives a signal from the ground (uplink), amplifies it, and sends it back down (downlink) to a wide area (its **footprint**).

## 2. Clarke's prediction (lecture)
In **1945**, Arthur C. Clarke calculated that a satellite at **35,800 km** in a circular **equatorial** orbit would **appear motionless** in the sky, so it would not need to be tracked. He thought it impractical because of fragile vacuum tubes.

## 3. The three orbits (lecture table of properties)

| Type | Altitude | Round-trip delay | Satellites for global coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GEO** (Geostationary) | **35,800 km** | ~**270 ms** | **3** | Appears fixed; big footprint; TV broadcasting, VSAT |
| **MEO** (Medium-Earth Orbit) | ~**18,000 km** (GPS at **20,200 km**) | ~35–85 ms | ~**10** | *"6 hours to circle the earth"*; must be **tracked**; smaller footprint; used for **navigation (GPS, ~30 satellites)** |
| **LEO** (Low-Earth Orbit) | ~**1,000 km** (hundreds to ~2,000) | ~1–7 ms | ~**50** (Iridium uses 66) | Move fast; many needed; low delay, low power handsets |

The two **Van Allen belts** (charged particles) lie between the orbits; satellites avoid them. *(Altitude/delay values are from the standard Tanenbaum figure used on the slide.)*

### Satellite bands (lecture table)
| Band | Downlink | Uplink | Problems |
|---|---|---|---|
| L | 1.5 GHz | 1.6 GHz | Low bandwidth; crowded |
| S | 1.9 GHz | 2.2 GHz | Low bandwidth; crowded |
| C | 4.0 GHz | 6.0 GHz | Terrestrial interference |
| Ku | 11 GHz | 14 GHz | Rain |
| Ka | 20 GHz | 30 GHz | Rain, equipment cost |

## 4. VSAT (Very Small Aperture Terminals)
- Low-cost **microstations** with **≤ 1 m antennas** (vs 10 m for a standard GEO antenna), about **1 W** power.
- Uplink up to **1 Mbps**, downlink several Mbps (direct broadcast TV uses this one-way).
- Microstations are too weak to talk to each other directly, so a ground **hub** with a large high-gain antenna **relays traffic between VSATs** (VSAT → satellite → hub → satellite → VSAT, which doubles the delay).

## 5. LEO systems
- **Globalstar:** satellites act as simple **"bent pipes"**: calls are **relayed on the ground** (satellite → ground station → terrestrial network).
- **Iridium:** **66 satellites in six "necklaces"** (polar orbits) around the earth; **1,628 moving cells** cover the earth; calls are **relayed in space** from satellite to satellite.

## 6. Satellites vs fibre *(slide heading; standard points)*
| Satellites better when… | Fibre better when… |
|---|---|
| **Broadcast** to many receivers (TV) | Very high bandwidth point-to-point |
| **Remote / rough terrain**, ships, aircraft, disaster areas | Low delay needed |
| **Rapid deployment** without laying cable | Long-term, high-volume traffic (cheaper per bit) |
| Mobile users anywhere on earth | Secure, weather-independent links |

## 7. Global Internet service (2022/23 Q4d) → **LEO constellations**
- **Why LEO:** **low altitude → very low latency** (a few ms instead of ~270 ms for GEO, so video calls and gaming work); **lower power** needed so small user terminals work; **global coverage including poles** with a large constellation; capacity can be reused across many small cells. Example *(beyond slides)*: SpaceX **Starlink**, OneWeb; in the lecture, **Iridium**.
- **Challenges:** need **hundreds/thousands of satellites** (cost, launches); satellites move fast, so **frequent handoffs** between satellites and **tracking antennas** (phased arrays); **inter-satellite links** and routing in a moving network; short satellite lifetime and replacement; **space debris** and collisions; weather (rain fade at Ku/Ka bands); spectrum regulation and permission in each country.
