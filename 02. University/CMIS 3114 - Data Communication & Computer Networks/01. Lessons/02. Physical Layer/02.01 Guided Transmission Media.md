# 02.01 · Guided (Wired) Transmission Media

> [!info] [[02.00 Physical Layer|Lesson 2]] · Lecture ref Ch2 slides 2–13 · Priority 🔴 High (5/6 papers)
> **Asked as:** "Describe the physical construction characteristics of the following transmission media using a diagram: twisted pair, optical fiber" (2019/20 Q4a, 2023/24 Q4a + usage) · "…twisted pair, coaxial cable, optical fiber" (2021/22 Q4a) · "Describe three types of wired media… advantages and disadvantages" (2020/21 Q4a) · media choice in design questions (2018/19 Q1c)

## 1. What is it?
**Guided media** carry the signal **along a physical path** (a wire or a glass fibre). The lecture's list: **magnetic media, twisted pair, coaxial cable, fibre optics**. (Unguided/wireless media: [[02.02 Wireless Transmission]].)

> [!note] Slides are pictures
> The twisted-pair and coax slides are images only. The construction details below are the standard textbook descriptions (Tanenbaum 6e §2.2), matching those images.

## 2. Magnetic media
Write data to tape or disks and **physically carry** them to the destination ("never underestimate the bandwidth of a station wagon full of tapes"). Very **high bandwidth, very high delay**, cheap per bit. This is the idea behind the **dog-carrying-tapes** calculation (2021/22 Q4c) → [[02.08 Maximum Data Rate - Nyquist and Shannon]].

## 3. Twisted pair

### Construction
```text
   Copper conductor (≈1 mm)            Outer PVC jacket
        │  ┌── insulation (colour coded)   │
        ▼  ▼                               ▼
   ════╗╔═══╗╔═══╗╔═══╗╔════   ← two insulated copper wires
       ╚╝   ╚╝   ╚╝   ╚╝          twisted together in a helix
   A UTP cable: 4 such pairs inside one plastic sheath (Cat 5e / Cat 6)
```
- **Two insulated copper wires**, about 1 mm thick, **twisted together in a helix** (like a DNA molecule).
- **Why twist?** The waves from the two wires **cancel**, so the pair **radiates less** and picks up **less interference (crosstalk)**. A signal is usually sent as the **difference in voltage** between the two wires (balanced signalling), so noise that hits both equally cancels.
- **UTP** (Unshielded Twisted Pair): categories **Cat 3** (telephone), **Cat 5/5e** (100 Mbps–1 Gbps), **Cat 6** and above (faster, more twists per cm). **STP** adds a **metal foil/braid shield** for extra noise protection.
- Connector: **RJ-45** (LAN), RJ-11 (telephone).

### Usage
**Telephone local loops**, **Ethernet LANs** (computer labs, offices), ADSL.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Cheapest**, light, flexible, easy to install | **Limited distance** (~100 m for Ethernet) before a repeater is needed |
| Good performance for LANs (up to 10 Gbps on Cat 6a+) | **Susceptible to noise / EMI** and crosstalk (UTP) |
| Widely available, used everywhere | **Lower bandwidth** than coax/fibre; can be tapped |

## 4. Coaxial cable

### Construction (from the centre outwards)
```text
  ┌──────────────── protective plastic covering (outer jacket)
  │ ┌────────────── braided outer conductor (copper mesh shield)
  │ │ ┌──────────── insulating material (dielectric)
  │ │ │ ┌────────── copper core (inner conductor)
  ▼ ▼ ▼ ▼
 ((( ( ● ) )))
```
- A **stiff copper core** surrounded by **insulating material**, encased in a **cylindrical conductor** (often a **woven braided mesh**), all covered in a **protective plastic sheath**.
- Better **shielding** than twisted pair → **higher bandwidth** and **longer distances**, excellent noise immunity.
- Two kinds: **50-ohm** (originally digital transmission) and **75-ohm** (**cable TV**, analog/Internet over cable).

### Usage
**Cable television** and **Internet over cable** (HFC, see [[02.12 Cable Television and Internet over Cable]]), old Ethernet (10Base2 bus), CCTV, MANs.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Higher bandwidth** than twisted pair (around 6 GHz for modern cable) | More **expensive** and **bulkier/stiffer** than twisted pair |
| **Better shielding**: low noise and crosstalk | Harder to install; a break in a bus cable stops the whole segment |
| **Longer distances** than twisted pair | Mostly replaced by fibre for backbones and UTP for LANs |

## 5. Fibre optics

### How light is trapped: total internal reflection
- A fibre has a **glass core** with a **higher refractive index** than the **cladding** around it.
- When a light ray hits the core/cladding boundary at an angle **greater than the critical angle**, it is **totally reflected back** into the core. The lecture shows (a) light rays at different angles hitting the silica/air boundary, (b) **light trapped by total internal reflection**.
- So the light bounces along the fibre for long distances with little loss.

```text
 cladding  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
 core        ╲    ╱╲    ╱╲    ╱╲    ╱   ← light ray zig-zags by total
              ╲  ╱  ╲  ╱  ╲  ╱  ╲  ╱       internal reflection
 cladding  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
```

### Construction (lecture: side view of a single fibre; end view of a sheath with three fibres)
```text
 Single fibre (side view)              Sheath with 3 fibres (end view)
 ┌──────────────── jacket (plastic)         ┌─────────── sheath
 │ ┌────────────── cladding (glass, lower n)│  (●) (●) (●)  ← core + cladding
 │ │ ┌──────────── core (glass, higher n)    │   jacket around each
 ▼ ▼ ▼                                     └───────────
 [ ( ═══ ) ]
```
- **Core:** very thin glass (≈ **8–10 µm** single-mode, **50 µm** multimode).
- **Cladding:** glass with lower refractive index keeps the light in the core.
- **Jacket:** thin plastic to protect the cladding. Fibres are grouped in bundles protected by an **outer sheath**.

### Attenuation
The lecture graph shows **attenuation of light in the infrared region**; three low-loss bands are used, around **0.85, 1.30 and 1.55 µm**. Loss is so low that signals travel **tens of kilometres** without repeaters.

### Light sources: LED vs semiconductor laser (lecture comparison table)

| Item | LED | Semiconductor laser |
|---|---|---|
| Data rate | Low | High |
| Fibre type | Multimode | Multimode or single-mode |
| Distance | Short | Long |
| Lifetime | Long life | Short life |
| Temperature sensitivity | Minor | Substantial |
| Cost | Low cost | Expensive |

*(The table is an image of the standard Tanenbaum figure.)* At the receiver a **photodiode** converts light back to an electrical pulse.

### Fibre networks
A **fibre optic ring with active repeaters**: each node converts light → electrical signal → regenerates → light, so rings can be very long. The lecture also shows **global fibre links**, **Sri Lankan fibre connectivity** and **undersea cables** (Sri Lanka connects to the world through submarine cable systems landing at its coast).

### Single-mode vs multimode *(additional explanation)*
| Multimode | Single-mode |
|---|---|
| Wider core (~50 µm); many rays bounce at different angles | Very thin core (~8–10 µm); light goes almost straight |
| LED source; shorter distances (≤ ~2 km) | Laser source; very long distances (100 km without amplification) |
| Cheaper | More expensive, highest bandwidth |

### Usage
**Long-distance backbones**, **undersea cables**, **telephone trunks** between switching offices, campus backbones (e.g. linking a faculty 6 km away, 2018/19 Q1c), ISP connections (FTTH).

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Enormous bandwidth** (Gbps–Tbps), **very low attenuation** → long distances | **Expensive** interfaces/lasers; skilled installation (splicing) |
| **Immune to electromagnetic interference**, power surges, lightning | **Fragile**: can be damaged by bending too sharply |
| **Thin and light** (many fibres per cable) | **Unidirectional** per fibre: two-way needs two fibres or two bands |
| **Secure**: hard to tap without being detected; does not leak light | Optical–electrical conversion needed at the ends |

## 6. Comparison of the three wired media

| Feature | Twisted pair | Coaxial cable | Optical fibre |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signal | Electrical | Electrical | **Light** |
| Bandwidth | Lowest | Medium | **Highest** |
| Distance without repeater | ~100 m (Ethernet) | Hundreds of m to km | **Tens of km +** |
| Noise immunity | Low (UTP) | Good (shield) | **Immune to EMI** |
| Cost | **Cheapest** | Medium | Highest |
| Typical use | LANs, telephone lines | Cable TV, HFC | Backbones, undersea, trunks |
