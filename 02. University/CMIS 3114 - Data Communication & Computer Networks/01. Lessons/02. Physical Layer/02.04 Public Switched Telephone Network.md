# 02.04 · The Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN)

> [!info] [[02.00 Physical Layer|Lesson 2]] · Lecture ref Ch2 slides 32–46, 106–107 · Priority 🔴 High (Telephone vs Internet, 3/6 identical wording incl. 2023/24)
> **Asked as:** "Compare the Telephone network and the Internet, discussing the similarities and their differences" (2019/20 Q2c, 2021/22 Q2c, 2023/24 Q2c)

## 1. Structure of the telephone system (lecture figure)
1. **Fully-interconnected network:** every phone wired to every other phone, impossible to scale ($n(n-1)/2$ wires).
2. **Centralised switch:** every phone wired to one switching office, which connects calls.
3. **Two-level hierarchy:** local switching offices connected to higher-level (toll) offices; this grows into the modern multi-level hierarchy.

A **typical circuit route** for a medium-distance call: telephone → **local loop** → **end office** → **toll office** → (intermediate switching offices, high-bandwidth trunks) → toll office → end office → local loop → telephone.

## 2. Major components (lecture)

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Local loops** | **Analog twisted pairs** going to houses and businesses ("the last mile") |
| **Trunks** | **Digital fibre optics** connecting the switching offices (multiplexed with TDM/WDM) |
| **Switching offices** | Where calls are **moved from one trunk to another** (circuit switching) |

## 3. Politics of telephones (US model in the lecture)
- **LATA** (Local Access and Transport Area): roughly the area covered by one area code.
- **LEC** (Local Exchange Carrier): one per LATA, with a monopoly on local service.
- **IXC** (IntereXchange Carrier): handles **all inter-LATA** (long-distance) traffic.
- An IXC that wants calls from a LATA builds a switching office called a **POP (Point of Presence)** in it.

## 4. The local loop: modems, ADSL, wireless

### Modems and codecs
A computer-to-computer call uses **both analog and digital** transmission: the **modem** converts the computer's digital bits into analog signals for the analog local loop; at the end office a **codec** (coder–decoder) digitises the analog signal for the digital trunks, and the reverse happens at the other end. The lecture shows modem **constellation diagrams** (9,600 bps and 14,400 bps), which are QAM-style ([[02.05 Digital Modulation - Baseband and Passband]]).

### DSL / ADSL
- **Bandwidth vs distance** over Cat 3 UTP: the further the house from the end office, the lower the DSL speed.
- **ADSL** uses **discrete multitone (DMT)** modulation: the ~1.1 MHz local-loop spectrum is split into **256 channels of ~4 kHz**: channel 0 for **voice (POTS)**, some unused guard channels, then **upstream** and many more **downstream** data channels (hence *Asymmetric*). This is an OFDM-like idea.
- A typical ADSL setup: at home, a **splitter** separates voice (to the phone) and data (to the **ADSL modem** → PC); at the end office another splitter sends voice to the voice switch and data to a **DSLAM** → ISP.

### Wireless local loops
**LMDS (Local Multipoint Distribution System):** *"broadband microwave wireless transmission direct from a local antenna to homes and businesses within a line-of-sight radius,"* a solution to the **last-mile** problem.

## 5. Telephone network vs Internet (the exam question)

| Feature | Telephone network (PSTN) | Internet |
|---|---|---|
| Original purpose | **Voice** calls | **Data** (any application) |
| Switching | **Circuit switching**: dedicated path set up for the whole call | **Packet switching**: store-and-forward, no dedicated path |
| Call/connection setup | **Required** (dial, ring) | **Not needed** (IP is connectionless) |
| Bandwidth | **Fixed** per call (e.g. 64 kbps), reserved even when silent → may be **wasted** | **Shared dynamically**, used only when data is sent |
| Route | All data follows **the same path** | Each packet routed **independently** |
| Order | Always **in order** | Packets **may arrive out of order** (TCP reorders) |
| Failure of a switch | **Call is dropped** | Packets **rerouted** around failures |
| Congestion | At **setup** time (busy tone) | On **every packet** (delay, loss) |
| Intelligence | In the **network** (central switches); simple phones | At the **edges** (hosts); simple routers |
| Billing | Usually **per minute / distance** | **Flat rate / per volume** |
| Structure | **Hierarchical** (end office → toll office), local loop + trunks + switching offices | **Network of networks**: ISPs, POPs, backbones, NAPs ([[01.18 Example Networks]]) |

**Similarities:**
- Both are **large, global WANs** connecting millions of users.
- Both use the **same physical infrastructure**: copper local loops, **fibre trunks**, microwave, satellites; and multiplexing (TDM/WDM).
- Both use **hierarchical** structures and **switching** elements (switching offices vs routers) and **addressing** (phone numbers vs IP addresses).
- Today they **converge**: the telephone core is digital and often carries **VoIP**, and Internet access is delivered over telephone lines (**DSL**) and the ISP connects at a **POP**.
