# 04.02 · ALOHA: Pure and Slotted

> [!info] [[04.00 MAC Sublayer|Lesson 4]] · Lecture ref Ch4 slides 8–10 · Priority 🔴 High (5/6 papers, always compared with a CSMA variant)
> **Asked as:** "Compare and contrast **Slotted Aloha and p-persistent CSMA**" (2019/20 Q7a, 2021/22 Q7a) · "**Pure Aloha and 1-persistent CSMA**" (2020/21 Q6b) · "Using diagrams… **Pure Aloha and p-persistent CSMA**" (2022/23 Q6b) · "Using diagrams… **Slotted Aloha and 1-persistent CSMA**" (2023/24 Q6a). Comparison tables are in [[04.03 CSMA and CSMA-CD]].

## 1. Origin
**Norman Abramson** and colleagues at the **University of Hawaii** wanted to connect users on remote islands to the main computer in **Honolulu** by radio.

## 2. Pure ALOHA: how it works (lecture)
1. **Let users transmit whenever they have data** to send (no listening first).
2. After a station sends its frame to the central computer, the computer **rebroadcasts the frame to all stations**, so the sender can **confirm correct reception**.
3. If the frame was **destroyed** (collision), the sender **waits a random amount of time** and sends it again.

*"In pure ALOHA, frames are transmitted at **completely arbitrary times**."*

```text
 User A  ▭      ▭
 User B      ▭                    Frames start at any time.
 User C    ▭        ▭       ▭     Any overlap = collision:
 User D   ▭ ▭          ▭     ▭    BOTH frames are destroyed
 User E ▭      ▭    ▭      ▭
        ───────────────────────► time
```

**Vulnerable period** *(textbook)*: a frame of length t collides with any frame that starts up to t before **or** t after it starts → vulnerable for **2t**.

## 3. Slotted ALOHA (lecture)
- *"Divide time into **discrete intervals called slots**, each interval corresponding to **one frame**."*
- A station may transmit **only at the start of a slot**.
- So frames either **overlap completely** in a slot or not at all: the vulnerable period is cut to **t** (one slot), doubling throughput. Requires **synchronised** clocks.

```text
 Slots: |  1  |  2  |  3  |  4  |  5  |  6  |
 A        ▭                       ▭
 B              ▭▭ ← collision (2 frames in slot 2)
 C                    ▭                 ▭
        ─────────────────────────────────► time
```

## 4. Throughput (from the lecture's utilisation graph)
The lecture graph *"Comparison of the channel utilization versus load"* shows throughput S against offered load G (attempts per packet time):
- **Pure ALOHA** peaks at about **S ≈ 0.18** (18%) at G = 0.5.
- **Slotted ALOHA** peaks at about **S ≈ 0.37** (37%) at G = 1.
- All CSMA variants do much better (up to ~0.9+).

> [!note] Additional explanation: formulas (textbook)
> $$S_{pure} = G\,e^{-2G}\ (\max \tfrac{1}{2e}\approx 0.184)\qquad S_{slotted} = G\,e^{-G}\ (\max \tfrac{1}{e}\approx 0.368)$$

## 5. Pure vs Slotted ALOHA

| Feature | Pure ALOHA | Slotted ALOHA |
|---|---|---|
| When to send | **Any time** | Only at the **start of a slot** |
| Time model | Continuous | **Slotted** (needs synchronisation) |
| Vulnerable period | **2 frame times** | **1 frame time** |
| Max efficiency | **~18%** | **~37%** (double) |
| Collisions | Partial overlaps destroy both frames | Only frames in the same slot collide |
| Carrier sense | No | No |

## 6. Advantages / disadvantages
- ✅ Very **simple**, fully decentralised, no carrier sensing needed.
- ❌ **Low efficiency** (most of the channel wasted at high load); collisions waste whole frames; delay unpredictable.
