## 1. What is mobile computing?

**Simple English:** using a computer (laptop, phone, tablet) and staying connected to your data and services **while you move**.

From the lecture:
- **Mobile users** want *"to be connected to their home base even when away from home or en route."*
- People on the road want to *"send and receive telephone calls, faxes, and electronic mail, surf the Web, access remote files, and log on to remote machines."*
- *"Mobile computers, such as notebook computers and PDAs, are one of the fastest-growing segments of the computer industry."*
- *"Since having a wired connection is impossible in cars and airplanes, there is a lot of interest in **wireless networks**."*
- The lecture shows **combinations of wireless networks and mobile computing** (and a UAV/drone example).

### Wireless vs mobile are not the same thing

| Wireless | Mobile | Typical application (Tanenbaum table on the slide) |
|---|---|---|
| No | No | Desktop computers in offices |
| No | Yes | A notebook computer used in a hotel room (plugged into a wired port) |
| Yes | No | Networks in older, unwired buildings |
| Yes | Yes | Portable office; PDA for store inventory |

*(The slide is an image of this standard Tanenbaum table.)*

## 2. Wireless networks (lecture §1.2)

A shared wireless access network connects the end system to a router via a **base station (access point)**. Types of wireless signal: **sound, light and mirrors, infrared, microwave**.

| Category | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **System interconnection** | Connecting the components of a computer using **short-range radio** | **Bluetooth**: cameras, headsets, scanners connect just by being in range |
| **Wireless LANs** | Small offices, homes, cafeterias, conference rooms; **over 300 Mbps over tens of metres** | **802.11b/g/n (WiFi)** |
| **Wireless WANs** | Wider-area wireless access **provided by a telco operator** | Mobile data (3G/4G) |

### Wireless LAN modes (lecture §1.5)
1. **With a base station** (access point): all traffic goes through the AP.
2. **Without a base station (ad hoc networking)**: computers send directly to each other.

A **multicell 802.11 network** has several cells, each with its own base station, and the base stations are connected by Ethernet. Details: [[04.06 IEEE 802.11 WiFi]].

### Challenges the 802.11 designers faced (lecture list)
Finding a frequency band available worldwide · finite range of radio signals · **user privacy** · **limited battery life** · **human safety** (do radio waves cause cancer?) · implications of **computer mobility** · enough **bandwidth** to be economically viable.

## 3. Advantages and disadvantages of wireless networks / WLAN

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Mobility**: work anywhere in range | **Lower security**: signals can be intercepted (needs WPA2) |
| **No cabling**: cheaper and faster to install, good for old buildings | **Interference** from other devices and walls |
| **Easy to add users** (scalability) | **Limited range** (tens of metres) |
| Supports laptops, phones, IoT devices | **Lower / shared bandwidth** than wired; slows with many users |
| Useful in temporary sites (events, disasters) | **Battery drain** on mobile devices; **health concerns** raised in lecture |

The disadvantage list is built from the lecture's design challenges (privacy, range, battery, safety, bandwidth).

## 4. Benefits and issues of mobile computing (short-note answer)

| Benefits | Issues |
|---|---|
| Access information and services anywhere, any time | Security and privacy of data on lost/stolen devices |
| Higher productivity (work while travelling) | Limited battery life and small screens |
| Real-time communication (calls, e-mail, messaging) | Dependence on network coverage; dropped connections |
| Location-based services (maps, ride hailing) | Lower bandwidth than wired, data cost |
| Supports business, health, education in the field | Health and safety concerns, distraction |

## 5. Usage scenarios (applied questions)

> [!example] Emergency healthcare (2018/19 Q2d)
> An ambulance paramedic uses a **tablet** with **4G** to send the patient's ECG, blood pressure and photos to the hospital while driving. Wearable sensors on the patient form a **BAN** (see [[01.09 Network Classification by Scale]]). The doctor views the data, advises treatment on a **video call**, and the emergency room prepares before arrival. GPS lets dispatch send the **nearest** ambulance.

> [!example] Online business (2020/21 Q2d)
> A small clothing shop sells through a **mobile app / website**. Customers browse and **pay by mobile wallet**; the owner receives orders on her phone, updates stock from anywhere, and the delivery rider uses GPS and confirms delivery on a phone. Push notifications deliver promotions.

## 6. Beyond the slides: WiFi vs Li-Fi and MANET

> [!warning] Not in the CMIS 3114 lecture slides
> **Li-Fi** and **MANET** are examined (2022/23 Q2(c), Q2(d)) but are not named in the slides. The closest lecture content is **lightwave/infrared transmission** ([[02.02 Wireless Transmission]]) and **ad hoc mode** ([[04.06 IEEE 802.11 WiFi]]). The material below is general networking knowledge.

| | **Wi-Fi** (IEEE 802.11) | **Li-Fi** |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | **Radio waves** (2.4 / 5 GHz) | **Visible light** from LED bulbs (photodetector receives) |
| Through walls | Yes | **No**: light is blocked, stays in the room |
| Range | Tens of metres | A few metres, needs line of sight |
| Speed | Hundreds of Mbps (802.11n up to 600 Mbps) | Very high in labs (Gbps) |
| Interference | Crowded radio spectrum | No radio interference; usable in hospitals, aircraft |
| Security | Signal leaks outside the building | More secure: light cannot pass walls |
| Maturity | Everywhere | Few products, needs lights to be on |

**MANET (Mobile Ad hoc Network):** a self-configuring network of **mobile devices with no fixed infrastructure** (no access point). Each node acts as a **router** that forwards packets for others, and the topology changes as nodes move.
- *Usage scenario:* disaster rescue: after a flood, rescue teams' radios/phones form a MANET where cellular towers are down. Also military field units, vehicle-to-vehicle networks.
- *Advantages:* no infrastructure, fast to deploy, self-healing when nodes move, low cost.
- *Disadvantages:* limited battery and bandwidth, frequent route changes, weaker security, hidden-terminal problems ([[04.05 Hidden and Exposed Terminals and MACA]]).
