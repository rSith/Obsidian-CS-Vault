## 2. Business applications

| Use | Lecture explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Resource sharing** | *"Make all programs, equipment, and especially data available to anyone on the network without regard to the physical location of the resource and the user."* | Office workers share a common printer |
| **Communication medium among employees** | E-mail, videoconferencing, people far apart writing a report together | Teams meeting between Colombo and Kandy branches |
| **Business with other businesses (B2B)** | Doing business electronically with other businesses | A manufacturer places orders with suppliers **in real time**, which *reduces the need for large inventories and enhances efficiency* |
| **Business with consumers (B2C) / e-commerce** | Doing business with consumers over the Internet | Airlines, bookstores and music vendors take orders online |
| **Computerised information** | Customer records, inventories, accounts receivable, financial statements, tax information online | Company database accessed from any branch |

Businesses usually use the **client–server model** → [[01.04 Client-Server and Peer-to-Peer Models]].

## 3. Home applications

| Use | Examples from the lecture |
|---|---|
| **Access to remote information** | Online newspapers, digital libraries, surfing the Web (arts, business, cooking, health, sports, travel…) |
| **Person-to-person communication** | E-mail, instant messaging, social networks, chat rooms, newsgroups, wiki (group collaboration), peer-to-peer communication, **VoIP**, remote desktop sharing |
| **Interactive entertainment** | Video on demand, game playing, **IPTV**, Internet radio/TV, interactive movies |
| **Electronic commerce** | Home shopping, access to financial institutions (paying bills, managing accounts, investments), **electronic flea markets** |

> **Electronic flea markets / online auctions** (e.g. eBay) are *"more of a peer-to-peer system, sort of consumer-to-consumer"*, unlike traditional e-commerce which is client–server.

E-commerce forms named on the slides: **B2C** (business-to-consumer), **B2B** (business-to-business), **G2C** (government-to-consumer), **C2C** (consumer-to-consumer), **P2P** (peer-to-peer). *(The slide is an image of the standard Tanenbaum table; the five names above are the standard entries.)*

## 4. Mobile users
People on the move want to send/receive calls, faxes, e-mail, surf the Web, access remote files and log on to remote machines, and stay **connected to their home base** even in cars and aeroplanes, where a wired connection is impossible. Full note: [[01.05 Mobile Computing and Wireless Networks]].

## 5. Applied scenario questions: how to answer them

These questions (pandemic, economic crisis, online business, emergency healthcare) test whether you can **apply** the categories. Use this structure:

1. Name the **category of use** (business / home / mobile).
2. Give the **network service** (e-mail, video conferencing, e-commerce, remote access…).
3. Give a **concrete Sri Lankan / real example**.
4. State the **benefit**.

> [!example] Pandemic (2018/19 Q1a), worked outline
> - **Work from home:** VPN/remote desktop + e-mail + video conferencing (Zoom/Teams) keep businesses running → *communication medium among employees*.
> - **Online education:** LMS (Moodle), recorded lectures, online exams → *access to remote information*.
> - **E-commerce and home delivery:** supermarkets and pharmacies take online orders → *B2C e-commerce*.
> - **Online banking and payments:** pay bills without going out → *access to financial institutions*.
> - **Telemedicine / health information:** doctor channelling, government health updates → *remote information + person-to-person communication*.
> - **Social contact and entertainment:** social networks, VoIP calls, video on demand.

> [!example] Restoring Sri Lanka's economy (2019/20 Q1d), three examples
> 1. **E-commerce and export markets (B2C/B2B):** small producers (tea, handicrafts, apparel) sell directly to foreign buyers online → foreign exchange.
> 2. **IT/BPO and remote work:** freelancers and software companies earn foreign income over the Internet; real-time ordering cuts inventory cost for manufacturers.
> 3. **E-government and digital payments:** online services reduce paperwork, travel and fuel use; digital payments improve tax collection and transparency.
> (Also acceptable: tourism promotion and online booking, e-learning to upskill workers, telemedicine.)
