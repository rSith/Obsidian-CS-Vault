# 01.06 · Social Issues of Computer Networks (and the Q1(d) Essay)

> [!info] [[01.00 Introduction|Lesson 1]] · Lecture ref Ch1 §1.1 (slides 43–46) · Priority 🔴 High (Q1(d) essay in 5/6 papers)
> **Asked as (Q1(d) statements):** "In this information age, the security of data is critical" (2020/21) · "Misinformation distribution through computer networks must be controlled" (2021/22) · "Emerging Artificial Intelligence trend has a significant impact on computer networks" (2022/23) · "Social Networks and AI have created uncertainty in the minds of their users" (2023/24)

## 1. Lecture content

The lecture lists these **social issues**:
- **Newsgroups**: trouble comes when groups discuss topics people care about (politics, religion, sex). Posts *"may be deeply offensive to some people"* or *"not politically correct."*
- **Employee rights vs employer rights**: *"Many employers have claimed the right to read and possibly censor employee messages."*
- **Universities vs students / high schools vs students**: e.g. a university turned off newsgroups it felt were inappropriate for minors (**censorship**).
- **Government vs citizen**: monitoring and control of communication.
- **Violation of privacy**: *"small files called **cookies** that Web browsers store on users' computers allow companies to **track users' activities** in cyberspace."* Cookies *"may allow credit card numbers, social security numbers, and other confidential information to leak all over the Internet."*
- **Identity theft**: *"Thieves collect enough information about a victim to obtain credit card information and other documents in the victim's name."*
- **Copyright violations**: illegal copying and sharing of music, films, software.

And from §1.1 "Problems": **security**, because distributed resources are harder to protect and *"the network itself [is] a target."*

## 2. How to write the Q1(d) essay

These are discussion questions. The lecture gives the **social** side; you must add **technical** points from general knowledge. Use this frame:

1. **Interpret the statement** in one or two sentences (agree, then qualify).
2. **Social aspects** (use lecture vocabulary): privacy, identity theft, rights (employee/employer, government/citizen), censorship vs freedom of speech, copyright, offensive content.
3. **Technical aspects**: security threats, controls, technology trends.
4. **Balanced conclusion**: benefits vs risks, and who must act (users, companies, government, law).

> [!example] "Security of data is critical" (2020/21), outline
> - *Why critical:* banking, health, e-commerce and government data are online; networks make resources **distributed, so harder to protect**, and the **network itself is a target** (lecture).
> - *Threats:* identity theft, credit-card leaks via cookies/tracking, malware, eavesdropping on **wireless** links, denial of service.
> - *Controls:* encryption (**WPA2/AES** on WiFi, HTTPS), authentication (passwords, 2FA), firewalls and proxies, backups and redundancy, user awareness.
> - *Social side:* privacy laws, employer monitoring vs employee privacy.

> [!example] "Misinformation must be controlled" (2021/22), outline
> - Networks spread content instantly to millions (social networks, newsgroups) → fake news can cause panic, violence, election manipulation, health risks.
> - Lecture tensions: **government vs citizen** (control vs freedom of expression), **censorship** (university/newsgroup example), offensive content.
> - Controls: platform fact-checking and labelling, reporting tools, digital-literacy education, laws against harmful falsehoods; risk that "control" becomes censorship.

> [!warning] Beyond the slides: AI topics (2022/23, 2023/24)
> AI is not covered in the CMIS 3114 slides. General points:
> - **Technical (AI → networks):** AI-driven traffic management and routing, anomaly/intrusion detection, predictive maintenance, self-configuring networks; AI workloads (cloud, video, LLM APIs) increase **bandwidth and data-centre traffic**, needing faster links and edge computing.
> - **Social:** deepfakes and AI-generated misinformation, privacy (data collected to train models, tracking via cookies), job changes, bias, dependence on algorithms; users become **uncertain** what is real or who sees their data (2023/24 wording).
> - **Balanced view:** benefits (personalisation, security automation) vs risks (manipulation, surveillance) → need regulation, transparency and user awareness.

## 3. Exam relevance
- 🔴 Q1(d) appears in 5/6 papers and always mixes **social + technical**. Spend about 10 minutes; write 6–8 clear points with headings.
- Use the lecture vocabulary (privacy, cookies, identity theft, rights, copyright) to show course knowledge.
