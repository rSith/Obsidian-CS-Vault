## Why this lesson matters
Direct methods ([[04.00 Gaussian Elimination]]) do a fixed amount of work, about $n^3$, and fill sparse matrices with non-zeros. **Iterative methods** start from a guess and improve it repeatedly using only multiplications by $A$. For large sparse systems (finite difference grids, networks) they are faster, need less memory and keep round-off under control. SOR ([[08.00 Successive Over-Relaxation]]) is a speeded-up Gauss-Seidel.

> [!warning] 2024 Q4(c)(d) system diverges
> The system $x+y+z=3,\ 2x-y-z=3,\ x-y+z=9$ is **not diagonally dominant**, and both Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel **diverge** on it (spectral radii 1.73 and 3.73). Knowing how to check convergence ([[05.03 Convergence of Iterative Methods]]) is what earns the marks there. See [[PP 05 - Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel]].
