## Why this lesson matters
Every number a computer produces is an **approximation**. This lesson gives the vocabulary to measure how wrong an answer is (true error, relative error), explains **where** errors come from (rounding, chopping, limited mantissa), and asks whether the *problem* is sensitive (conditioning) and whether the *algorithm* is trustworthy (stability).

In the exam the lesson has appeared in **every paper**, nearly always as an **applied scenario about rounding in GPA / marks calculation** at the Faculty of Applied Sciences:

| Paper | What was asked |
|---|---|
| 2023 | forward & backward error; impact of rounding on grades & GPA; method to minimise loss; write `floor`/`ceil` in Java |
| 2024 | relative error & true fractional relative error; max/min rounding error in final GPA + method to minimise it |
| 2025 | rounding error & chopping error in GPA (with given example); Java program computing GPA, round-off error, chopping error |
| 2026 | impact of rounding when scaling 500 → 100 marks, rounding marks for grading, calculating GPA; solutions |
