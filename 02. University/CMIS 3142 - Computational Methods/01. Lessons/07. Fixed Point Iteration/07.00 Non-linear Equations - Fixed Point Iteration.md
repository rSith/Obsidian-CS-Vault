## Why this lesson matters
Up to now the equations were **linear**. Many real equations are **non-linear**, e.g. $x^3 + x = 1$, $\sin x = 0.5x$, $\tan x = x$, and *"in general there are no direct formulas, except in a few simple cases"* (Kreyszig). They are solved by **iteration**: start from a guess $x_0$ and compute better and better approximations $x_1, x_2, \dots$ The same idea, $x^{(k)} = g(x^{(k-1)})$, drives Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel ([[05.00 Iterative Methods - Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel]]).

## Key terms
- **Algebraic equation:** $f$ is a polynomial (e.g. $x^3 + x - 1 = 0$); its solutions are called **roots**.
- **Transcendental equation:** involves transcendental functions such as $\sin$, $\cos$, $e^x$, $\ln$ (e.g. $2\sin x = x$).
- **Iteration method:** start from an initial guess $x_0$ (*sometimes this may be poor*) and compute step by step (in general better and better) approximations.

Kreyszig adds: iteration methods are **easy to program** (the same operations each step, only the data change) and, *if they converge, are stable in general*.
