# 09.02 · Forward, Backward and Central Differences, and Truncation Error

> [!info] [[09.00 Finite Difference Method|Lesson 9]] · Lecture 09 slides 6-4, 6-8 to 6-11, 6-23, 6-24 · Priority 🟡 Medium
> **Asked as:** "Define the forward difference, backward difference and central difference using the Taylor series expansion of a continuous differentiable function" (2026 Q3(a), 10 marks) · "Comment on the truncation error in the equations in part (a)" (2023 Q2(b), 3 marks).

## 1. What are they?
Three ways to approximate the first derivative $\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i$ at grid point $x_i$ using neighbouring values:

| Name | Uses points | Formula (equal spacing $\Delta x$) | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forward** difference (FDS) | $x_i$, $x_{i+1}$ (ahead) | $\dfrac{f_{i+1} - f_i}{\Delta x}$ | $O(\Delta x)$, 1st order |
| **Backward** difference (BDS) | $x_{i-1}$, $x_i$ (behind) | $\dfrac{f_i - f_{i-1}}{\Delta x}$ | $O(\Delta x)$, 1st order |
| **Central** difference (CDS) | $x_{i-1}$, $x_{i+1}$ (both sides) | $\dfrac{f_{i+1} - f_{i-1}}{2\Delta x}$ | $O(\Delta x^2)$, 2nd order |

```text
          B
   A  ●───●───●  C          forward  : slope of chord B→C
      i-1  i  i+1           backward : slope of chord A→B
                            central  : slope of chord A→C
```

## 2. Derivations from Taylor series (2026 Q3(a))

### (1) Forward difference (slide 6-8)
Expand about $x_i$ to $x_{i+1}$:
$$
f(x_{i+1}) = f(x_i) + \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i(x_{i+1} - x_i) + \frac{(x_{i+1}-x_i)^2}{2!}\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)_i + \frac{(x_{i+1}-x_i)^3}{3!}\left(\frac{\partial^3f}{\partial x^3}\right)_i + \dots \tag{a}
$$
Neglecting higher-order terms and rearranging:
$$
\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i \approx \frac{f(x_{i+1}) - f(x_i)}{x_{i+1} - x_i} = \frac{f(x_{i+1}) - f(x_i)}{\Delta x_{i+1}},\qquad \Delta x_{i+1} = x_{i+1} - x_i
$$

### (2) Backward difference (slide 6-9)
Expand about $x_i$ to $x_{i-1}$:
$$
f(x_{i-1}) = f(x_i) - \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i(x_i - x_{i-1}) + \frac{(x_i - x_{i-1})^2}{2!}\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)_i - \frac{(x_i - x_{i-1})^3}{3!}\left(\frac{\partial^3f}{\partial x^3}\right)_i + \dots \tag{b}
$$
$$
\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i \approx \frac{f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})}{x_i - x_{i-1}} = \frac{f(x_i) - f(x_{i-1})}{\Delta x_i}
$$

### (3) Central difference (slide 6-9)
Subtract (b) from (a): the $f(x_i)$ terms cancel and, for equal spacing, so do the second-derivative terms:
$$
f(x_{i+1}) - f(x_{i-1}) = 2\Delta x\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i + \frac{2(\Delta x)^3}{3!}\left(\frac{\partial^3f}{\partial x^3}\right)_i + \dots
$$
$$
\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i \approx \frac{f(x_{i+1}) - f(x_{i-1})}{x_{i+1} - x_{i-1}} \tag{c}
$$

### (4) Equal spacing $\Delta x_i = \Delta x_{i+1} = \Delta x$ (slide 6-10)
$$
\text{Forward: }\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i = \frac{f_{i+1} - f_i}{\Delta x}\ \ (d)\qquad
\text{Backward: }\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i = \frac{f_i - f_{i-1}}{\Delta x}\ \ (e)\qquad
\text{Central: }\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i = \frac{f_{i+1} - f_{i-1}}{2\Delta x}\ \ (f)
$$

## 3. Truncation error (slide 6-11)
> *The higher-order terms neglected in Eqs. (a), (b), (c) constitute the **truncation error**.*

$$
\text{Forward: }\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i = \frac{f_{i+1} - f_i}{\Delta x} + O(\Delta x)
$$
$$
\text{Backward: }\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i = \frac{f_i - f_{i-1}}{\Delta x} + O(\Delta x)
$$
$$
\text{Central: }\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)_i = \frac{f_{i+1} - f_{i-1}}{2\Delta x} + O(\Delta x)^2
$$

Where the orders come from:
- Forward: dividing (a) by $\Delta x$ leaves $\frac{\Delta x}{2}f''_i + \dots$ → leading error term proportional to $\Delta x$ → **first order** (lecture: "order of accuracy $P_{\text{kest}} = 1$").
- Central: the $f''$ terms cancel; the leading error is $\frac{(\Delta x)^2}{6}f'''_i$ → **second order** ($P = 2$).

**Meaning of order:** halving $\Delta x$ halves a first-order error but **quarters** a second-order error. Central differences are more accurate for the same step size, but need points on both sides.

### Numerical check: $f(x) = e^x$ at $x = 0$ (true $f'(0) = 1$)
| $\Delta x$ | Forward | error | Central | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 1.051709 | 0.0517 | 1.001668 | 0.00167 |
| 0.05 | 1.025422 | 0.0254 | 1.000417 | 0.00042 |
Halving $\Delta x$: forward error ÷2, central error ÷4 ✓.

## 4. Errors involved in numerical solutions (slides 6-23, 6-24)
- In the solution of differential equations with finite differences, many discretisation schemes exist; questions arise about **round-off** and **truncation** errors, and about the **consistency, stability and convergence** of the scheme.
- **Round-off errors:** *computations are rarely made in exact arithmetic … real numbers are represented in "floating point" form and errors are caused by rounding-off. In extreme cases such errors can accumulate and become a main source of error.* ([[02.02 Round-off and Chopping Errors]])
- **Truncation error:** *in finite difference representation of a derivative with Taylor's series expansion, the higher-order terms are neglected by truncating the series; the error caused is called the truncation error. The truncation error identifies the difference between the exact solution of a differential equation and its finite difference solution **without round-off error**.*

> [!note] Consistency, stability, convergence
> The slides name these three properties without defining them. Standard meanings (not from the course materials): **consistent** = the difference equation tends to the differential equation as $\Delta x \to 0$ (truncation error → 0); **stable** = errors (e.g. round-off) do not grow without bound as the computation proceeds; **convergent** = the numerical solution tends to the exact solution as $\Delta x \to 0$.

> [!tip] The step-size trade-off
> Smaller $\Delta x$ → smaller truncation error, but $f_{i+1} - f_i$ becomes a difference of nearly equal numbers → larger **round-off** (cancellation, [[02.03 Machine Precision and Arithmetic Round-off]]). Very small steps can make results worse.
