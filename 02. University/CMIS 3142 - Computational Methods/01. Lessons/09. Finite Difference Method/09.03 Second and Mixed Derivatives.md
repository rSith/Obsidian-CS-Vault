# 09.03 · Second Derivatives and Mixed Derivatives

> [!info] [[09.00 Finite Difference Method|Lesson 9]] · Lecture 09 slides 6-12, 6-13 · Priority 🟡 Medium
> **Asked as:** "Derive $\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x\partial y}$ from Taylor series using central difference, forward difference and backward difference where f is a function of x and y" (2023 Q2(a), 9 marks) · "Comment on the truncation error in the equations in part (a)" (2023 Q2(b), 3 marks).

## 1. Second derivatives (slide 6-12)
**Central difference.** If $\Delta x_i = \Delta x_{i+1} = \Delta x$, **adding** Taylor expansions (a) and (b) ([[09.02 First Derivative Differences and Truncation Error]]) cancels the odd-derivative terms:
$$
f_{i+1} + f_{i-1} = 2f_i + (\Delta x)^2\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)_i + \frac{2(\Delta x)^4}{4!}\left(\frac{\partial^4f}{\partial x^4}\right)_i + \dots
$$
$$
\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)_i = \frac{f_{i+1} - 2f_i + f_{i-1}}{(\Delta x)^2} + O(\Delta x)^2
$$

**Forward difference:**
$$
\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)_i = \frac{f_{i+2} - 2f_{i+1} + f_i}{(\Delta x)^2} + O(\Delta x)
$$

**Backward difference:**
$$
\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)_i = \frac{f_i - 2f_{i-1} + f_{i-2}}{(\Delta x)^2} + O(\Delta x)
$$

**Example:** $f(x) = x^3$ at $x = 1$, $\Delta x = 0.1$ (true $f''(1) = 6$). Central: $\frac{1.331 - 2 + 0.729}{0.01} = 6.000$ (exact here, because the error term involves $f''''$, which is 0 for a cubic). Forward: $\frac{1.728 - 2(1.331) + 1}{0.01} = 6.6$ (error 0.6, first order).

## 2. Mixed derivatives (slide 6-13)

### 2.1 Taylor series in two variables
$$
f(x + \Delta x, y + \Delta y) = f + \Delta x f_x + \Delta y f_y + \frac{(\Delta x)^2}{2!}f_{xx} + \frac{(\Delta y)^2}{2!}f_{yy} + 2\frac{\Delta x\,\Delta y}{2!}f_{xy} + O[(\Delta x)^3, (\Delta y)^3]
$$
(all derivatives at $(x, y) = (x_i, y_j)$; $f_{xy} = \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x\partial y}$). Write $h = \Delta x$, $k = \Delta y$ below. Including third-order terms:
$$
f(x+h, y+k) = f + hf_x + kf_y + \tfrac12\big(h^2f_{xx} + 2hkf_{xy} + k^2f_{yy}\big) + \tfrac16\big(h^3f_{xxx} + 3h^2kf_{xxy} + 3hk^2f_{xyy} + k^3f_{yyy}\big) + \dots
$$

### 2.2 Central difference (derivation for 2023 Q2(a))
Write the expansion at the four corner points $(x \pm h, y \pm k)$ by changing the signs of $h$ and $k$, and form
$$
S = f_{i+1,j+1} - f_{i-1,j+1} - f_{i+1,j-1} + f_{i-1,j-1}
$$
- The terms $f$, $hf_x$, $kf_y$, $h^2f_{xx}$, $k^2f_{yy}$ cancel (each appears with signs $+, -, -, +$ and the same value or paired opposite values).
- The $hkf_{xy}$ term has sign pattern $(+)(+)$, $-(-)(+)$, $-(+)(-)$, $(-)(-)$, which all give $+hk$: total $4hk\,f_{xy}$.
- Third-order terms ($h^2k$, $hk^2$, …) all cancel by symmetry; the first surviving error terms are fourth order ($h^3k$, $hk^3$).

$$
S = 4hk\,f_{xy} + O(h^3k,\ hk^3)
$$
$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x\partial y}\right)_{i,j} = \frac{f_{i+1,j+1} - f_{i-1,j+1} - f_{i+1,j-1} + f_{i-1,j-1}}{4\,\Delta x\,\Delta y} + O[(\Delta x)^2, (\Delta y)^2]}
$$

### 2.3 Forward difference
Use the points $(i,j)$, $(i+1,j)$, $(i,j+1)$, $(i+1,j+1)$:
$$
f_{i+1,j+1} = f + hf_x + kf_y + \tfrac12(h^2f_{xx} + 2hkf_{xy} + k^2f_{yy}) + \tfrac16(h^3f_{xxx} + 3h^2kf_{xxy} + 3hk^2f_{xyy} + k^3f_{yyy}) + \dots
$$
$$
f_{i,j+1} = f + kf_y + \tfrac12k^2f_{yy} + \tfrac16k^3f_{yyy} + \dots\qquad
f_{i+1,j} = f + hf_x + \tfrac12h^2f_{xx} + \tfrac16h^3f_{xxx} + \dots
$$
$$
f_{i+1,j+1} - f_{i,j+1} - f_{i+1,j} + f_{i,j} = hk\,f_{xy} + \tfrac12h^2k\,f_{xxy} + \tfrac12hk^2\,f_{xyy} + \dots
$$
Divide by $hk$:
$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x\partial y}\right)_{i,j} = \frac{f_{i+1,j+1} - f_{i,j+1} - f_{i+1,j} + f_{i,j}}{\Delta x\,\Delta y} + O(\Delta x, \Delta y)}
$$

### 2.4 Backward difference
Same steps with $-h$, $-k$, using $(i,j)$, $(i-1,j)$, $(i,j-1)$, $(i-1,j-1)$:
$$
f_{i,j} - f_{i-1,j} - f_{i,j-1} + f_{i-1,j-1} = hk\,f_{xy} - \tfrac12h^2k\,f_{xxy} - \tfrac12hk^2\,f_{xyy} + \dots
$$
$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x\partial y}\right)_{i,j} = \frac{f_{i,j} - f_{i-1,j} - f_{i,j-1} + f_{i-1,j-1}}{\Delta x\,\Delta y} + O(\Delta x, \Delta y)}
$$

### 2.5 Which grid points each formula uses
```text
 central:  (i-1,j+1) ·  · (i+1,j+1)      forward: (i,j+1) (i+1,j+1)     backward: (i-1,j) (i,j)
                 · (i,j) ·                         (i,j)   (i+1,j)                (i-1,j-1) (i,j-1)
           (i-1,j-1) ·  · (i+1,j-1)
```

## 3. Truncation error of the mixed-derivative formulas (2023 Q2(b))
| Formula | Leading neglected terms | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Central | fourth-order Taylor terms ÷ $4hk$ → terms in $h^2$, $k^2$ | $O[(\Delta x)^2, (\Delta y)^2]$, **second order** |
| Forward | $\frac12h f_{xxy} + \frac12k f_{xyy}$ | $O(\Delta x, \Delta y)$, **first order** |
| Backward | $-\frac12h f_{xxy} - \frac12k f_{xyy}$ | $O(\Delta x, \Delta y)$, **first order** |

Comment: the truncation error is caused by dropping the higher-order Taylor terms. It tends to zero as $\Delta x, \Delta y \to 0$ (the formulas are consistent). The central formula is more accurate (halving both steps divides its error by about 4, against 2 for the one-sided formulas), but needs points on all sides; forward/backward are useful at boundaries. Very small steps increase round-off error.

> [!note] Check the derivation numerically
> $f(x,y) = x^2y^3$ at $(1,1)$: $f_{xy} = 6xy^2 = 6$. With $h = k = 0.1$: central gives $6.02$ (error 0.02); forward gives $6.951$ (error 0.95) and backward $5.149$ (error 0.85). The central result is far more accurate, as the orders predict.
