# 04.01 · Systems of Linear Equations, Ax = b and the Augmented Matrix

> [!info] [[04.00 Gaussian Elimination|Lesson 4]] · Lecture 04 slides 2–4 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "Convert the above real world scenario into a system of linear equations to find the daily wages of each category" (2023 Q6(b)(i), 8 marks; 2024 Q6(b)(i)) · "Formulate a system of linear equations to find the price of each fruit" (2025 Q4(b)(i)).

## 1. What is it?
A **system of linear equations** is a set of equations where each unknown appears only to the first power (no $x^2$, $xy$, $\sin x$). Lecture example (slide 2):
$$
\begin{aligned}
-3x_1 + 2x_2 - x_3 &= -1\\
6x_1 - 6x_2 + 7x_3 &= -7\\
3x_1 - 4x_2 + 4x_3 &= -6
\end{aligned}
$$

### Matrix form $Ax = b$ (slide 3)
$$
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}-3&2&-1\\6&-6&7\\3&-4&4\end{bmatrix}}_{A\ \text{(coefficients)}}
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\x_3\end{bmatrix}}_{x\ \text{(unknowns)}}
=
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}-1\\-7\\-6\end{bmatrix}}_{b\ \text{(right-hand side)}}
$$

### Augmented matrix $[A \mid b]$ (slide 4)
Put $b$ as an extra column so that row operations act on both at once:
$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}-3&2&-1&-1\\6&-6&7&-7\\3&-4&4&-6\end{array}\right]
$$
In the program, $b$ is stored as the **$(n+1)$-th column** of $A$ ([[04.03 Gaussian Elimination Algorithm and Program]]).

## 2. Why is it used?
Many real problems reduce to $Ax = b$: prices from shopping totals, currents in circuits (Kirchhoff), forces in trusses, and the discretised equations of finite difference models ([[09.01 Taylor Series and Discretisation]]).

A **unique solution** exists when $A$ is square and non-singular ($\det A \neq 0$): a well-posed problem ([[02.05 Well-Posed Problems, Forward and Backward Error]]).

## 3. How to turn a word problem into equations
1. **Choose the unknowns** and say what each means (with units).
2. Each **given total** (sentence/row of data) becomes **one equation**: $\sum (\text{quantity} \times \text{unknown}) = \text{total}$.
3. Check: number of equations = number of unknowns.
4. Write the matrix form $Ax = b$ and the augmented matrix.

### Worked example 1: contractor (2023 Q6(b)(i), repeated in 2024 Q6(b)(i))
Groups: Teen, Man, Woman, Old. Quotation:
- 8 teens, 2 men, 3 women, 2 old → 189 \$/day
- 3 teens, 6 men, 2 women, 1 old → 192 \$/day
- 1 teen, 2 men, 9 women, 4 old → 233 \$/day
- 2 teens, 3 men, 4 women, 7 old → 224 \$/day

**Unknowns:** $t, m, w, o$ = daily wage (\$) of one teen, man, woman, old worker.

$$
\begin{aligned}
8t + 2m + 3w + 2o &= 189\\
3t + 6m + 2w + o &= 192\\
t + 2m + 9w + 4o &= 233\\
2t + 3m + 4w + 7o &= 224
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\left[\begin{array}{cccc|c}8&2&3&2&189\\3&6&2&1&192\\1&2&9&4&233\\2&3&4&7&224\end{array}\right]
$$
Solution (by GE, see [[PP 04 - Gaussian Elimination]]): $t = 10,\ m = 20,\ w = 15,\ o = 12$ \$ per day.

### Worked example 2: fruit prices (2025 Q4(b)(i))
Akila, Bala, Pathma have 15, 28, 18 rupees (spent exactly):
- Akila: 8 pineapples, 2 mangoes, 1 apple
- Bala: 4 pineapples, 9 mangoes, 2 apples
- Pathma: 1 pineapple, 1 mango, 5 apples

**Unknowns:** $p, m, a$ = price (Rs) of one pineapple, mango, apple.
$$
\begin{aligned}
8p + 2m + a &= 15\\
4p + 9m + 2a &= 28\\
p + m + 5a &= 18
\end{aligned}
$$
Solution: $p = 1,\ m = 2,\ a = 3$ rupees. This system is **diagonally dominant** ($8 > 2+1$, $9 > 4+2$, $5 > 1+1$), which is why Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel converge on it ([[05.03 Convergence of Iterative Methods]]).

## 4. Exam-relevant points
- **Define the unknowns explicitly** (with units): a common loss of marks.
- Write the system **three ways**: equations, $Ax = b$, augmented matrix.
- The assumption "the quoted amount equals the sum of individual wages" (linearity) can be stated.
