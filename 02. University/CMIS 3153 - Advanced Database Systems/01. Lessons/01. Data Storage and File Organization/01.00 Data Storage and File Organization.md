## Why this lesson matters (the big picture)
A database is just a very large collection of data that must **survive a power cut** and be **found quickly**. Lesson 1 answers two questions that everything else in the course builds on:

1. **Where does the data physically live?** Mostly on a hard disk, which is thousands of times slower than memory. So the single most important cost in a database is *how many disk blocks we have to read*.
2. **How are records arranged inside files on that disk?** In no order (heap), in sorted order, or scattered by a hash function. Each arrangement makes some operations cheap and others expensive.

> [!tip] Analogy for the whole lesson
> Think of a library. **RAM** is the desk you are working at (small, instant). The **disk** is the bookshelves across the room (huge, but you must walk there). A **file organization** is how books are placed on the shelves: thrown in anywhere (heap), sorted by title (sorted file), or put in a numbered shelf chosen by a formula (hashing). [[02.00 Indexing Structures|Indexes]] (Lesson 2) are the library catalogue cards.
