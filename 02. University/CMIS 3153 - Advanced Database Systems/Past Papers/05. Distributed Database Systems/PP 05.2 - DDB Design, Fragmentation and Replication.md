# PP 05.2 · DDB Design Issues, Homogeneous vs Heterogeneous, Fragmentation and Replication

> [!info] [[PP 05 - Distributed Database Systems|PP 05 index]] · Lessons: [[05.03 Transparency, Autonomy and Reliability]] §5, [[05.04 Fragmentation, Replication and Allocation]], [[05.05 Homogeneous, Heterogeneous and Federated Systems]]

## 2021/22 · Q3(b) [25 marks]
> **Question:** Explain possible issues associated with Distributed Database design.

### Expected answer
1. **Data fragmentation:** choosing the logical units (horizontal, vertical, mixed); fragments must be **complete** and **reconstructable** (union / outer union or join), ideally **disjoint**.
2. **Data allocation:** deciding which site stores each fragment, based on where and how often data is used; finding an optimal allocation is a **complex optimization problem**.
3. **Replication:** how many copies and where: more copies → better availability and reads, but **costly updates**, harder concurrency control and recovery.
4. **Distributed catalog/directory management:** keeping track of fragments, replicas and locations; global vs local directory placement.
5. **Distributed query processing:** communication cost (WAN) dominates; choosing strategies (which site does what, semijoins).
6. **Distributed transaction management and concurrency control:** multiple copies, distributed locking (primary site/copy, voting), **distributed deadlock**.
7. **Recovery and reliability:** site failures, **communication link failures**, **network partitioning**, **distributed commit** (2PC/3PC).
8. **Heterogeneity:** different DBMSs, data models, constraints, query languages (federated systems).
9. **Security** across sites and networks; **autonomy** vs global control.
10. **Consistency vs availability trade-off** (CAP).

### How to approach
Group issues under *data distribution* (1–4), *processing* (5), *transactions/reliability* (6–7), *other* (8–10); 1–2 sentences each.

## 2021/22 · Q3(c) [25 marks]
> **Question:** Compare homogeneous and heterogeneous databases.

### Expected answer
- **Homogeneous DDBS:** **all sites run the same DBMS software** (e.g. all Oracle), are **aware of each other** and **agree to cooperate**; local sites give up some autonomy (cannot freely change schema or software); operating systems may differ.
- **Heterogeneous DDBS:** sites may use **different DBMSs and different schemas** (even data models); they may **not be aware** of each other and offer **limited cooperation**. Schema differences complicate **query processing**; software differences complicate **transactions** across sites. Sub-types: **federated** (single global schema, minimum local autonomy) and **multidatabase** (no global schema, full autonomy). Sources of heterogeneity: **data models, constraints, query languages**.

| | Homogeneous | Heterogeneous |
|---|---|---|
| DBMS software | identical | different |
| Schemas / data models | common | may differ |
| Cooperation | full | limited |
| Local autonomy | lower | higher |
| Query processing | straightforward | translation/integration needed |
| Transactions | standard protocols (2PC) | difficult |
| Design/admin | simpler | complex (schema integration) |
| Typical origin | built as a DDB | integrating existing/legacy systems |

## 2021/22 · Q3(d) [25 marks]
> **Question:** Describe the data distribution approaches *Data Fragmentation* and *Replication*, highlighting their benefits and drawbacks compared to a centralised database.

### Expected answer
**Fragmentation:** splitting relations into fragments stored at different sites.
- **Horizontal:** subsets of rows, $\sigma_C(R)$ (e.g. EMPLOYEE where Dno = 5 at department 5's site); rebuilt by **UNION**. **Derived horizontal** fragmentation follows a related relation's fragments.
- **Vertical:** subsets of columns, $\pi_L(R)$, **each including the primary key**; rebuilt by outer union / join on the key.
- **Mixed:** $\pi_L(\sigma_C(R))$.
**Replication:** storing copies of fragments at more than one site: **full** (whole DB everywhere), **partial**, or **none**.

| | Benefits vs centralized | Drawbacks vs centralized |
|---|---|---|
| **Fragmentation** | **data locality** (stored where used) → less network traffic, faster local access; **parallel** query execution; smaller relations to scan; local control/security of sensitive data | queries needing several fragments require **distributed joins/unions** and communication; integrity constraints across fragments harder; complex design; reconstruction overhead |
| **Replication** | **higher availability and reliability** (no single point of failure); **faster reads** from a nearby copy; load sharing; disaster recovery | **updates must propagate** to all copies (slow writes); **consistency**, concurrency control and recovery much more complex; more storage; risk of stale data |
| (Centralized DB) | simple, strongly consistent, no communication within | single point of failure, latency for remote users, limited scalability |

Lecture guidance: full replication suits high availability with mostly read-only transactions; data used mainly at one site should live only there; limit replication when updates are frequent.

## 2023/24 · Q6(b) [25 marks] and Q7(d)(i) [15 marks]
> **Questions:** Discuss appropriate data fragmentation and replication strategies for storing the customer orders database across the company's three locations (Colombo, London, Singapore), with justification. · A multinational retail company maintains separate databases for its branches in New York, London, and Singapore … Suggest appropriate data distribution strategies (fragmentation, replication, or allocation).

### Expected answer
**Fragmentation**
- **Orders (and OrderItems): horizontal fragmentation by branch/region**: $Orders_{Colombo} = \sigma_{branch='Colombo'}(Orders)$, etc. *Justification:* each branch creates and mostly queries its **own** orders → local, fast processing; branches keep working if the WAN fails; fragments are **complete and disjoint**, rebuilt by **UNION** for global reports.
- **OrderItems/Payments: derived horizontal fragmentation** following their Order, so an order and its lines are at the same site (local joins).
- **Customers:** horizontal by home region; optionally **vertical** fragmentation separating sensitive personal/payment columns (kept at the home site, data-protection laws) from general profile columns (both keep Customer_ID).

**Replication**
- **Product catalogue, prices, branch list, exchange rates** (read-heavy, rarely updated): **fully replicated** at all three sites → local reads, high availability.
- **Order fragments:** **not fully replicated** (constant inserts would have to be applied at every site); keep the primary copy at the home branch plus **one asynchronous replica** at another site or a head-office/reporting site → **disaster recovery** and global reporting.
- **Customer data needed by several branches** (global customers): partial replication to those branches.

**Allocation**
| Fragment | Site(s) |
|---|---|
| Orders/OrderItems of branch X | branch X (primary) + backup replica at partner site |
| Product catalogue, prices | all sites |
| Customers of region X | region X (+ replica where frequently accessed) |
| Global analytics | data warehouse / head office (periodic loads) |

**Justification summary:** maximizes **locality** (most transactions single-site, low latency), **availability** (local operation during partitions; replicas for failures), keeps **update cost** low (write-heavy data not replicated everywhere), supports **fragmentation transparency** for global queries, and respects data-residency rules.

### How to approach
Three headings (fragmentation, replication, allocation) → for each table, the strategy **and a justification tied to read/write patterns and locations**.
