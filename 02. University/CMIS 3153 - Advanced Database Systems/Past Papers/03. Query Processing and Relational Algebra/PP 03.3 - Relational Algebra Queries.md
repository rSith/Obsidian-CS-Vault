> [!tip] Method for every question
> **Filter → Join → Reduce.** "not" = difference; "or" = union (or ∨); "and" across two facts = intersection/join on the key; "at least two" = self-join with ρ and ≠.

## 2020/21 · Q7(d)(i)–(vi) [5 marks × 6] (identical to Tutorial 3 Q4)
Schema: `lives(person-name, street, city)`, `works(person-name, company-name, salary)`, `located-in(company-name, city)`, `manages(person-name, manager-name)`. (Short names: pname, cname, mname.)

**(i) Names of all employees who work for City Bank.**
$$\pi_{pname}(\sigma_{cname='City\ Bank'}(works))$$
**(ii) Name and city of all employees who work for City Bank.**
$$\pi_{pname,\,city}(\sigma_{cname='City\ Bank'}(works) \bowtie lives)$$
**(iii) Name, street and city of all employees who work for City Bank and earn more than \$10,000.**
$$\pi_{pname,\,street,\,city}(\sigma_{cname='City\ Bank' \wedge salary>10000}(works) \bowtie lives)$$
**(iv) All employees who live in the same city as the company they work for.**
$$\pi_{pname}(lives \bowtie works \bowtie located\text{-}in)$$
(natural join on pname, then on cname **and** city, so person's city = company's city)
**(v) All persons who do not work for City Bank.**
$$\pi_{pname}(lives) - \pi_{pname}(\sigma_{cname='City\ Bank'}(works))$$
**(vi) All employees who live in the same city and on the same street as their manager.**
$$\pi_{pname}\big(lives \bowtie manages \bowtie \rho_{M(mname,\,street,\,city)}(lives)\big)$$
(second copy of lives renamed so its person column = mname; natural join then matches manager, street and city)

Detailed explanations: [[TUT 3 - Relational Model and Relational Algebra]].

## 2021/22 · Q5 (Suppliers / Parts / Catalog)
`Suppliers(sID, sName, address)`, `Parts(pID, pName, colour)`, `Catalog(sID, pID, price)`; $Catalog[sID] \subseteq Suppliers[sID]$, $Catalog[pID] \subseteq Parts[pID]$.

### (a) [20 marks] If sID is a key for Suppliers, could it be a key for Catalog? Discuss.
**No (in general).** In Suppliers, sID is unique: one row per supplier. In Catalog, sID is a **foreign key** (inclusion dependency into Suppliers). A supplier normally supplies **many parts**, so the same sID appears in many Catalog rows, e.g. (s1, p1, 10), (s1, p2, 25): sID is **not unique** there. The key of Catalog is the **composite (sID, pID)** (a supplier lists each part once with one price). sID could only be a key of Catalog under an extra constraint that each supplier supplies **at most one** part. Being a key is a property **of a relation**, not of an attribute name: uniqueness in Suppliers says nothing about uniqueness in Catalog.

### (b) [80 marks] Using only σ, π, × and natural join
**(i) Names of all red parts.**
$$\pi_{pName}(\sigma_{colour='red'}(Parts))$$
**(ii) All prices for parts that are red or green.**
$$\pi_{price}\big(\sigma_{colour='red' \vee colour='green'}(Parts) \bowtie Catalog\big)$$
(natural join on pID)
**(iii) sIDs of suppliers who supply a red or green part.**
$$\pi_{sID}\big(\sigma_{colour='red' \vee colour='green'}(Parts) \bowtie Catalog\big)$$
**(iv) sIDs of suppliers who supply a red and a green part.** (∩ is not allowed, so use a natural join on sID, which acts as an intersection)
$$\pi_{sID}\big(\sigma_{colour='red'}(Parts) \bowtie Catalog\big) \bowtie \pi_{sID}\big(\sigma_{colour='green'}(Parts) \bowtie Catalog\big)$$
Both operands have the single attribute sID, so the natural join keeps only sIDs present in both. (A single part cannot be both red and green, so "a red and green part" must mean one red part **and** one green part.)
**(v) Names of suppliers who supply a red or green part.**
$$\pi_{sName}\big(\sigma_{colour='red' \vee colour='green'}(Parts) \bowtie Catalog \bowtie Suppliers\big)$$

## 2022/23 · Q4(c) (employee / works / company / manages)
`employee(person-name, street, city)`, `works(person-name, company-name, salary)`, `company(company-name, city)`, `manages(person-name, manager-name)`.

### (i) [3 × 10 marks]
**1. Names of all employees who work for First Bank Corporation.**
$$\pi_{person\text{-}name}(\sigma_{company\text{-}name='First\ Bank\ Corporation'}(works))$$
**2. Names and cities of residence of all employees who work for First Bank Corporation.**
$$\pi_{person\text{-}name,\,city}\big(employee \bowtie \sigma_{company\text{-}name='First\ Bank\ Corporation'}(works)\big)$$
**3. Names, street addresses and cities of employees who work for First Bank Corporation and earn more than \$10,000 per annum.**
$$\pi_{person\text{-}name,\,street,\,city}\big(\sigma_{company\text{-}name='First\ Bank\ Corporation' \wedge salary>10000}(works) \bowtie employee\big)$$

### (ii) [20 marks] Describe, compare
1. $\pi_{person\text{-}name}(\sigma_{company\text{-}name \ne 'First\ Bank\ Corporation'}(works))$
2. $\pi_{person\text{-}name}(employee) - \pi_{person\text{-}name}(\sigma_{company\text{-}name \ne 'First\ Bank\ Corporation'}(works))$

**Expression 1:** names of people who **work for at least one company other than** First Bank Corporation.
**Expression 2:** all employees **minus** those who work for some company other than FBC → employees who **do not work for any company other than FBC**, i.e. those who work **only for FBC**, **plus** those in `employee` who work for **no company at all**.
**Similarities:** both use the same selection $\sigma_{company\text{-}name \ne FBC}(works)$ and project person names; both return a set of names.
**Differences:** (1) is a simple selection-projection; (2) uses **set difference** and is essentially the **complement** of (1) with respect to the employee relation. They are **disjoint** and together cover every employee. Neither is the same as "people who do not work for FBC": a person working for FBC **and** another company appears in (1); "employees not working for FBC" needs $\pi(employee) - \pi(\sigma_{=FBC}(works))$.

## 2023/24 · Q4(c) (Sailors / Boats / Reserves)
`S(sid, sname, rating, age)`, `B(bid, bname, colour)`, `R(sid, bid, date)`.

### (i) [10 × 3 marks]
**1. Colours of boats reserved by Albert.**
$$\pi_{colour}\big(\sigma_{sname='Albert'}(S) \bowtie R \bowtie B\big)$$
**2. IDs of sailors with a rating of at least 8 or who reserved boat 103.**
$$\pi_{sid}(\sigma_{rating \ge 8}(S)) \cup \pi_{sid}(\sigma_{bid=103}(R))$$
**3. Names of sailors who have not reserved a red boat.**
$$\pi_{sname}\Big(\big(\pi_{sid}(S) - \pi_{sid}(\sigma_{colour='red'}(B) \bowtie R)\big) \bowtie S\Big)$$
(subtract the IDs, not the names, because two sailors could share a name)

### (ii) [10 × 2 marks] Describe, compare
1. $\pi_{sid}(\sigma_{age>20}(S)) - \pi_{sid}(\sigma_{colour='red'}(B) \bowtie R)$
2. $\pi_{S.sname}\big[(\sigma_{R.sid=R2.sid \wedge R.bid \ne R2.bid}(R \times \rho_{R2}(R))) \bowtie S\big]$

**Expression 1:** IDs of sailors **older than 20 who have not reserved any red boat** (older-than-20 sailors minus sailors who reserved a red boat; "not" via difference).
**Expression 2:** **names of sailors who have reserved at least two different boats**: $R \times \rho_{R2}(R)$ forms every pair of reservations; σ keeps pairs by the **same sailor** for **different boats**; joining S attaches the sailor's name (the printed brackets are loose: the selection must apply to $R \times \rho_{R2}(R)$).
**Similarities:** both are about sailors and their reservations; both use a projection to produce sailor identifiers/names; both involve R.
**Differences:** (1) uses **set difference** (negation) and returns **sids**; (2) uses **rename + Cartesian product (self-join)** with a **≠** condition (counting "at least two") and returns **names**; (1) involves Boats (colour) while (2) does not; (1) includes sailors with **no** reservations at all, (2) requires **two or more**.

## How to approach RA questions
1. Write the schema abbreviations you use.
2. For each query: identify output columns, tables, filters; write the expression; one line of explanation.
3. Check natural joins don't accidentally match extra same-named columns.
4. For "describe" questions: read inside-out, translate to English, then give **similarities** and **differences** explicitly.
