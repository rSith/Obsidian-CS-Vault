## 2020/21 · Q7(b) [25 marks]
> **Question:** Discuss the operator precedence and associativity of relational algebra operators. Use examples where necessary.

> [!warning] Not stated in the lecture notes
> The lecture defines the operators and lists associativity among the transformation rules (rule 9), but gives **no precedence table**. The convention below is the usual textbook one. This information could not be verified from the available course materials.

### Expected answer
**Precedence (highest to lowest), conventional:**
1. **Unary operators** σ (select), π (project), ρ (rename): bind tightest and apply to the expression immediately to their right: $\pi_A \sigma_c R = \pi_A(\sigma_c(R))$.
2. **Cartesian product and joins** ×, ⋈, ⋈θ.
3. **Intersection** ∩.
4. **Union and difference** ∪, − (equal precedence, evaluated **left to right**).
Example: $\pi_{name}\sigma_{age>20} S \bowtie R \cup T$ is read as $\big((\pi_{name}(\sigma_{age>20}(S))) \bowtie R\big) \cup T$. Because conventions vary, **always use parentheses** in practice.

**Associativity (lecture transformation rule 9)**
- ×, ⋈, ∪ and ∩ are **associative**: $(R \bowtie S) \bowtie T = R \bowtie (S \bowtie T)$; $(R \cup S) \cup T = R \cup (S \cup T)$. Combined with **commutativity** (rules 5, 8) this lets the optimizer **reorder joins** freely.
- **Difference is not associative** (nor commutative): $(R - S) - T \ne R - (S - T)$. Example: $R = \{1, 2\}$, $S = \{1\}$, $T = \{2\}$: $(R - S) - T = \{2\} - \{2\} = \emptyset$, but $R - (S - T) = \{1,2\} - \{1\} = \{2\}$.
- Operators of the **same precedence without associativity** (−) are evaluated **left to right**.
- Unary operators nest from right to left; a cascade of σ can be reordered (rules 1–2), a cascade of π reduces to the outermost π (rule 3).

**Why it matters:** correct reading of an expression determines its meaning; associativity/commutativity are what make **join reordering** in query optimization valid.

## 2020/21 · Q7(c) [25 marks]
> **Question:** Is $\pi_A(E_1 - E_2)$ equal to $\pi_A(E_1) - \pi_A(E_2)$? Discuss.

### Expected answer
**No, not in general.** Projection distributes over **union** (lecture rule 11: $\pi_L(R \cup S) = \pi_L(R) \cup \pi_L(S)$) but **not over difference**.

**Counter-example:** relations on attributes (A, B):
$E_1 = \{(a_1, b_1)\}$, $E_2 = \{(a_1, b_2)\}$.
- Left: $E_1 - E_2 = \{(a_1, b_1)\}$ (the tuples differ) → $\pi_A = \{a_1\}$.
- Right: $\pi_A(E_1) = \{a_1\}$, $\pi_A(E_2) = \{a_1\}$ → $\{a_1\} - \{a_1\} = \emptyset$.
- $\{a_1\} \ne \emptyset$.

**Reason:** projection can make **different tuples identical** (it discards attribute B). Subtracting **after** projecting removes an A-value whenever **any** tuple of $E_2$ has it, even if the specific $E_1$ tuple was not in $E_2$.

**General relationship:** $\pi_A(E_1) - \pi_A(E_2) \subseteq \pi_A(E_1 - E_2)$.
**When equal:** if A contains a **key** of the relations (so projection does not merge distinct tuples), or if $E_1$ and $E_2$ agree on all other attributes for equal A-values.
**Optimization consequence:** an optimizer may push π through ∪ but **must not** push π through − (it would give wrong answers).

### How to approach
Answer "No" → counter-example with tiny relations → explain the reason → state the subset relation and when equality holds → link to transformation rules.
