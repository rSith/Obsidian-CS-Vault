# 04.02 · Colour: Psychology, Schemes and Best Practices

> [!info] [[04.00 Web Design Principles|Lesson 4]] · Lecture ref L4.3–L4.5 · Priority 🟡 Medium (**taught, not yet examined**). It is also useful in scenario questions such as "features not to include" for a religious-artefact store (2019/20 Q5(b)(ii)).

## 1. Role of colour (lecture)
- A website's **colour scheme should enhance the page** and **help establish or reinforce branding**.
- It **should provide good contrast**.
- **Different colours spark different emotions**. *Studies show colour can be about **85% of the reason people make buying decisions***.

## 2. Colour psychology (lecture infographic)

| Colour | ✅ When to use | ❌ When not to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | Draw attention to particular content; recommended for **CTA buttons**; food, fashion, marketing, sports, advertising | Too much looks **aggressive**; avoid for **professional services or luxury goods** |
| **Orange** | Portray **energy and power**; targeting **kids** (a favourite colour) or adult buyers; **good for CTAs**; black and white go well with orange | Hard to combine with warm colours (yellow, red); **hard to read as text** |
| **Yellow** | Energy, happiness, sunshine; hope, uplifting; catch attention to a CTA | Becomes **overpowering** quickly; too much causes **anxiety, nervousness, agitation** |
| **Blue** | **Relaxing, calming**; loyalty and strength; preferred by **IT, security, financial** institutions and large organisations | **Curbs appetite**, so bad for **food** websites; too much feels "cool" (cold) |
| **Green** | Nature, growth, balance; **dark green = wealth/prestige**, **light green = freshness/growth**; environmental and health themes; calming, new beginnings | Avoid for sites selling **luxury or tech products** |
| **Gray** | **Professionalism, sophistication**; neutral **background** colour; good for **luxury goods**; calming, balancing | Some shades are **dull**, not good for attracting attention |
| **Pink** | Love, kindness, sincerity; **feminine products**, young girls or women; designers pair it with red for backgrounds | Avoid if **men are targeted**; needs the right combinations |
| **White** | **Purity, cleanliness, safety, happiness**; calm and peace; **healthcare**, science and latest-technology sites | Be careful when **pairing it** with other colours |

## 3. Colour vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hue** | The dominant **colour family** of a colour (e.g. "blue") |
| **Tint** | Hue + **white** (lighter) |
| **Tone** | Hue + **grey** (muted) |
| **Shade** | Hue + **black** (darker) |

## 4. Colour combinations (schemes)

| Scheme | How it is built on the colour wheel | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Monochromatic** | Varying shades, tints and tones of **one hue**. Start with a (usually darker) base and choose **at least two other shades** of it | Calm, unified |
| **Analogous** | Colours **next to each other** on the wheel: a **dominant**, a **supporting** and an **accent** colour | Usually a good match; **eye-pleasing** |
| **Complementary** | Two hues **directly opposite** each other, typically **one warm + one cool** | **Strong contrast** side by side |
| **Triadic** | Three colours at the **corners of an equilateral triangle** | Vibrant but balanced |
| **Split-complementary** | One colour + the **two colours adjacent to its complement** (a triangle) | Contrast with less tension than complementary |
| **Tetradic / rectangle / double-complementary** | **Two colours + their two opposites** (a rectangle) | Rich, many colours |
| **Square** | Two colours + their opposites, **evenly distributed** (every 90°) | Balanced, bold |

```text
        Yellow
   Y-Green   Y-Orange          Complementary: Blue ↔ Orange
  Green          Orange        Analogous:     Blue, Blue-Green, Green
 B-Green          R-Orange     Triadic:       Red, Yellow, Blue
  Blue             Red         Square:        every 90° around the wheel
   B-Violet   R-Violet
        Violet
```

## 5. Choosing colours: best practices (lecture)
1. **Analyse your target audience.** The slide says mostly men prefer bright colours and women prefer soft colours.
2. **Check which colours work in your industry:**
   - Blue: banks, healthcare, IT, utilities, legal, dentistry
   - Red: retail, automotive, food, fashion
   - Green: medical, finance, recruitment, tourism
   - Gray: journalism, sports stores, technology, automotive
   - Black: cosmetics, construction, finance, fashion, advertising and marketing
   - Orange: fitness, retail · Yellow: retail, food, technology, automotive · Pink: cosmetics, retail
3. **The 60-30-10 rule:** 60% **dominant** colour, 30% **secondary** colour, 10% **accent** colour.
4. **Design in greyscale first.** This helps you spot layout problems and check the design against the **rule of continuity** before colour distracts you.
5. **Do not use complementary colours for text and background**, because they "vibrate" and are hard to read.
6. **Use bright colours for call-to-action (CTA) buttons.**
7. **Understand the interplay between base, accent and text colours:**
   - **Base colour:** the fundamental colour you build on;
   - **Accent colour:** for emphasis and contrast;
   - **Text colour** (black, white or grey): adjusted to the background for contrast and readability.
8. **Use negative space.** Balance positive and negative space; you don't need to fill every inch with colour. Empty space gives a **clean, uncluttered look**.

### The 60-30-10 rule in CSS
```css
:root {
  --dominant: #f5f7fa;  /* 60% – page background */
  --secondary: #1f3b5c; /* 30% – header, footer, cards */
  --accent: #ff6a00;    /* 10% – CTA buttons, highlights */
}
body   { background: var(--dominant); }
header { background: var(--secondary); color: #fff; }
.cta   { background: var(--accent); color: #fff; }
```
