# 04.04 · Functional and Usable Design

> [!info] [[04.00 Web Design Principles|Lesson 4]] · Lecture ref L4.8–L4.9 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "What is usability in web design? Describe two usability testing methods." (2023/24 Q8(a), 5 marks; testing methods in [[05.10 Usability Testing Methods]]). Accessibility basics support 2023/24 Q8(b).

## 1. Functional design
A **functional** website **"renders without error and functions as expected"**:
- **All internal and external links work**;
- **All forms of interactivity function** (forms, buttons, menus);
- **The page loads quickly**.

## 2. Usability

### What is it?
**"Usability assesses how easy a user interface is to use."** It **measures the quality of a person's experience while interacting with a website**.

**Examples (lecture):**
- The site **anticipates and responds to the needs of visitors** (e.g. an **FAQ** page);
- Visitors can **quickly and easily locate needed information**.

### HCI and the five usability attributes
**Human–Computer Interaction (HCI)** is *"the study, planning, design and uses of the interaction between people and computers"* (more in [[05.02 HCI Goals and Factors]]).

| Attribute | Question it asks (lecture) | Web example |
|---|---|---|
| **Learnability** | How easy is it for people to use **the first time** they try? | A new visitor can place an order without help |
| **Efficiency** | Once familiar, how **quickly** can users do what they want? | A returning customer re-orders in two clicks |
| **Memorability** | After a break, how easily can they become familiar **again**? | You return after 3 months and still know where things are |
| **Errors** | How **many errors** do users make, how quickly do they **recover**, how much trouble is it to fix them? | Clear message: "Card number must be 16 digits" |
| **Satisfaction** | How **pleasant** is it to use? | A clean, fast, attractive site |

> [!tip] Mnemonic
> **LEMES:** Learnability, Efficiency, Memorability, Errors, Satisfaction.

## 3. Writing web content (lecture)
- **Do not use industry jargon or slang.**
- **Be cautious using humour or clever headings.**
- **Write headings that clearly communicate the content** of the page or subtopic.
- **Do not underline text or headings**, because they **may be confused with hyperlinks**.

## 4. Browser compatibility
**Websites should be tested in a variety of browsers and devices to verify that they display consistently.** This links to configuration testing ([[03.04 Testing WebApps and Best Practices]]) and responsive design ([[06.00 Responsive Web Design]]).

## 5. Accessibility basics (lecture)
**Accessibility** is **"the practice of making websites usable by people of all abilities and disabilities."**

| Guideline | Why |
|---|---|
| Provide **text links as an alternative to image links** | Screen readers can read text |
| Choose **high contrast** between background and text | Low-vision users can read it |
| **Do not use colour alone to convey meaning** | Colour-blind users and screen-reader users would miss it |
| Provide **alternative text** for images and other visuals | Screen readers read the alt text aloud |
| **Summarise tables and provide headings** for line-by-line reading | Screen readers read tables cell by cell |
| Provide **transcripts for audio** and **captions for video** | Deaf and hard-of-hearing users |

```html
<img src="logo.png" alt="Wayamba University logo">          <!-- alt text -->
<p class="error">⚠ Error: email is required</p>               <!-- not colour alone: icon + text -->
<video controls><track kind="captions" src="talk.vtt" srclang="en"></video>
```
Full coverage: [[09.00 Web Accessibility]].

## 6. Multimedia and interactivity (lecture)
- **Multimedia** = *"the combined use of audio, video, animation and other interactive features."*
- **Common interactivity components:** clicking a link; moving the mouse to make an image appear; clicking buttons on a form or survey; customising a page's view or contents; watching a video or listening to audio; taking surveys or live chats.
- *"Multimedia and interactivity can make web pages more interesting and informative."*

## 7. Important distinction: functionality vs usability
| Functionality | Usability |
|---|---|
| Does it **work**? (links, forms, speed) | Is it **easy and pleasant** to use? |
| Checked by testing for errors | Measured by user experience (LEMES) |

A site can be fully functional and still have poor usability, for example when every feature works but the menu is confusing.
