# 05.03 · Understanding Your Materials: Users, Affordance and Norman's Doors

> [!info] [[05.00 HCI in Web Design|Lesson 5]] · Lecture ref L5.3, L5.7 · Priority 🟢 Low

## 1. Understand your materials (lecture)
Good design comes from understanding three things:
| Material | What to understand |
|---|---|
| **Users** | **Psychological and social aspects**; **human error** (people make mistakes, so design for them) |
| **Computers** | **Limitations, capacities, tools, platforms** |
| **Their interactions** | How the two meet: the interface |

## 2. Don Norman and "Norman's doors"
- **Don Norman** is *"a pioneer in user experience (UX) and human–computer interaction."*
- He introduced the concepts of **affordance** and **user-centred design**, *emphasising the importance of intuitive and easy-to-use products*.
- A **"Norman door"** is a door whose design suggests the wrong action, for example a pull handle on a door that must be pushed. **If a door needs a "PUSH" sign, its design has failed.**

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> The slide shows a picture and the two concepts. The door explanation is the well-known example that the slide title refers to.

**User-centred design** means designing around the **needs, abilities and behaviour of real users**, rather than around the technology or the designer's preferences.

## 3. Affordance
**Lecture definition:** *"a psychological idea: things may suggest **by their shape and other attributes** what you can do to them."*

| Object | It "affords"… |
|---|---|
| A mug handle | **grasping** (lecture example) |
| A button-like screen object | a **mouse click** (lecture example) |
| Icons | **clicking or double-clicking**, learned from the **"culture of computer use"** |
| A text box with a blinking cursor | typing |
| An underlined blue word | clicking (a link) |

**Why it matters on the web:** if a clickable item does not look clickable (flat text instead of a button), users miss it. If non-clickable text is underlined, users try to click it (the reason for Lesson 4's "don't underline text" rule).

```html
<!-- Good affordance: looks like a button and is one -->
<button class="btn">Checkout</button>

<!-- Poor affordance: clickable text that looks like normal text -->
<span onclick="checkout()">checkout</span>
```
