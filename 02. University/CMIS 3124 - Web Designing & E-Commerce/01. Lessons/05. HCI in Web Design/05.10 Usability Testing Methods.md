# 05.10 · Usability Testing Methods (and OOHDM)

> [!info] [[05.00 HCI in Web Design|Lesson 5]] · Lecture ref L5.12–L5.13 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "What is usability in web design? Describe two (02) usability testing methods." (2023/24 Q8(a), 5 marks)

## 1. What is usability testing?
**Usability** is *how easy a user interface is to use*. It is measured by **learnability, efficiency, memorability, errors and satisfaction** ([[04.04 Functional and Usable Design]]).
**Usability testing** means **watching real users try real tasks** on the website to find where they struggle.

## 2. The seven methods (lecture)

| Method | How it works | ✅ Pros | ❌ Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In-person / in-house** | **On-site**; **moderated and observed by researchers** who give instructions to each participant and evaluate the test; participants should be people who **frequently use similar websites** | **In-depth information**; see *how* users interact; analyse usability issues | **Needs more resources** and **qualified researchers**; only a **small group per session** |
| **Unmoderated remote** | **No moderator or controlled environment**; participants do the test **anywhere on their own device** | **Less costly**; done in their **own environment**, so it reflects **real-world usage** | *(beyond the slides)* Less control; no follow-up questions |
| **Moderated remote** | Participants are in **their own environment**, but a **moderator instructs and observes** them (a **combination** of in-person and unmoderated remote) | *(beyond the slides)* Real environment plus guidance | Needs **trained moderators** to communicate with participants |
| **Guerrilla** | Present a **prototype to random people in a public place** (e.g. a café) | **Less costly, simple, time-efficient** | Participants **may not be actual users** of your website's niche |
| **Phone interview** | A researcher **asks questions or instructs participants over the phone**; participants give feedback | Reach participants across **wider geographic areas** | Crucial to employ **well-trained moderators** |
| **Card sorting** | Present **virtual cards** for different types of content; participants **reorder and organise** them, then explain their reasoning | Shows **how users see the site structure** and how they will navigate; **helps organise the website** | *(beyond the slides)* Tests structure only, not the real interface |
| **Recording session** | **Screen recording** of users' actions on the website, using tools such as **Hotjar** that record **mouse movements and clicks** | *(beyond the slides)* Real behaviour at scale | *(beyond the slides)* Shows *what* users do, not *why* |

> [!warning]
> Pros and cons marked *(beyond the slides)* are not in the lecture. They come from the study guide or general reasoning. Prefer the lecture's own pros and cons in an exam.

## 3. Comparing the main pair (moderated vs unmoderated)
| Moderated (in-person / remote) | Unmoderated remote |
|---|---|
| A researcher guides and observes | No researcher present |
| Can ask "why?" | Can't follow up |
| Costly, small groups | Cheap, larger groups |
| Controlled tasks | Real-world setting |

## 4. Model answer pattern (2023/24 Q8(a), 5 marks)
1. **Usability** (about 1 mark): *how easy a website's interface is to use; the quality of a user's experience*, measured by learnability, efficiency, memorability, errors, satisfaction.
2. **Method 1** (about 2 marks): what happens + one advantage + one disadvantage. *E.g. in-person testing.*
3. **Method 2** (about 2 marks): the same. *E.g. unmoderated remote testing.*

## 5. OOHDM (Object-Oriented Hypermedia Design Method)
The deck **ends with only a title slide** for OOHDM, so it is **unlikely to be examined in depth**.

> [!warning] Not in the slides
> Background from the study guide: OOHDM is a model-based method for designing hypermedia/web applications in four activities: **conceptual design** (domain classes and relationships), **navigational design** (nodes, links, access structures such as indexes; NSUs fit here), **abstract interface design** (what interface objects users see) and **implementation**.
