# 05.04 · Know Your User and Scenarios

> [!info] [[05.00 HCI in Web Design|Lesson 5]] · Lecture ref L5.6 · Priority 🟢 Low

## 1. Know your user (user focus)

| Technique | Lecture description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Personas (personae)** | **A description of an example user** | "Nimali, 45, teacher, uses a phone, wants to book a doctor quickly." |
| **Cultural probes** | Gather **qualitative data** about people's interactions and experiences with a product or service, used when **direct observation is hard**. **Probe packs** are collections of tools and tasks given to participants. | Participants get a diary, a disposable camera and postcards to record a week of online shopping |
| **Ethnographic field studies** | Listed under cultural probes on the slide: **observing users in their real environment** | Watching shop staff use a billing system in the shop |

**Qualitative data** means descriptive information (feelings, stories, reasons) rather than numbers.

## 2. Scenarios
**"Scenarios are stories for design."** They:
1. **Force you to think about the design in detail** and **notice potential problems before they happen**;
2. **Help verify that the design would make sense to the user**;
3. Can be used to **communicate with others**, **validate other models** (navigation models, task models) and **express dynamics** (what happens over time).

> [!example] Scenario example (illustration)
> "Nimali opens the channelling site on her phone during a lunch break. She searches for a cardiologist at Kurunegala, sees two sessions on Friday, picks 4 pm, pays by card and gets an SMS with her number."
>
> Writing this reveals the design needs: mobile layout, location filter, fast payment, SMS confirmation.

## 3. Why it matters
You cannot design a usable site without knowing **who** uses it and **what** they are trying to do. Personas and scenarios turn vague ideas into concrete design requirements. They are part of **UX research** ([[05.01 UI vs UX]]).
