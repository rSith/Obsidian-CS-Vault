# 13.14 · IoT Security

> [!info] [[13.00 Web Security and Cyber Threats|Lesson 13]] · Lecture ref L13.14 · Priority 🟡 Watch (8 slides, not examined yet)

## 1. What is IoT security? (lecture)
- **IoT (Internet of Things)** = everyday physical devices connected to the Internet: cameras, smart locks, medical monitors, sensors, smart TVs.
- **IoT security** is a **cybersecurity strategy to protect IoT devices and the vulnerable networks they connect to** from cyber attacks.
- Why it's needed:
  - IoT devices **have no built-in security**;
  - they **transfer data over the Internet unencrypted** and **operate undetected** by standard cybersecurity systems;
  - IoT security is needed **to help prevent data breaches**.

## 2. Challenges (lecture)
| Challenge | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Weak authentication and authorisation** | Many devices use **default passwords**, so hackers easily get in; **rogue (undetected) IoT devices** on the network can steal data or launch attacks |
| **Lack of encryption** | Most IoT traffic is **unencrypted** (e.g. **medical imaging and patient monitoring, security cameras**), so confidential data is exposed to malware |
| **Vulnerabilities in firmware and software** | **Short development cycles and low prices** limit the budget for secure firmware and testing; vulnerable IoT web apps compromise networks |
| **Insecure communications** | IoT devices share the **same network** as other devices, so **an attack on one can spread**; no segmentation or oversight. *Lecture example: in **2022**, millions of **Bluetooth digital locks in smart cars** could be **remotely unlocked** by exploiting a Bluetooth vulnerability* |
| **Difficulty patching and updating** | Many devices **aren't designed to receive regular updates**; no built-in security makes secure upgrades and testing difficult |

## 3. How to address IoT security: Learn → Segment → Protect (lecture)
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **Learn** | With **complete network visibility**, **authenticate and classify** IoT devices, build a **risk profile** and assign them to **device groups** |
| **Segment** | Put devices into **policy-driven groups** based on their **risk profiles** |
| **Protect** | Use the groups and internal **segmentation** to **monitor, inspect and enforce policies** at points across the infrastructure |

## 4. Best practices (lecture)
- **Regular patches and updates**
- **Strong passwords** (change the defaults!)
- Focus on **Wi-Fi security**
- **Monitor network and device behaviour** to detect deviations
- Keep a **full inventory** of networked devices
- **Network segmentation**: put IoT devices on a **separate network** to stop malware spreading
- Apply **zero-trust network access** ([[13.12 Zero Trust Security]])
- Use **digital certificates** to authenticate IoT devices
- **Multiple layers of protection**, including **endpoint encryption**
- An extra layer of **cloud-based security**
- Monitor **GPS-connected devices** for **fake or jammed GPS signals**
- **Understand the protocols** the devices use
- Use **IoT detection services and tools** that discover devices, block malicious traffic and enable **virtual patching**
