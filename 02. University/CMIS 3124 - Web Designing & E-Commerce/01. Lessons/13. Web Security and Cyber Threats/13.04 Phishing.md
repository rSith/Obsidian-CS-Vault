# 13.04 · Phishing

> [!info] [[13.00 Web Security and Cyber Threats|Lesson 13]] · Lecture ref L13.4 · Priority 🟡 Medium. Used in older scenario questions: credit-card robberies, online-banking drawbacks, spoofing, the travel-agent card photocopy.

## 1. What is phishing? (lecture)
**Phishing**: attackers **pose as trustworthy entities** to **fool victims into disclosing confidential information** (e.g. **login passwords, bank account information**).
- **Mode:** **fraudulent emails, websites or text messages**.

The name comes from "fishing": the attacker throws out bait and waits for someone to bite.

## 2. Types (lecture)

| Type | How it works (lecture) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Email phishing** | Mass fraudulent emails | "Your account is locked, click here to verify" sent to thousands |
| **Spear phishing** | A **particular user** (organisation or individual) is targeted, with personalised messages | An email to a university accountant that mentions a real supplier |
| **Whaling** | Targets **the head of a company** (CEO, CFO) | A fake legal notice sent to a CEO |
| **Smishing** | **SMS texts** containing **links to phishing websites** | "Your parcel is held, pay Rs 150 here: bit.ly/…" |
| **Vishing** | The attacker **calls** the victim, using **caller-ID spoofing** | A "bank officer" call asking for your OTP |
| **Clone phishing** | The attacker **copies an email sent from a trusted source** and **alters it** by adding a link to a malicious or fake website | A real invoice email resent with a fake "updated invoice" link |

## 3. How to recognise and prevent phishing

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> The lecture lists types only. These defences come from the best-practice and Zero Trust parts of the lesson and from the study guide.

- **Check the sender and URL** (look-alike domains such as `paypa1.com`); look for HTTPS and the correct certificate.
- Be suspicious of **urgency**, threats, prizes, and requests for passwords or OTPs (banks never ask for your OTP).
- **Enable 2FA/MFA**, so a stolen password alone is not enough ([[10.07 Authentication in E-Commerce]]).
- AI-based **phishing detection** analyses email language and sender information ([[13.11 AI and ML in Web Security]]).
- **User awareness training.**

## 4. Scenario: two instances of credit-card robberies (2017/18 Q7(a))
1. **Phishing/smishing:** a fake bank email or SMS leads to a cloned website where the victim enters card details and the OTP.
2. **Data breach / spyware:** a merchant storing card data insecurely is hacked, or a **keylogger/data harvester** captures card details typed on an infected computer ([[13.06 Spyware]]).
*(Also: card skimming at ATMs/POS, stolen photocopies of cards, full card numbers printed on receipts.)*
