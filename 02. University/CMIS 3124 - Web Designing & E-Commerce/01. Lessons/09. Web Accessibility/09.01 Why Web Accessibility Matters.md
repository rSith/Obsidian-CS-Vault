# 09.01 · Why Web Accessibility Matters

> [!info] [[09.00 Web Accessibility|Lesson 9]] · Lecture ref L9.1 · Priority 🟢 Low (useful as an opening sentence in accessibility answers)

## 1. What is web accessibility?
**Web accessibility** is the practice of making websites **usable by people of all abilities and disabilities** ([[04.04 Functional and Usable Design]]): people with **visual, hearing, motor (physical) and cognitive** impairments must be able to perceive, understand, navigate and interact with the web.

## 2. Four reasons from the lecture's opening slides

| Evidence | Message |
|---|---|
| **A large share of the world's population experiences some form of disability** | The audience you exclude is very large. *(The exact percentage is in a slide image; the study guide gives about **1 in 6 people** (WHO).)* |
| **W3C:** *"Accessibility is essential for developers and organizations that want to create high-quality websites and web tools, and not exclude people from using their products and services."* | Accessibility = **quality** + **not excluding** customers |
| **Tim Berners-Lee** (W3C Director, inventor of the Web): *"The power of the Web is in its universality. Access by everyone regardless of disability is an essential aspect."* | The Web was designed to be **universal** |
| **UN CRPD:** *access to information and communications technologies, including the Web, is defined as a **basic human right*** (UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities) | Accessibility is a **right**, not a favour |

> [!warning]
> The disability percentage appears only in a slide image. "1 in 6 (WHO)" comes from the study guide and could not be checked against the slide text.

## 3. Who benefits? (examples)

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> Examples to make the idea concrete.

| Disability | Barrier | Accessible solution |
|---|---|---|
| Blind / low vision | Can't see images or small text | Alt text, screen-reader-friendly HTML, zoomable real text, contrast |
| Colour blind | Can't tell red from green | Don't use colour alone to convey meaning |
| Deaf / hard of hearing | Can't hear video | Captions and transcripts |
| Motor impairment | Can't use a mouse | Full keyboard access, large targets |
| Cognitive | Overwhelmed by clutter | Simple navigation, minimal content |

Accessible design also helps **everyone**: captions in a noisy bus, keyboard shortcuts for power users, clear layouts for older users. That is the idea of **inclusive design**.
