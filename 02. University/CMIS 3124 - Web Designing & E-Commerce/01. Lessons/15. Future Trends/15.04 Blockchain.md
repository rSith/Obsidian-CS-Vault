# 15.04 · Blockchain

> [!info] [[15.00 Future Trends|Lesson 15]] · Lecture ref L15.4 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "Describe how blockchain works and list two (02) benefits of it." (2023/24 Q3(c), 5 marks). Its public-key cryptography is also the nearest current topic to the old "secret key vs public/private key" question.

## 1. What is blockchain? (lecture)
- Operates as a **decentralised, distributed database**, with data **stored across multiple computers**, which makes it **resistant to tampering**.
- **Transactions are validated through a consensus mechanism**, ensuring agreement across the network.
  - Transactions are **grouped into blocks**;
  - the blocks are **linked together**, forming a **secure and transparent chain**;
  - this **guarantees data integrity** and a **tamper-proof record**.
- **Ideal for cryptocurrencies and supply-chain management.**

**Key terms:**
- **Ledger:** a record book of transactions.
- **Distributed:** every participant (node) holds a copy.
- **Node:** a computer participating in the network.
- **Consensus:** the way nodes agree which new blocks are valid.

## 2. Evolution (lecture)
- **2008: Bitcoin**, created by the anonymous **Satoshi Nakamoto**, a **decentralised digital currency** for **peer-to-peer transactions without a bank**. Blockchain served as its **public ledger**.
- **2015: smart contracts** supported, i.e. digital contracts **automatically executed when predetermined conditions are met**.
- Then spread to **real estate, finance, supply chain, healthcare, voting systems**; a key player in **DeFi and NFTs**.
- Today: improving **scalability, privacy**, and integration with **AI and IoT**.

## 3. How blockchain works (lecture: 4 steps) ⭐

### Step 1: Records transactions as blocks
- Each transaction is recorded as a **"block"** of data.
- Blocks capture the movement of **tangible** (a product) or **intangible** (intellectual property) assets.
- Data includes **who, what, when, where, the amount, and specific conditions** (e.g. **the temperature of a food shipment**).
- Each block has a **timestamp**, which ensures **chronological order** and prevents **retrospective alterations**.

### Step 2: Connects blocks together
- Each block is **linked to the previous block and the next**, creating a **secure chain**.
- The links are made with **cryptographic hashes**, **unique identifiers** for each block.
- **The hash of a block includes data from the previous block**, fixing the exact sequence and timing.
- So it is **nearly impossible to alter any block without changing all later blocks**.

$$
H_n = \text{hash}\big(\,\text{data}_n \;\|\; \text{timestamp}_n \;\|\; H_{n-1}\,\big)
$$

```text
┌─────────────┐    ┌─────────────┐    ┌─────────────┐
│ Block 1     │    │ Block 2     │    │ Block 3     │
│ data, time  │    │ data, time  │    │ data, time  │
│ prev: 0000  │◄───│ prev: H1    │◄───│ prev: H2    │
│ hash: H1    │    │ hash: H2    │    │ hash: H3    │
└─────────────┘    └─────────────┘    └─────────────┘
Change Block 1's data → H1 changes → Block 2's "prev" no longer matches → chain broken
```
> [!note]
> The formula and diagram illustrate the lecture's sentence "the hash of a block includes data from the previous block". They are not slide content.

### Step 3: Builds an irreversible blockchain
- Blocks are grouped in an **irreversible chain**; **each new block reinforces** the security and validation of the previous ones.
- **Nodes validate and maintain** the chain by confirming each transaction through **consensus algorithms**: **Proof of Work (PoW)** and **Proof of Stake (PoS)** are the most common.

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> **PoW:** nodes compete to solve a hard puzzle, and the winner adds the block (Bitcoin). **PoS:** validators are chosen according to the coins they "stake" (lock up) (Ethereum today).

### Step 4: Ensures trust and immutability
- With each new block the chain becomes **more secure**, so **past transactions are nearly impossible to change**.
- The result is a **trusted, transparent ledger** that **prevents fraud** and keeps records **accurate and unchangeable**.

## 4. Benefits (lecture)
| Benefit | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Greater trust** | A **secure, members-only network**; confidential records shared **only with authorised members**; **end-to-end visibility** |
| **Enhanced security** | **Agreement among members** is needed to validate data; validated transactions are **immutable and permanent**: **no transaction can be deleted, even by a system administrator** |
| **Better traceability** | An instant, **transparent audit trail of an asset's journey**; shares **provenance** data (verifying ethical practices); reveals supply-chain inefficiencies |
| **Increased efficiency** | A **shared ledger** removes time-consuming **record reconciliations**; smart contracts speed up processes |
| **Automated transactions** | **Smart contracts** automatically trigger the next step when **predefined conditions** are met, reducing manual work |

## 5. Key features (lecture)
| Feature | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Distributed ledger technology** | All participants access the **shared, immutable ledger**; transactions are recorded **only once**, with no duplicated effort |
| **Immutable records** | No one can change a recorded transaction. **Errors are fixed by adding a new reversing transaction**, and **both are visible** |
| **Smart contracts** | **Self-executing agreements** stored on the blockchain; terms written **in code** and executed **automatically** (e.g. **transferring corporate bonds, triggering travel-insurance payouts**); faster, fewer intermediaries, transparent |
| **Public-key cryptography** | Two keys: the **public key** = an **address for receiving** cryptocurrency or data; the **private key** = **confidential**, **grants control** over the assets, **authorises transactions** and **verifies ownership** |

### Public-key vs secret-key cryptography (for the old exam question)

> [!warning] Partly beyond the slides
> The lecture explains only public-key cryptography (above) and mentions AES ([[12.04 Tools for Ethical Web Development]]). The comparison below is standard knowledge.

| Secret-key (symmetric) | Public/private-key (asymmetric) |
|---|---|
| **One shared key** encrypts and decrypts (e.g. **AES**) | A **key pair**: the public key encrypts or verifies; the private key decrypts or signs |
| **Fast**, good for bulk data | **Slower** |
| Problem: **securely sharing the key**; many keys are needed for many pairs | **Solves key distribution**; enables **digital signatures, non-repudiation, certificates** |
| | |
| **Both:** provide confidentiality using mathematical algorithms and keys. **HTTPS uses both**: asymmetric to exchange a session key, then symmetric (AES) for the data. | |

## 6. Protocols and platforms (lecture)
- **Blockchain protocols:** the **set of rules** governing how data is **recorded, shared and secured**; the foundation of the network.
- **Blockchain platforms:** **extend protocols** to build practical solutions.

| Platform | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Hyperledger Fabric** | Open source, from the **Linux Foundation**; **modular** framework; the unofficial standard for **enterprise** platforms (e.g. **IBM Blockchain Platform**); **plug-and-play** components |
| **Ethereum** | **Decentralised, open-source**; build and deploy **smart contracts and dApps** |
| **Corda** | A distributed ledger for **businesses**; **secure, private transactions on permissioned networks**, shared **only with relevant parties**; finance, healthcare, supply chain |
| **Quorum** | **Open-source, permissioned**, **based on Ethereum**; enterprise; **transaction privacy, faster consensus**; ideal for **financial institutions** |

**Permissioned** = only approved participants can join (private). **Permissionless** = anyone can join (e.g. Bitcoin).

## 7. Blockchain and security (lecture)
Needs a **comprehensive security strategy** using **cybersecurity frameworks, assurance services and best practices**, including **identity and access management (IAM)** (only authorised users access critical components) and **strong encryption**.

## 8. Model answer for 2023/24 Q3(c) (5 marks)
A blockchain is a **decentralised, distributed ledger** shared across many nodes.
1. **Records transactions as blocks:** details (who, what, when, where, amount, conditions) plus a **timestamp**.
2. **Links blocks with cryptographic hashes:** each block's hash includes the previous block's data, so changing one block breaks all later ones.
3. **Builds an irreversible chain:** nodes validate new blocks by **consensus (PoW / PoS)**.
4. **Ensures trust and immutability:** past records can't be altered or deleted, giving a transparent, tamper-proof ledger.

**Two benefits:** e.g. **enhanced security** (immutable, consensus-validated; not even an admin can delete) and **better traceability** (an audit trail of an asset's journey). *(Or greater trust, increased efficiency, automated transactions through smart contracts.)*
