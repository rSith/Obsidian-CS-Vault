# 15.05 · AR and VR for the Web

> [!info] [[15.00 Future Trends|Lesson 15]] · Lecture ref L15.5 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "Explain how AR and VR transform web design." (2023/24 Q3(d), 5 marks)

## 1. Definitions (lecture)
*"Web design is being **revolutionised** by AR and VR technologies, which produce **more engaging and dynamic user experiences**."*

| | **Augmented Reality (AR)** | **Virtual Reality (VR)** |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | **Overlays digital content onto the real world** | **Creates fully immersive digital environments** |
| User sees | The real world **plus** digital objects | **Only** the digital world |
| Typical use | **Visualise products in your environment**; access information in context | **Virtual tours**, **interactive training** |
| Device | Phone camera, AR glasses | VR headset (or a 360° view in the browser) |

## 2. Examples in web design (lecture)
| Area | Example |
|---|---|
| **E-commerce** | **AR:** furniture retailers let customers **see how furniture fits in their homes** before buying. **VR:** **immersive virtual stores**: browse and **try on products** |
| **Real estate** | **Interactive 3D tours**: explore properties remotely |
| **Tourism** | VR **transports users** to destinations to explore **hotels, resorts, cultural sites** |
| **Education** | **Interactive learning environments**: explore **complex systems or historical events in 3D** |

## 3. How AR and VR are changing web design (lecture: 6 ways)
| Change | Lecture explanation |
|---|---|
| **Enhanced user engagement** | Immersive experiences **increase engagement and time spent on the website**, and create a **stronger emotional connection** |
| **Real-world integration** | AR lets users **see products in their own homes**, **try on clothes virtually**, or interact with **digital information overlaid on real locations** |
| **Immersive storytelling** | VR **transports users** to different environments: **virtual showrooms, events, interactive training simulations** |
| **Improved product development** | Better **collaboration**: teams **visualise and iterate on designs in a shared virtual space** |
| **Data collection and insights** | AR interactions give **data on user behaviour**, helping businesses understand preferences and **optimise** their websites |
| **Cost-effectiveness** | Showcase products and services virtually, **reducing the need for physical demonstrations or showrooms** |

> [!tip] Mnemonic
> **"E-R-I-P-D-C"**: **E**ngagement, **R**eal-world, **I**mmersive storytelling, **P**roduct development, **D**ata, **C**ost. Think: "**E**very **R**eal **I**dea **P**roduces **D**igital **C**ash."

## 4. Web technology note (beyond the slides)
Browsers support AR/VR through the **WebXR Device API**, and 3D scenes are built with libraries such as **Three.js** or **A-Frame**:
```html
<!-- A-Frame: a VR scene in plain HTML (illustration) -->
<script src="https://aframe.io/releases/1.5.0/aframe.min.js"></script>
<a-scene>
  <a-box position="0 1 -3" color="#4CC3D9"></a-box>
  <a-sky color="#ECECEC"></a-sky>
</a-scene>
```

## 5. Model answer for 2023/24 Q3(d) (5 marks)
- **Definitions:** AR overlays digital content on the real world; VR creates fully immersive digital environments. Together they make web experiences more engaging and dynamic.
- **Enhanced engagement:** immersive experiences increase time on site and emotional connection.
- **Real-world integration:** customers view products in their own homes or try clothes virtually (e.g. furniture retailers).
- **Immersive storytelling:** virtual showrooms, 3D property tours, tourism previews, interactive training and education.
- **Product development, data and cost:** shared virtual design spaces; interaction data on preferences; fewer physical showrooms.

One point per mark, each with an example.
