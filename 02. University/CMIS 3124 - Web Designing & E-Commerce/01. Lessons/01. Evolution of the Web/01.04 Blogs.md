# 01.04 · Blogs

> [!info] [[01.00 Evolution of the Web|Lesson 1]] · Lecture ref L1.5(a) · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** short note on Blogs (2019/20 Q6(b)(i)); compare a blog with Wikipedia (2018/19 Q5(a)(i)); which tool to use for a story or poem (2017/18 Q8(d)(ii), 2019/20 Q6(a)).

## 1. What is it?

**Blog** is short for **"web log"**. The lecture defines a blog as:

- *"A powerful **two-way web-based communication tool** that businesses and individuals can effectively utilise"*
- *"A website where people can enter their **thoughts, ideas, suggestions and comments**"*
- It *"can be **private** (internal to an organisation) or **public** (open to anyone)"*

## 2. Why is it used?

A blog gives **one author** (or a small team) a simple way to publish regularly and **get feedback from readers**. The feedback is what makes it "two-way": the author posts, and readers reply with comments.

- **Individuals** use it to share opinions, stories, poems or tutorials, and to improve their writing through reader comments.
- **Businesses** use it for news, marketing and customer engagement. An internal (private) blog can share updates with staff.

## 3. How does it work?

1. The author writes a **post** using a simple editor. A CMS such as WordPress or Blogger handles the HTML. See [[02.10 Content Management Systems]].
2. Posts appear in **reverse date order**, so the newest is on top.
3. Readers **comment** under the post, and the author can reply.
4. Readers can **subscribe** through [[01.07 RSS Feeds|RSS]] to be told about new posts.

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> The slides do not mention reverse-chronological posts, comment threads or RSS syndication. They are standard blog features and make good extra points in a short note.

## 4. Important components
- **Author/owner:** controls the content. Readers **cannot edit** posts.
- **Posts:** dated entries.
- **Comments:** the two-way part.
- **Visibility:** private (internal) or public.

## 5. Blog vs Wiki (asked in 2018/19 Q5(a)(i))

| | Blog | Wiki (e.g. Wikipedia) |
|---|---|---|
| Authors | One author or a small team | **Anyone**, collaboratively |
| What readers can do | **Comment** only | **Edit the article itself** |
| Style | Personal opinions, news, dated posts | Neutral, topic-based articles |
| Communication | Two-way (post ↔ comments) | Collaborative authoring |
| Access | Private or public | Usually public, with revision history |
| **Similarities** | Both are Web 2.0 user-generated content, read in a browser and free to use | |

## 6. When should you choose a blog? (scenario questions)
Choose a blog when the goal is **reader feedback through comments**:
- A fictional story such as "Ghost and me", to get more comments that improve your writing → **blog**.
- A poem ("Kavi") → **blog**.
- A culture article to share widely and discuss → **public blog** plus social sharing.

## 7. Exam-relevant points (short-note checklist)
- "Web log"; a site where people enter thoughts, ideas, suggestions, comments.
- **Two-way** web-based communication tool.
- **Private or public**.
- Used by individuals and businesses; newest posts first; can be syndicated by RSS.
