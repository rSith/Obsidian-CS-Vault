# 12.06 · Dark Patterns in UX

> [!info] [[12.00 Web Design Ethics and Policies|Lesson 12]] · Lecture ref L12.6 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "Briefly explain three (03) types of dark patterns." (2023/24 Q7(e), 6 marks). Also the core of "features not appropriate / with negative impact on e-commerce websites" (2019/20 Q5(a), 5(b)(ii); 2018/19 Q4(c); 2017/18 Q2(c)).

## 1. What are dark patterns? (lecture)
**Dark patterns** are **"tricks used in websites and apps that make users do things that they didn't mean to."**

Dark patterns can deceive users into:
- **paying more** for a service or product;
- **subscribing or opting in** to ongoing communications;
- **sharing their data** for company use;
- **making ill-informed decisions** based on fake or vague information;
- **agreeing to unsavoury terms and conditions**.

They are **unethical**: they break **transparency and fairness** ([[12.01 Ethical Web Development Principles]]) and often **consumer-protection and privacy laws** ([[10.10 GDPR]]).

## 2. The 12 types (lecture)

| # | Dark pattern | How it tricks you (lecture) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hidden costs** | **Extra fees** (service fees, shipping, VAT) tacked onto the order, so the **end price is significantly higher** than advertised | A Rs 2,000 ticket becomes Rs 2,950 at the last checkout step |
| 2 | **Bait and switch** | Users are "baited" to click a button they think does one thing, but **something unexpected happens** | *Lecture:* Microsoft Windows users clicked "close" on an upgrade pop-up, and **it started the update** |
| 3 | **Forced continuity** | Payment details are required for a **"free trial"**, then **billing starts when the trial ends, with no warning** | *Lecture:* Amazon "Subscribe & Save": the item is **re-ordered automatically** without asking |
| 4 | **Roach motel** | **Signing up is easy** (two steps), but **cancelling** involves **unnecessary extra steps** | Join online in seconds; cancel only by phone during office hours |
| 5 | **Misdirection** | **Distracts users** from the task by focusing their attention elsewhere | *Lecture:* an airline's bright banner says it **pre-selected seats for you, at extra cost**, so you don't notice the opt-out |
| 6 | **Confirm-shaming** | **Clever wording that shames users** for declining, to **guilt-trip** them | "No thanks, I don't like saving money" |
| 7 | **Sneak into basket** | **Undesired items are sneaked into the basket** during the purchase | Travel insurance auto-added to a flight booking |
| 8 | **Friend spam** | Asks for **social media login** "to connect with people you know", then **spams your contacts** with messages that **appear to come from you** | Invites sent to your whole contact list |
| 9 | **Disguised ads** | **Ads disguised as something else** (an article, news item, or real content) | *Lecture:* a fake **"Download" button** on a free-font website |
| 10 | **Trick questions** | **Subtle wording** tricks users into answering wrongly or agreeing to something unintended | "Untick this box if you prefer not to stop receiving emails" (double negative) |
| 11 | **FOMO** (fear of missing out) | Pressure to buy quickly with information about **how many people bought**, creating the impression of **high demand** | "27 people bought this in the last hour, only 2 left!" |
| 12 | **Price-comparison prevention** | **Hides or obscures pricing** so users can't pick the **cheaper alternative** | Different pack sizes with no unit price |

> [!note]
> Examples marked *Lecture* are from the slides. The others are illustrations from the study guide.

> [!tip] Memory groups
> - **Money tricks:** hidden costs, forced continuity, sneak into basket, price-comparison prevention
> - **Click tricks:** bait and switch, disguised ads, misdirection, trick questions
> - **Pressure tricks:** confirm-shaming, FOMO
> - **Trap tricks:** roach motel, friend spam

## 3. Example in code: a trick question vs an honest version
```html
<!-- ❌ Trick question + pre-ticked (also invalid GDPR consent) -->
<label><input type="checkbox" checked> Uncheck to not stop receiving offers</label>

<!-- ✅ Honest, clear, opt-in -->
<label><input type="checkbox"> Send me weekly offers by email</label>
```

## 4. Model answer pattern for 2023/24 Q7(e) (6 marks)
Define dark patterns in one line, then for **each of three** patterns (2 marks each): **name + how it works + example**.
- **Hidden costs:** fees such as service charges, shipping or VAT are added at the final checkout step, so the price is far higher than advertised.
- **Roach motel:** signing up takes two steps, but cancelling needs many unnecessary steps.
- **Confirm-shaming:** guilt-tripping opt-out text such as "No thanks, I prefer paying full price".

## 5. "Features not appropriate / with negative impact" on e-commerce sites
Combine dark patterns with design and usability failures:
| Category | Features to avoid | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Dark patterns** | Hidden costs, sneak into basket, forced continuity, roach motel, confirm-shaming, fake FOMO counters, disguised ads, trick questions | This note |
| **Poor aesthetics** | Clutter, too many colours/fonts, garish colours, flashing animations, auto-playing audio/video, text as images, ALL CAPS, underlined non-links, complementary text/background | [[04.02 Colour Psychology and Colour Schemes]], [[04.03 Graphics and Typography]] |
| **High cognitive load** | Long one-page forms, **forced registration before checkout**, too many pop-ups | [[05.09 Cognitive Load Theory]] |
| **Performance and responsiveness** | Slow loading, heavy images, broken links, not mobile-friendly | [[06.07 Web Performance Optimisation]] |
| **Accessibility failures** | No alt text, low contrast, keyboard traps | [[09.03 Applying Accessible Design]] |
| **Security and privacy failures** | No HTTPS, storing card data insecurely, collecting unnecessary data (e.g. asking for religion), no privacy policy | [[13.10 Best Practices for Securing a Website]], [[10.10 GDPR]] |
| **Legal** | Copyrighted images without permission; misleading product information | [[12.07 Web Compliance and Copyright]] |
