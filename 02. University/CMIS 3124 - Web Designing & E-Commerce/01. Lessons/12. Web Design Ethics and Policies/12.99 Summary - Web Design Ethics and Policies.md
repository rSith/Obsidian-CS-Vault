# 12.99 · Lesson Summary: Web Design Ethics and Policies

> [!abstract] Back to [[12.00 Web Design Ethics and Policies]] · Practice: [[PP 12 - Web Design Ethics and Policies]]

## Must know
- **Ethical web development** = designing, creating and maintaining sites that **prioritise users' rights, safety and well-being**, committed to **transparency, accountability and fairness**.
- **5 practices (PTASS):** respect for **privacy** · **transparency and consent** · **accessibility and inclusivity** · **sustainability** · **security**.
- **Impact on users:** trust and confidence (more engagement); better UX. **On businesses:** brand reputation; legal compliance and risk mitigation; long-term loyalty.
- **Tools:** privacy management platforms (**OneTrust, TrustArc**; GDPR/CCPA) · **HTTPS with TLS** · **AES** encryption (at rest and in transit) · **anonymisation/pseudonymisation**.
- **Challenges:** functionality vs privacy · changing regulations · resource constraints.
- **Dark patterns** = tricks that make users do things they didn't mean to (pay more, subscribe, share data, ill-informed decisions, bad terms). **12 types:** hidden costs, bait and switch, forced continuity, roach motel, misdirection, confirm-shaming, sneak into basket, friend spam, disguised ads, trick questions, FOMO, price-comparison prevention.
- **Web compliance:** legal (GDPR, CCPA) · data security · accessibility (WCAG, ADA) · industry standards (PCI, HIPAA). **Why:** legal protection, user protection, better UX, reputation.
- **Copyright:** no use without permission (credit ≠ permission); royalty-free or licensed content (check the terms); a UGC policy in the terms of service.

## Lecture examples of dark patterns
- Windows "close" started the upgrade (**bait and switch**) · Amazon Subscribe & Save (**forced continuity**) · airline pre-selected paid seats (**misdirection**) · fake "Download" on a font site (**disguised ads**).
