# 11.07 · Off-Page SEO: Links, PageRank, Social Media, Sitemaps, robots.txt

> [!info] [[11.00 Social Media and Web Influence|Lesson 11]] · Lecture ref L11.7 · Priority 🟡 Medium (used in "promote your website/blog" answers)

## 1. Links and PageRank (lecture)
- **Links remain the single largest factor in determining search ranking.**
- Google's **PageRank algorithm**:
  - **External links pointing to a web page can be interpreted as votes.**
  - **Initially, all votes are considered equal.**
  - **Over time, pages that receive more votes become more important.**
  - **More important pages cast more important votes.**
  - Each page **divides** its voting power among its outgoing links:

$$
\text{Votes per page} = \frac{\text{Page importance}}{\text{Number of links}}
$$

### Worked example (illustration)
Page A has importance 12 and links to 3 pages → each linked page receives $12 \div 3 = 4$ "votes" from A.
Page B has importance 12 but links to 12 pages → each receives only $12 \div 12 = 1$.
➡ **A link from an important page with few outgoing links is worth the most.**

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> The full PageRank formula (with a damping factor $d \approx 0.85$) is
> $$PR(A) = (1-d) + d\sum_{i}\frac{PR(T_i)}{C(T_i)}$$
> where $T_i$ are the pages linking to $A$ and $C(T_i)$ is the number of outgoing links on $T_i$. The lecture gives only the simplified "votes per page" idea.

**A backlink** is a link from another website to yours. Getting backlinks from reputable sites (guest posts, partnerships, directories) is the core of off-page SEO.

## 2. Social media
Shares and mentions on social media **drive traffic and visibility**, which indirectly helps ranking ([[11.02 Benefits of Social Media Integration]]).

## 3. XML / HTML sitemap (lecture)
- **"A list of pages of a website accessible to search engines and users."**
- **Improves how often and the manner in which search engines crawl** the site.
- Can also **help users navigate**.

```xml
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<urlset xmlns="http://www.sitemaps.org/schemas/sitemap/0.9">
  <url><loc>https://www.example.lk/</loc></url>
  <url><loc>https://www.example.lk/lamps/brass-oil-lamp</loc></url>
</urlset>
```

## 4. robots.txt (lecture)
A file **located at the root level of the domain** (e.g. `example.lk/robots.txt`) that:
- **prevents search engines from accessing non-public parts** of a website;
- **blocks search engines from pages of code**;
- **avoids indexing duplicate content**, such as **"print" or "mobile" versions** of pages;
- **auto-discovers sitemaps** (it can point to the sitemap).

```text
User-agent: *
Disallow: /admin/
Disallow: /print/
Sitemap: https://www.example.lk/sitemap.xml
```
- `User-agent: *` means the rules apply to all crawlers.
- `Disallow:` lists paths that must not be crawled.

> [!warning] Beyond the slides
> robots.txt is a **request**, not security. Bad bots can ignore it, so private areas must still be protected with authentication.

## On-page vs off-page SEO
| On-page | Off-page |
|---|---|
| On your own pages | Signals from elsewhere |
| Titles, meta descriptions, URLs, alt text, headings, keywords | **Backlinks (PageRank)**, social media, sitemaps, robots.txt |
| Fully under your control | Partly under your control |
