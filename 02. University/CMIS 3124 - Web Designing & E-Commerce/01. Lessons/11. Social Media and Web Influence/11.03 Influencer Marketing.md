# 11.03 · Influencer Marketing

> [!info] [[11.00 Social Media and Web Influence|Lesson 11]] · Lecture ref L11.3 · Priority 🔴 High (2023/24 Q5(e), 5 marks)

## 1. What is it? (lecture)
**Influencer marketing** is a **"strategic collaboration between brands and social media personalities who have established credibility and influence over their followers."**
Influencers **promote products, services and deals by creating relatable content**, often **sharing personal experiences and recommendations**.

## 2. Tiers of influencers by follower count (lecture)

| Tier | Followers | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Mega-influencers** | **Over 1 million** | **Celebrities**; **global reach**; used by **large brands** for major product launches or campaigns |
| **Macro-influencers** | **100,000 – 1 million** | Well-known personalities in **specific fields** (beauty, fitness, gaming); **broad yet still targeted** audiences |
| **Micro-influencers** | **10,000 – 100,000** | **More intimate relationships**, often in **niche markets**; seen as **more trustworthy and relatable**; **higher engagement and conversion rates** |
| **Nano-influencers** | **Fewer than 10,000** | **Extremely high engagement rates**; specialise in niches; seen as **the most relatable**, like **peers or friends** |

> [!tip] The trade-off
> As follower count goes **up**, **reach** increases, but **engagement and trust** usually go **down**. Small brands often get better value from **micro and nano** influencers.

## 3. Strategies (lecture)
| Strategy | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Sponsored posts** | Brands **pay influencers** to create engaging posts or videos |
| **Affiliate marketing** | Influencers share **unique discount codes or affiliate links**; they earn a commission per sale, and the brand can track sales per influencer |
| **Product reviews and unboxing videos** | Showing the product being opened and used builds confidence |
| **Live streams** | Real-time demonstrations and Q&A; live shopping |

## 4. Why are influencer-driven deals effective? (lecture)
1. **Influencers build psychological trust** with their followers (they seem like friends, not advertisers).
2. They create a sense of **urgency and exclusivity** with **limited-time promotions** and **"special access" deals** for their followers (fear of missing out).

## 5. Challenges (lecture)
| Challenge | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fake engagement and followers** | Bought followers and likes; the brand pays for an audience that doesn't exist |
| **Disclosure and transparency** | **Influencers must disclose when content is sponsored** (e.g. #ad); hiding it is unethical (see [[12.06 Dark Patterns]], disguised ads) |
| **Market saturation** | **Constant brand promotions may overwhelm followers**, reducing trust and effect |

## 6. Exam pattern for 2023/24 Q5(e)
Discuss **why** social media + influencers maximise presence and sales:
1. Social media features (visual, UGC, real-time, micro-targeting, social commerce).
2. Benefits for **presence** (awareness, community, traffic) and **sales** (targeting, social proof, analytics).
3. Influencers: **trust + urgency/exclusivity**; strategies; micro/nano engagement.
4. **Balance** with the challenges, then conclude.
