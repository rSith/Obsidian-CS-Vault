# 10.01 · What is E-Commerce? Definition and Evolution

> [!info] [[10.00 E-Commerce|Lesson 10]] · Lecture ref L10.1–L10.2 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "What is e-commerce?" (2023/24 Q5(a), 2 marks); "effects of e-commerce during a pandemic / how e-commerce helped people during corona" (2017/18 Q1(a), 2018/19 Q1(a), 2019/20 Q1(a)).

## 1. Definition (lecture)
> **E-commerce** is the *"use of electronic communications and digital information processing technology in business transactions to create, transform and redefine relationships for value creation between or among organisations, and between organisations and individuals."*
>
> *"Any transaction that is completed **solely through electronic measures** can be considered e-commerce."*

**In plain English:** e-commerce is doing business (buying, selling, paying, building customer relationships) **electronically**. Examples: ordering on Daraz or Amazon, paying a bill online, buying an app, subscribing to Netflix.

**Breaking the definition down:**
- *Electronic communications + digital information processing* → the Internet, websites, apps, payment systems.
- *Business transactions* → buying, selling, paying.
- *Create, transform, redefine relationships* → e-commerce changes how businesses and customers deal with each other.
- *Value creation* → both sides benefit.
- *Between organisations, and between organisations and individuals* → covers B2B and B2C ([[10.04 Types of E-Commerce and e-Government]]).

## 2. Evolution of e-commerce: timeline (lecture)

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1969** | **CompuServe** launched, *the first e-commerce company*: computer-sharing services to businesses, sending data over phone lines (**Electronic Data Interchange, EDI**) |
| **1979** | **Electronic shopping invented**: British inventor **Michael Aldrich** connected a modified TV to a transaction-processing computer via telephone line |
| **1982** | **Boston Computer Exchange (BCE)**: *the world's first e-commerce company*, letting people buy and sell **used computers** |
| **1984** | **CompuServe opens the Electronic Mall**: users buy from about **100 merchants** |
| **1994** | **First secure online transaction** using **encryption** (11 August 1994): **Phil Brandenberger** bought Sting's CD *Ten Summoners' Tales* through **NetMarket** |
| **1995** | **Amazon** (Jeff Bezos, initially books) and **AuctionWeb** → **eBay** (Pierre Omidyar, first online auction site): the **online marketplace boom** |
| **1998** | **PayPal** launched (originally **Confinity**, a money-transfer tool); merged in 2000 with Elon Musk's online banking company |
| **2000** | **Dot-com bust** (NASDAQ fell **75%** from March 2000 to October 2002; many companies went bankrupt). **Google AdWords** introduced, and **pay-per-click (PPC)** advertising took off |
| **2005** | **Comeback**: **Cyber Monday** (the Monday after Black Friday) introduced; **Amazon Prime** (free 2-day shipping in the US) made fast shipping a customer expectation |
| **2006** | **Shopify** founded (Tobias Lütke, Daniel Weinand, Scott Lake): easy online stores for merchants |
| **2012** | **Instacart**: food shopping goes virtual (shoppers pick groceries ordered online) |
| **2017** | **"Unstoppable"**: brick-and-mortar declines; global e-commerce **$29.267 trillion** (**$25.516T B2B**, **$3.851T B2C**) |
| **2020** | **COVID-19 boost**: stores closed and people were quarantined, so they bought essentials online. **IBM's US Retail Index:** the pandemic **accelerated the shift to digital shopping by roughly five years** |

> [!tip] Memory chain
> **EDI (CompuServe 1969) → TV shopping (1979) → BCE (1982) → e-Mall (1984) → secure purchase (1994) → Amazon/eBay (1995) → PayPal (1998) → bust + AdWords (2000) → Cyber Monday/Prime (2005) → Shopify (2006) → Instacart (2012) → $29T (2017) → COVID (2020)**

The 2017 figures also show that **B2B is far larger than B2C** by value.

## 3. E-commerce and the pandemic (scenario questions)
The lecture's evidence is the **2020 COVID-19 boost** (the five-year acceleration). Combine it with the benefits and drawbacks in [[10.02 Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce]].

**How e-commerce helped people:**
- **Safety:** contactless shopping and home delivery, fewer crowds; people in quarantine still got food, medicine and essentials.
- **Convenience and 24×7 access**; businesses kept trading and stayed afloat.
- Growth of online grocery/delivery services (Instacart-style), **online payments**, e-government services, online learning and work-from-home tools.

**Threats and drawbacks during the pandemic:**
- **No physical inspection** (wrong size or quality; returns harder).
- **Cyber fraud**: phishing sites, fake sellers, card theft, scams exploiting fear ([[13.04 Phishing]]).
- Delivery delays, stock-outs, price increases; possible virus spread through packages or delivery staff.
- **Digital divide**: elderly and rural people without Internet or cards are excluded; not suitable for perishables.
