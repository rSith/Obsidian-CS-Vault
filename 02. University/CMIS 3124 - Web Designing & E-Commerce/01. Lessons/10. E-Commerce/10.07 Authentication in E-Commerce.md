# 10.07 · Authentication in E-Commerce

> [!info] [[10.00 E-Commerce|Lesson 10]] · Lecture ref L10.10 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "Why is authentication crucial in e-commerce? List three (03) common authentication methods." (2023/24 Q5(b), 4 marks); **Gmail 2-step verification, pros and cons** (2018/19 Q2(e); 2019/20 Q2(d), **10 marks**).

## 1. What is authentication? (lecture)
**Authentication** is **"the process of verifying the identity of users before granting them access to an online store's features or sensitive information like payment details."**

In other words: *proving you are who you claim to be.*

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> **Authentication** (who are you?) is different from **authorisation** (what are you allowed to do?).

## 2. Why is it crucial? (lecture)
| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Security** | Protects against **unauthorised access** to customer data, financial information and business systems |
| **Fraud prevention** | Strong authentication mitigates **phishing, credential stuffing and payment fraud** |
| **Customer trust** | Customers shop more when they feel their accounts are safe |
| **Non-repudiation** | Ensures **users cannot deny their actions or transactions** |

Key terms:
- **Credential stuffing:** attackers try username/password pairs stolen from *another* site, because people reuse passwords.
- **Non-repudiation:** proof that someone did something, so they can't later deny it (e.g. "I never placed that order").

## 3. Authentication factors (the idea behind MFA)
| Factor | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Something you know** | A secret | Password, PIN |
| **Something you have** | A physical object | Phone (OTP/prompt), bank token, card |
| **Something you are** | A body feature | Fingerprint, face, iris |

## 4. Common authentication methods (lecture)
| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Username and password** | The basic method (something you know). Weakest on its own. |
| **Two-factor authentication (2FA)** | **Adds an extra layer of security by requiring a second form of verification** (e.g. a **code sent to a phone**) |
| **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** | **A more robust approach that combines multiple factors**: *something you know, something you have, something you are* |
| **Biometrics** | Fingerprint, face recognition |
| **One-time passwords (OTPs)** | A code valid for **one login or one transaction** (SMS, email, authenticator app) |
| **Digital certificates** | **Verify the identity of both the user and the e-commerce site** (e.g. SSL/TLS certificates) |
| **Keystroke dynamics** | **Analysing typing patterns** to identify users |

### 2FA vs MFA
| 2FA | MFA |
|---|---|
| **Exactly two** factors | **Two or more** factors |
| e.g. password + SMS code | e.g. password + phone + fingerprint |
| 2FA is a type of MFA | The broader category |

## 5. Examples of authentication in e-commerce transactions (lecture)
- **Logging into an account:** verifying identity before access to the **profile or order history**.
- **Making a purchase:** confirming identity **before processing payment** (e.g. a bank OTP for a card payment).
- **Accessing payment details:** only authorised users can **view sensitive financial information**.

## 6. Scenario: Gmail 2-step verification (10 marks in 2019/20)
*"When you log in to Gmail, in addition to the username and password, you must confirm your identity via the phone."* This is **2FA/MFA**: something you **know** (password) + something you **have** (phone).

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| A **stolen or guessed password alone is not enough**, which protects against **phishing, brute force and credential stuffing** | **Depends on the phone**: lost, stolen, dead battery, or **no signal/roaming abroad** locks you out |
| **Alerts you** to unauthorised login attempts | **SIM-swap** and **SMS interception** attacks can bypass SMS codes |
| Protects **linked services** (Drive, YouTube, payments) | **Extra time and inconvenience** at every new login |
| Supports the **Zero Trust identity pillar** ([[13.12 Zero Trust Security]]) and is a listed **security best practice** ([[13.10 Best Practices for Securing a Website]]) | Setup confusion for less technical users; **backup codes** must be stored safely |

**Conclusion:** the benefits outweigh the costs. Using an authenticator app and backup codes reduces the cons.
