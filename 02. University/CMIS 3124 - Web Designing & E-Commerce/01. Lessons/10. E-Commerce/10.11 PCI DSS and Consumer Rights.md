## Part A: PCI Security Standards

**PCI** = **Payment Card Industry**. The card brands (Visa, Mastercard…) created these standards so that **card data is handled safely**.

### 1. The four PCI standards (lecture)
| Standard | Applies to |
|---|---|
| **PCI DSS**: Data Security Standard | **All entities that store, process and/or transmit cardholder data**; covers the technical and operational system components connected to cardholder data |
| **PTS**: PIN Transaction Security | **Devices** that protect cardholder **PINs** and other payment processing; includes **PIN Security**, **Point of Interaction (POI)** and **Hardware Security Module (HSM)** requirements |
| **PA-DSS**: Payment Application DSS | **Software vendors** who develop **payment applications** that store, process or transmit card data, when the applications are **sold, distributed or licensed** to third parties |
| **P2PE**: Point-to-Point Encryption | **P2PE solution providers** validating their encryption solutions |

### 2. PCI DSS: 6 goals and 12 requirements (lecture)
*"A global data security standard adopted by the payment card brands for all entities that process, store or transmit cardholder data and/or sensitive authentication data."*

| Goal | Requirements |
|---|---|
| **Build and maintain a secure network and systems** | **1.** Install and maintain a **firewall** configuration to protect cardholder data · **2.** **Do not use vendor-supplied defaults** for system passwords and other security parameters |
| **Protect cardholder data** | **3.** **Protect stored cardholder data** · **4.** **Encrypt transmission** of cardholder data across open, public networks |
| **Maintain a vulnerability management program** | **5.** Protect all systems against **malware** and regularly update **antivirus** · **6.** Develop and maintain **secure systems and applications** |
| **Implement strong access control measures** | **7.** **Restrict access** to cardholder data by business **need to know** · **8.** **Identify and authenticate** access to system components · **9.** **Restrict physical access** to cardholder data |
| **Regularly monitor and test networks** | **10.** **Track and monitor all access** to network resources and cardholder data · **11.** **Regularly test** security systems and processes |
| **Maintain an information security policy** | **12.** Maintain a **policy** that addresses information security **for all personnel** |

> [!tip] Memory aid: 6 goals, 2-2-2-3-2-1 requirements
> **N**etwork · **D**ata · **V**ulnerability · **A**ccess · **M**onitor · **P**olicy → "**N**ever **D**isclose **V**ital **A**ccount **M**aterial **P**ublicly"

### 3. Applying PCI DSS to card-safety scenarios
| Scenario | Problem | Relevant requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Saving card details** on a website for future payments | If the merchant is breached, stored card numbers are stolen; anyone using your account or device can buy | **Req. 3** (protect stored data), 7–8 (access) |
| **Photocopy of your credit card** sent to a travel agent | Exposes number, name, expiry (and CVV if the back is copied) → **card-not-present fraud**; no audit trail | Req. 3, 4, 7 (need-to-know) |
| **Full card number printed on a bill** | Anyone who sees the bill can misuse it; the merchant should show only the **last 4 digits** | Req. 3 (and a merchant compliance failure) |

> [!note]
> "Receipts should show only the last 4 digits" and "card-not-present fraud" are standard PCI and payment knowledge, not slide content.

## Part B: Consumer rights

### 1. Definition (lecture)
*"A set of principles and legal protections designed to ensure **fair and ethical treatment of consumers** in the marketplace."* (Source: **Consumer Affairs Authority, Sri Lanka**.)

### 2. The eight consumer rights (lecture)
| # | Right | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Satisfaction of basic needs** | Access to essential goods and services: food, clothing, shelter, health care, education, public utilities, water and sanitation |
| 2 | **Safety** | Protection against products, processes and services **hazardous to health or life** |
| 3 | **To be informed** | Information to make an **informed choice**; protection against **dishonest or misleading advertising and labelling** |
| 4 | **To choose** | Select from a **range of products at competitive prices** with satisfactory quality |
| 5 | **To be heard** | Consumer interests **represented** in government policy and in product development |
| 6 | **To redress** | A **fair settlement of just claims**, including **compensation** for misrepresentation, substandard goods or unsatisfactory services |
| 7 | **Consumer education** | Knowledge and skills to make **informed, confident choices** and to know your rights and responsibilities |
| 8 | **Healthy environment** | Live and work in an environment that does not threaten present and future generations |

### 3. Applying consumer rights to scenarios
| Scenario | Rights used | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Charged twice by debit card** at a supermarket | **Redress**, **to be heard** | Collect evidence (bill, statement/SMS) → contact the supermarket and the bank → formal dispute or chargeback → if not refunded in a week: bank complaints unit → Central Bank / Financial Ombudsman → **Consumer Affairs Authority** |
| **Delivery person asks you to meet part-way** although you paid for delivery | **Redress** (unsatisfactory service), fairness | Politely refuse or ask for the agreed delivery; record details; complain; request a refund of the delivery fee; review; escalate |
| **Electronics store with a return facility** | **Redress** builds trust | Pros: trust and loyalty; cons: logistics, cost and health risk of returns during the pandemic |
| **Misleading product photos** | **To be informed** | Request a return or refund |

> [!warning]
> Named escalation bodies such as the Central Bank / Financial Ombudsman are not in the slides. They come from the study guide. The slides cite only the Consumer Affairs Authority.
