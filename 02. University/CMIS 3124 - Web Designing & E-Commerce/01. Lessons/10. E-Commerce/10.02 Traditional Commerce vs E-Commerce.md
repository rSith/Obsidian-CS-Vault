# 10.02 · Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce (Benefits and Drawbacks)

> [!info] [[10.00 E-Commerce|Lesson 10]] · Lecture ref L10.3 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "Concisely explain the benefits of e-commerce to organizations and consumers separately" (2023/24 Q5(d), 4 marks). It is also the reasoning base for most older **scenario** questions (pandemic, foreign purchases, National Express tickets, the electronics shop).

## 1. The lecture comparison

| Traditional commerce | E-commerce |
|---|---|
| **Less cost-effective** | **Cost-effective**: costs of labour, maintenance and office rent can be substituted |
| Takes a **lot of time** to complete a transaction | **Time saving** |
| **Less convenient** | **Convenient**: the website can be accessed **from anywhere, any time** |
| Needs **a lot of investment to expand** | **Easy to expand the size of the market** |
| Needs a lot of time and money to **introduce a new product** and analyse customer response | **Easy to introduce a product** and get **immediate customer feedback** |
| **Less profit** (middlemen, overheads, inventory, limited sales) | **Increases sales** |
| **Allows inspection of goods** before purchase | **No physical inspection**: customers rely on **electronic images** |
| **Open only for a limited time** | **24 × 7** service |
| **Suitable for perishable goods** | **Not suitable for perishable goods and high-value items** |
| Fewer problems recruiting and retaining staff | **Requires technically qualified staff** |
| **Face-to-face** interaction | **Screen-to-face** interaction |
| **Manual** processing → human errors | **Automated** processing |
| Business relationship is **vertical/linear** | Business relationship is **end-to-end** |
| **Fraud comparatively less** | **Lots of cyber frauds can occur** |

## 2. Benefits of e-commerce, split by who benefits (2023/24 Q5(d))

| To organisations | To consumers |
|---|---|
| **Lower costs** (less rent, labour, middlemen, inventory) → cost-effective, more profit | **Convenience**: shop **anywhere, any time (24×7)**; saves time and travel |
| **Global market reach** and easy expansion | **Wider choice** and easy **price comparison** |
| **24×7 sales** | **Reviews and ratings** to guide decisions |
| **Automated processing** (fewer errors) | **Personalised recommendations** ([[10.08 Recommendation Engines]]) |
| Easy **product launch** with **immediate feedback**, plus customer data and analytics | **Home delivery**; often **lower prices** |

> [!note]
> The lecture gives a two-column comparison, not a split by stakeholder. The split above comes from the study guide and is built from the lecture's points (the question is marked "partial" in the mapping).

## 3. Drawbacks of e-commerce
- **No physical inspection** of goods.
- **Not suitable for perishable or very high-value items.**
- **Needs technically qualified staff.**
- **Screen-to-face**, so it is less personal.
- **Cyber fraud** risk ([[13.00 Web Security and Cyber Threats]]).

## 4. Applying the comparison to scenarios
| Scenario (past paper) | Lecture points to use |
|---|---|
| Pandemic shopping | 24×7, convenience, contactless vs no inspection, fraud |
| Buying from foreign countries during Sri Lanka's economic crisis | Market reach, price comparison vs LKR depreciation, import limits, card limits, fraud, weak consumer protection abroad |
| National Express £2 online vs £20 at the station | **Cost-effective** (fewer counter staff) → savings passed on; advance booking helps demand forecasting; but the digital divide and last-minute travellers pay more |
| Electronics shop with 4 computers + paper slips + tokens | A mix of traditional and e-commerce: accurate stock info and physical inspection vs **manual processing errors**, queues, limited hours, no delivery or online payment |
| Pay on delivery vs online payment | Trust and no card exposure vs cash handling, seller risk, slower process |
