# 10.08 · Recommendation Engines

> [!info] [[10.00 E-Commerce|Lesson 10]] · Lecture ref L10.11 · Priority 🟡 Watch (taught, not examined; also relevant to AI personalisation in [[14.02 AI for User Experience and Design]])

## 1. What is a recommendation engine?
A **recommendation engine (recommender system)** is software that **suggests products or content a user is likely to want**, based on data about users and items. Examples: "Customers who bought this also bought…", Netflix's "Because you watched…".

**Why e-commerce uses it:** more sales (cross-selling, upselling), better user experience, customer loyalty.

## 2. The five types (lecture)

### (1) Collaborative filtering
- **Recommends items based on how other users with similar preferences and behaviour have interacted with them.**
- **Relies on the community's combined wisdom.**
- **Two forms:**
  - **User-based:** suggests products based on the **preferences or behaviour of similar users**;
  - **Item-based:** recommends products **similar to those the user has interacted with** previously (similarity worked out from how users rate or buy items together).
- **Example (lecture):** **Netflix** recommends movies based on **your viewing history and the viewing habits of similar users**.

### (2) Content-based filtering
- **Suggests products according to the similarity of the items** themselves (their attributes).
- **Recommends things most similar to what the user has already purchased.**
- *Example (illustration):* after buying a Canon camera, you are shown Canon lenses.

### (3) Demographics-based
- Considers **age, gender, location and interests**.
- **Recommends items popular with people who have similar demographics.**

### (4) Community-based
- **Relies on the collective feedback of a community of users.**
- First **collects ratings and reviews from a group**, then uses this data to make recommendations **for individual ("solitary") users**.
- Based on the **collective preferences of the community**, not any one user's individual preferences.

### (5) Hybrid recommender systems
- A **combination of collaborative and content-based filtering**.
- *E.g.* use collaborative filtering to **identify users with similar tastes**, then content-based filtering to give **personalised recommendations based on item attributes**.

## 3. Comparison
| Type | Data used | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Collaborative (user-based) | Behaviour of **similar users** | "Users like you watched…" |
| Collaborative (item-based) | Items **co-liked** with what you used | "Customers also bought…" |
| Content-based | **Attributes** of items you liked | Same brand or genre |
| Demographic | Your **age, gender, location** | Popular among 20–25-year-olds in Colombo |
| Community-based | **Ratings and reviews** of the community | Top-rated products |
| Hybrid | A mix | Netflix and Amazon in practice |

### Collaborative vs content-based (likely question)

> [!note] Beyond the slides
> The strengths and weaknesses below are standard knowledge (study guide), not lecture content.

| Collaborative | Content-based |
|---|---|
| Uses **other users' behaviour** | Uses **item attributes** |
| Can suggest surprising new items | Suggestions stay close to past choices (over-specialisation) |
| Needs lots of user data (**cold-start problem** for new users/items) | Works for new items if they have descriptions |
