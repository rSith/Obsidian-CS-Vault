# 10.03 · E-Business and the Architectural Framework for E-Commerce

## Part A: E-business

### 1. What is it? (lecture)
**Electronic business (e-business)**:
- **goes beyond the simple buying and selling** of products and services online;
- includes a **much wider range of business processes**: **supply chain management, electronic order processing and customer relationship management (CRM)**;
- uses IT to **enhance communications and transactions with all of an organisation's stakeholders**;
- can help companies **operate more effectively and efficiently**;
- **a money transaction is not necessary**.

### 2. Components of e-business (lecture diagram)
**E-commerce** · **Customer relationship management (CRM)** · **Supply chain management (SCM)** · **Enterprise resource management (ERP)** · **Business intelligence** · **Electronic transfer within the firm** · **Collaboration** · **Online activities between businesses**

### 3. E-commerce vs e-business
| E-commerce | E-business |
|---|---|
| **Buying and selling** online | **All** electronic business processes |
| Involves a **money transaction** | **Money transaction not necessary** |
| Customer-facing (sales) | Internal + external (SCM, CRM, ERP, HR, collaboration) |
| **A part of** e-business | The **wider** concept |

*Example:* placing an order on a website = e-commerce. The company's system automatically updating stock, notifying the warehouse and logging the customer in its CRM = e-business.

## Part B: Architectural framework for e-commerce (six layers, lecture)

```text
1. Application services                      ← what e-commerce app (B2C shop, B2B portal…)
2. Information brokerage & management        ← intermediary, data management, transactions
3. Interface & support services              ← catalogues, directories
4. Secure messaging                          ← encryption, authentication
5. Middleware services                       ← makes different software talk
6. Network infrastructure                    ← packet-switched network links
```

| # | Layer | Lecture description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Application services** | **Decides what type of e-commerce application** is going to be implemented |
| 2 | **Information brokerage and management** | Works as an **intermediary** providing **service integration between customers and information providers**, given constraints such as low price, fast service or profit maximisation; supports **data management and traditional transaction services** |
| 3 | **Interface and support services** | Provides the **interface** for e-commerce applications, e.g. **interactive catalogues and directory support services** |
| 4 | **Secure messaging** | Electronic messaging gives access to **the right information at the right time** across diverse work groups; the main constraints are **security, privacy and confidentiality**, achieved through **data encryption and authentication** |
| 5 | **Middleware services** | **Integrate diversified software programs** and **make them talk to one another** |
| 6 | **Network infrastructure** | Provides **effective and efficient linkage**, e.g. **packet switching** |

> [!tip] Mnemonic (top → bottom)
> **"All Information Is Securely Moved over Networks"**: Application, Information brokerage, Interface, Secure messaging, Middleware, Network.
