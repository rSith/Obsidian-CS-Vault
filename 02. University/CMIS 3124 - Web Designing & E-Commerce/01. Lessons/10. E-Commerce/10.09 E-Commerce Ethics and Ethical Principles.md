# 10.09 · E-Commerce Ethics and Ethical Principles

> [!info] [[10.00 E-Commerce|Lesson 10]] · Lecture ref L10.12 · Priority 🟡 Medium. Supports scenario answers (religious-artefact store, eBay listings, delivery-person request) and pairs with [[12.00 Web Design Ethics and Policies]].

## 1. Why are e-commerce ethics and policies important? (lecture)
1. **Build trust** between businesses and consumers → **customer loyalty and positive word-of-mouth**.
2. Help businesses **comply with laws and regulations** on **consumer protection and data privacy**.
3. **Positive brand image.**
4. **Long-term sustainability.**

## 2. Key areas (lecture)
| Area | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Transparency** | Be **open and honest** about practices: **data collection, product information, sourcing**. Builds trust and helps informed decisions. |
| **Fairness** | Treat all stakeholders fairly: **fair pricing, no hidden charges, ethical labour practices** in the supply chain |
| **Data privacy** | Respecting privacy is **paramount**: be transparent about how data is collected, used and protected; get **explicit consent** where required ([[10.10 GDPR]]) |
| **Consumer rights** | **Accurate information, fair returns and refunds, protection against fraud** ([[10.11 PCI DSS and Consumer Rights]]) |
| **Sustainability** | Consider **environmental impact**: packaging, shipping, product disposal |
| **Intellectual property** | **Avoid infringing trademarks, copyrights and patents** ([[12.07 Web Compliance and Copyright]]) |
| **Ethical marketing** | Advertising should be **truthful**, avoiding misleading or **manipulative** tactics (see [[12.06 Dark Patterns]]) |

## 3. Ethical principles for decision-making (lecture)
| Principle | Rule (lecture) | Apply it like this |
|---|---|---|
| **The Golden Rule** | **Put yourself in the place of others**; think of yourself as the object of the decision | "Would I want a hidden fee added at checkout?" |
| **Universalism** | **If an action is not right for all situations, it is not right for any specific situation** | "Would it be OK if *every* shop sold customer emails?" |
| **Slippery Slope** | **If an action cannot be taken repeatedly, it is not right to take at all** | A "small" one-time data misuse leads to more |
| **Collective Utilitarian** | **Take the action that achieves the greater value for all of society** | Choose the option that benefits most people |
| **Risk Aversion** | **Take the action that produces the least harm, or the least potential cost** | When unsure, choose the safest option |
| **Perfect Information Rule** | **Assume the results of your decision will be the lead article in the national daily newspaper tomorrow** | "Would I be embarrassed if this were front-page news?" |
| **Social Contract Rule** | **Would you like to live in a society where the principle you support became an organising principle of the entire society?** | If everyone did this, would society be good? |

> [!tip] Mnemonic
> **"Good Users Shop Carefully, Reducing Public Scandal"**: Golden, Universalism, Slippery slope, Collective utilitarian, Risk aversion, Perfect information, Social contract.

## 4. Applying ethics to scenario questions
| Scenario | Ethical areas to cite |
|---|---|
| **Religious-artefact store (details to include)** | Transparency (material, origin, authenticity, meaning), fairness (clear total prices), consumer rights (returns), privacy (policy, consent), IP (own photos), respectful presentation of all religions |
| **Selling used laptops/cameras on eBay** | Transparency: honest condition, faults, real photos; fair pricing; returns |
| **Delivery person asks you to meet part-way** | Fairness (you paid for delivery), the consumer right to **redress** |
| **Electronics store with a return facility during the pandemic** | Consumer rights (redress) vs the cost and health risk of returns |
