# 10.04 · Types of E-Commerce and e-Government

> [!info] [[10.00 E-Commerce|Lesson 10]] · Lecture ref L10.6–L10.7 · Priority 🔴 High
> **Asked as:** "Identify the type of e-commerce/e-Government involved in the following scenarios" (2023/24 Q5(c), 5 × 1 mark). Also: selling used laptops on eBay (C2C) in 2017/18 Q2(d) and 2018/19 Q4(d).

## Part A: Types of e-commerce (lecture diagram)

| Type | Who sells to whom | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **B2B**: Business-to-Business | A **company** trades with **another company** (the **largest by value**) | A manufacturer buys raw materials from a supplier's online portal; Alibaba |
| **B2C**: Business-to-Consumer | A **company** sells to **individual customers** | Amazon, Daraz, a **Netflix** subscription |
| **C2C**: Consumer-to-Consumer | **Individuals** sell to **individuals**, usually through a third-party site | **ikman.lk**, eBay auctions |
| **C2B**: Consumer-to-Business | **Individuals** sell products/services **to companies** | A freelance designer sells a logo to a company (Fiverr/Upwork); an influencer paid by a brand; stock photos |

> [!note]
> The examples in the right-hand column (Alibaba, Daraz, Fiverr…) come from the study guide and the exam paper. The lecture slide shows only icons for the four types.

### B2B vs B2C vs C2C (lecture table, Cengage)
| Factor | B2B | B2C | C2C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value of sale** | Thousands or millions of dollars | Tens or hundreds of dollars | Tens of dollars |
| **Length of sales process** | Days to months | Days to weeks | Hours to days |
| **Number of decision makers** | Several people to a dozen or more | One or two | One or two |
| **Uniformity of offer** | Typically a uniform product offering | More customised product offering | Single product, one of a kind |
| **Complexity of buying process** | Extremely complex; much negotiation on price, payment, delivery, quantity, quality, options, features | Relatively simple; limited discussion over price, payment and delivery | Relatively simple; limited discussion over payment and delivery; negotiation over price |
| **Motivation for sale** | Driven by a business decision or need | Driven by an individual consumer's need or emotion | Driven by an individual consumer's need or emotion |

## Part B: e-Government

### Definition (lecture)
**e-Government** is the *"use of information and communications technology to simplify the sharing of information, speed formerly paper-based processes, and improve the relationship between citizens and government."*

| Type | Between | Examples (lecture) |
|---|---|---|
| **G2C**: Government-to-Citizen | Government and **individuals** | **Submit tax returns online**, renew licences, apply for student loans, make campaign contributions |
| **G2B**: Government-to-Business | Government and **businesses** (an *insignificant segment by volume, but growing*) | **Procurement, filing taxes, licensing procedures, business registrations** |
| **G2G**: Government-to-Government | **Various levels of government** (improves communication between them) | Sri Lanka: **LGN** (Lanka Government Network), **eSLIMS** (State Land Information Management System), **NSDI** (National Spatial Data Infrastructure), **eBMD** (Birth, Marriage and Death certificates), **e-GP** (e-Government Procurement) |

## Part C: How to classify a scenario

**Ask two questions:** *Who is the **seller/provider**? Who is the **buyer/receiver**?*
- Business → business = **B2B**; business → person = **B2C**; person → person = **C2C**; person → business = **C2B**.
- If **government** is involved: with a citizen = **G2C**; with a business = **G2B**; with another government agency = **G2G**.

### ⭐ 2023/24 Q5(c) answers
| Scenario | Type | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) A person selling his used car to another person via **ikman.lk** | **C2C** | Consumer sells to consumer through a classifieds platform |
| (ii) An individual submitting **income tax returns online** | **G2C** | Government ↔ citizen (e-government) |
| (iii) A graphic designer selling a **logo to a company** on a freelance marketplace | **C2B** | An individual sells a service to a business |
| (iv) A manufacturing firm buying **raw materials from a supplier** via an online portal | **B2B** | Business buys from business |
| (v) A user subscribing to **Netflix** | **B2C** | A company sells a subscription to a consumer |

### Extra practice (not from the papers)
- A company registering its business online with the Registrar → **G2B**
- The Department of Motor Traffic sharing data with the Police → **G2G**
- Daraz selling headphones to you → **B2C**
