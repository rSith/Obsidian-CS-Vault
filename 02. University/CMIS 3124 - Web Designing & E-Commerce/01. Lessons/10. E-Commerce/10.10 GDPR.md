# 10.10 · GDPR: General Data Protection Regulation

> [!info] [[10.00 E-Commerce|Lesson 10]] · Lecture ref L10.13 · Priority 🟡 Watch. 9 slides, never examined; it could become a new question ("explain the GDPR principles" or "data-subject rights").

## 1. What is GDPR? (lecture)
- The **toughest privacy and security law in the world**.
- **Drafted and passed by the European Union (EU).**
- Imposes obligations on **organisations anywhere** if they **process the personal data of EU citizens or residents**, or **offer goods or services to such people**. This is why even a Sri Lankan e-commerce site selling to Europeans must comply.
- **Fines for violation are very high.**

Key terms:
- **Personal data:** any information that identifies a person (name, email, IP address, location).
- **Data subject:** the person the data is about.
- **Data controller:** the organisation that decides why and how data is processed.
- **Processing:** anything done with data (collecting, storing, using, deleting).

## 2. Seven data protection principles (lecture)
| # | Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lawfulness, fairness and transparency** | Processing must be **lawful, fair and transparent** to the data subject |
| 2 | **Purpose limitation** | Process data only for the **legitimate purposes specified** explicitly when it was collected |
| 3 | **Data minimisation** | Collect and process **only as much data as absolutely necessary** |
| 4 | **Accuracy** | Keep personal data **accurate and up to date** |
| 5 | **Storage limitation** | Store identifying data **only as long as necessary** |
| 6 | **Integrity and confidentiality** | Ensure appropriate **security, integrity and confidentiality** (e.g. **encryption**) |
| 7 | **Accountability** | The **data controller** must be able to **demonstrate GDPR compliance** with all these principles |

> [!tip] Mnemonic
> **"L-P-M-A-S-I-A"**: **L**awful **P**eople **M**inimise **A**nd **S**ecure **I**nformation **A**ccountably.

## 3. Accountability in practice (lecture)
Data controllers must demonstrate compliance:
- **Designate data-protection responsibilities** to the team.
- **Maintain detailed documentation**: what data is collected, how it is used, where it is stored, which employee is responsible.
- **Train staff** and implement **technical and organisational security measures**.
- Have **Data Processing Agreement** contracts with third parties that process data for you.
- **Appoint a Data Protection Officer** (not all organisations need one).

## 4. Data security (lecture)
Handle data securely with **"appropriate technical and organisational measures"**:
| Technical measures | Organisational measures |
|---|---|
| **Two-factor authentication** on accounts storing personal data | **Staff training** |
| Cloud providers using **end-to-end encryption** | A **data privacy policy** in the employee handbook |
| | **Limit access** to personal data to employees who need it |

**Data breach → tell the data subjects within 72 hours.**

## 5. Data protection by design and by default (lecture)
Consider the data protection principles **when designing any new product or activity**.
*Example: launching a new app.* Ask what personal data it could collect, how to **minimise** it, and what the latest technology is to **secure** it.

## 6. When is processing allowed? Lawful bases (lecture)
1. **Consent:** the data subject gave **specific, unambiguous consent** (e.g. opted in to a marketing email list).
2. **Contract:** processing is necessary to perform or prepare a contract with the subject (e.g. a background check before leasing property).
3. **Legal obligation:** e.g. a court order.
4. **Vital interests:** to **save somebody's life**.
5. **Public task:** a task in the **public interest** or an official function (e.g. a private garbage collection company).
6. **Legitimate interest:** the **most flexible** basis, but the data subject's *"fundamental rights and freedoms"* always override your interests, **especially for a child's data**.

## 7. Consent rules (lecture)
- Must be **"freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous."**
- Requests must be **"clearly distinguishable from other matters"** and in **"clear and plain language."**
- Subjects can **withdraw consent whenever they want**, and you must honour it. You **can't simply switch** to another legal basis.
- **Children under 13** can only consent **with a parent's permission**.
- Keep **documentary evidence of consent**.

```html
<!-- GDPR-friendly consent: unticked by default, specific, plain language -->
<label>
  <input type="checkbox" name="marketing">
  Send me weekly offers by email. (You can unsubscribe at any time.)
</label>
```
A **pre-ticked** box would **not** be valid consent. This links to dark patterns ([[12.06 Dark Patterns]]).

## 8. Privacy rights of data subjects (8, lecture)
1. The right to **be informed**
2. The right of **access**
3. The right to **rectification** (correct wrong data)
4. The right to **erasure** ("right to be forgotten")
5. The right to **restrict processing**
6. The right to **data portability** (get your data to move elsewhere)
7. The right to **object**
8. Rights in relation to **automated decision-making and profiling**
