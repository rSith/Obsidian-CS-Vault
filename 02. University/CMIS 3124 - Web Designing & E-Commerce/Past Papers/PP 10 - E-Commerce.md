# 2023/24 (current syllabus)

## 2023/24 · Q5(a) (2 marks)
> **Question:** What is e-commerce?

**Lesson:** [[10.01 E-Commerce Definition and Evolution]]

### Expected answer
E-commerce is **the use of electronic communications and digital information-processing technology in business transactions to create, transform and redefine relationships for value creation between or among organisations, and between organisations and individuals**. In short, any transaction completed **solely through electronic means** is e-commerce, e.g. buying a product on Daraz or Amazon, or paying for a Netflix subscription online.

---

## 2023/24 · Q5(b) (4 marks)
> **Question:** Why is authentication crucial in e-commerce? List three (03) common authentication methods.

**Lesson:** [[10.07 Authentication in E-Commerce]]

### Expected answer
**Authentication** verifies a user's identity before granting access to an online store's features or sensitive information such as payment details. It is crucial because it provides:
- **Security:** protects customer data, financial information and business systems from unauthorised access.
- **Fraud prevention:** reduces phishing, credential stuffing and payment fraud.
- **Customer trust:** customers are confident their accounts and payments are safe.
- **Non-repudiation:** users cannot deny their actions or transactions.

**Three common methods (any three):** username and password; **two-factor authentication** (e.g. a code sent to the phone); **multi-factor authentication** (know + have + are); biometrics; one-time passwords (OTP); digital certificates; keystroke dynamics.

## 2023/24 · Q5(d) (4 marks)
> **Question:** Concisely explain the benefits of e-commerce to organizations and consumers separately.

**Lesson:** [[10.02 Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce]]

### Expected answer
**To organisations:**
- **Lower costs**: less spending on rent, labour, middlemen and inventory, so the business is more cost-effective and profitable.
- **Global market reach** and easy expansion; **24×7 sales**; automated processing.
- New products are easy to launch, with **immediate customer feedback** and customer data and analytics.

**To consumers:**
- **Convenience**: shop from anywhere at any time (24×7), saving time and travel.
- **Wider choice**, easy price comparison, reviews and personalised recommendations.
- **Home delivery** and often lower prices.

# 2019/20 (exam Nov/Dec 2022)

## 2019/20 · Q1(a) (5 marks)
> **Question:** Describe the effects of e-commerce during a pandemic period.

**Lesson:** [[10.01 E-Commerce Definition and Evolution]] · [[10.02 Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce]]

### Expected answer
- **Lecture evidence:** in 2020 the COVID-19 pandemic closed physical stores and kept people at home, so people bought essentials online. IBM's US Retail Index says the shift to digital shopping was **accelerated by about five years**.
- **Positive effects:** contactless shopping and home delivery reduced infection risk; quarantined people could still get food, medicine and essentials; **24×7 convenience**; businesses kept trading and new online grocery and delivery services grew; online payments, e-government services, online learning and work-from-home tools expanded.
- **Negative effects:** delivery delays and stock-outs, price increases, **more online fraud** and scams, and exclusion of people without Internet access or cards (the digital divide).

## 2019/20 · Q1(b) (10 marks)
> **Question:** What are the effects of e-commerce in the context of buying items from foreign countries during this economic crisis time in Sri Lanka?

**Lesson:** [[10.02 Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce]] (partial coverage: applied reasoning)

### Expected answer
**Positive effects**
1. Access to goods that are **unavailable or scarce locally** during the crisis.
2. **Global market reach**: compare prices across countries and sellers.
3. Convenience of ordering from home with international delivery.

**Negative effects**
4. **Rupee depreciation** makes foreign prices much higher in LKR; shipping costs rise.
5. **Import restrictions, customs duties and taxes** add to the cost and cause delays.
6. **Limits on foreign-currency card payments** imposed by banks and the Central Bank.
7. **Outflow of scarce foreign exchange**, which hurts the national economy and local businesses.
8. **Long delivery times**, and returns or refunds across borders are difficult.
9. **No physical inspection** and **higher fraud risk** with overseas sellers; weaker consumer protection.
10. Card security matters more, so use trusted payment methods (PCI DSS-compliant gateways).

**Conclusion:** e-commerce widened choice, but the crisis made foreign purchases costly and risky. Buy only essentials, from reputable sellers.

> [!warning]
> The crisis-specific points (exchange rate, import limits, card limits) are not in the lecture. This is applied reasoning (the mapping marks it "partial").

## 2019/20 · Q1(c) (5 + 5 marks)
> **Question:** Consider the tax calculation in an e-commerce web site:
> i. Describe the effect of calculating the tax in Great Britain Pounds (GBP) for Sri Lankans during this economic crisis in Sri Lanka.
> ii. Describe the effect of calculating the tax in Sri Lankan Rupees (LKR) for foreigners during this economic crisis in Sri Lanka.

**Lesson:** none (✗ not in current notes). Applied reasoning only.

> [!warning]
> No current lecture covers tax or currency calculation. The answer below is reasoning, not course content.

### Expected answer (reasoning)
**(i) Tax in GBP for Sri Lankans:** the rupee lost much of its value against the pound, so a tax fixed in GBP becomes **much more expensive in LKR**, and the amount keeps changing with the exchange rate. Sri Lankan buyers face **unpredictable, higher total costs**, foreign-currency payment limits and bank conversion fees. Demand from Sri Lanka falls, and customers may abandon carts or switch to local sellers. The seller gets a stable value, but loses Sri Lankan customers.

**(ii) Tax in LKR for foreigners:** for foreign buyers, an LKR tax amount becomes **cheaper in their own currency** as the rupee depreciates, which makes Sri Lankan products more attractive and **boosts exports and foreign-exchange earnings**. However, the **real value of the tax revenue falls** for the seller and the government, and prices may need frequent revision because of inflation. There is also some confusion for foreigners unfamiliar with LKR, so the site should show currency conversion.

## 2019/20 · Q2(d) (10 marks), also 2018/19 · Q2(e)
> **Question:** Suppose that when you login to Gmail, in addition to the user name and password, the user has to confirm his/her identity via the phone as well. What are pros and cons in this additional feature introduced by the Gmail?

**Lesson:** [[10.07 Authentication in E-Commerce]] · [[13.10 Best Practices for Securing a Website]] · [[13.12 Zero Trust Security]]

### Expected answer
This is **2-step verification (2FA, a form of MFA)**: something you **know** (password) plus something you **have** (the phone, which receives an OTP or prompt).

**Pros**
1. **A stolen or guessed password alone is not enough** to get in, which protects against phishing, brute-force and credential-stuffing attacks.
2. **Alerts the user** to unauthorised login attempts (an unexpected prompt or code).
3. Protects **linked services** (Drive, YouTube, Google Pay) and stored personal data.
4. Gives stronger **identity verification and non-repudiation**; follows security best practice ("enable 2FA") and the Zero Trust identity pillar.
5. Builds **user trust** in the service.

**Cons**
1. **Depends on the phone**: if it is lost, stolen, has a dead battery, or has **no signal while roaming abroad**, the user is locked out.
2. **SIM-swap and SMS interception** attacks can still defeat SMS codes.
3. **Extra time and inconvenience** at every new login.
4. **Confusing for less technical users**; account recovery is harder.
5. **Backup codes** must be stored safely, and they become a new risk if lost.

**Conclusion:** the security benefits outweigh the inconvenience. An authenticator app and backup codes reduce the cons.

### How to approach this question
For 10 marks, give about **5 pros and 5 cons, each explained**. Name the concept (2FA/MFA, authentication factors) first.

## 2019/20 · Q3(a) (5 marks)
> **Question:** You do not need to put your signature in the card payment receipt when you use a debit card to pay for items you buy at some Sri Lankan supermarkets these days. Describe the pros and cons of this method.

**Lesson:** [[10.07 Authentication in E-Commerce]] (partial)

### Expected answer
**Pros:** faster checkout and shorter queues; less paper; **chip + PIN or contactless verification** is more secure than a signature, which cashiers rarely check; convenient for customers.
**Cons:** weaker proof of the customer's consent (**non-repudiation**) in disputes; if the PIN is observed or the card is contactless, a thief can pay **without any signature check**; customers may not check the amount before leaving.
**Link to authentication factors:** the signature (something you do) is replaced by the PIN (something you **know**) plus the card (something you **have**).

## 2019/20 · Q3(b) (5 marks)
> **Question:** There are two options when you order items via online: 1) pay on delivery and 2) online payments. Describe the pros and cons of pay on delivery over online payments.

**Lesson:** [[10.06 Shopping Carts]] (payment processing) · [[10.02 Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce]] (fraud)

### Expected answer
**Pros of pay on delivery**
- **No need to share card details online**, so there is no risk of card theft or phishing.
- Pay **only when the item arrives**, which builds trust with new or unknown sellers.
- Usable by people **without bank cards**.

**Cons of pay on delivery**
- Must handle **cash** and have the exact amount; physical contact (a concern in a pandemic).
- **Higher risk and cost for sellers**: fake orders, refused deliveries, cash handling; sometimes a COD fee is added.
- Slower processing; you still often **can't inspect the item before paying**.

**Lesson:** [[10.06 Shopping Carts]]

## 2019/20 · Q3(d) (7 marks)
> **Question:** Suppose you ordered an item via online and paid fully for the delivery as well. But at the delivery, the delivery person requested you to meet them part way through to collect the item. Discuss your reaction to their request.

**Lesson:** [[10.11 PCI DSS and Consumer Rights]] · [[10.09 E-Commerce Ethics and Ethical Principles]]

### Expected answer
- **I paid for delivery to my address**, so the request **breaches the agreed service**. This is a question of **fairness** (e-commerce ethics) and of my **consumer right to redress** for unsatisfactory service.
- **Reaction:** politely refuse and ask for delivery as agreed. If I agree for convenience, I would consider **personal safety** (meeting a stranger), **time and extra cost**.
- **Record evidence:** order number, time, messages or calls.
- **Complain** to the seller or courier and **request a refund of the delivery fee** or compensation; leave an honest review.
- If unresolved, **escalate to the Consumer Affairs Authority** (right to be heard and right to redress).

## 2019/20 · Q4(a) (8 marks)
> **Question:** A shop that sells only electronic items has a customer area and four counters to collect orders of its customers. All the packed items are stored inside a room. At the customer area, there are 4 computers and 1 printed catalogue by the side of each computer. Customers may use the printed catalogues or the computer to check the items' specifications, available quantity, price, etc. and customer may use a paper slip available at the shop to order any items. Customers will be given a token when they submit the slip (order) and complete the payment. When the packing of ordered items is ready, it will be delivered to the relevant customer by checking the token. Discuss the pros and cons of this method.

**Lesson:** [[10.02 Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce]] · [[10.06 Shopping Carts]]

### Expected answer
**Pros**
- Customers see **accurate specifications, stock and prices** quickly on the computers.
- They can **physically inspect** and **collect items immediately** (an advantage of traditional commerce).
- **Tokens** give an orderly queue; staff pick items efficiently from one store room, which reduces theft and clutter on the shop floor.
- Suits customers **without Internet access** or cards; human help is available.

**Cons**
- **Manual paper slips** cause **human errors** and slow processing (traditional-commerce weakness: manual vs automated).
- **Only 4 computers** cause queues at busy times; customers must **travel** and the shop has **limited opening hours**.
- **No online payment, home delivery or 24×7 access**; paper waste; customers can't check stock before visiting.

**Improvement:** add an **online shopping cart with order status** and click-and-collect, so customers order from home and simply pick up.

## 2019/20 · Q4(b) (8 marks)
> **Question:** "National Express" of United Kingdom issues an online ticket worth £2 to travel by bus from the city of Bath to London, if you book the online ticket two weeks prior to the travel. You need to pay £20 if you directly go to the bus station on the date of travel to buy the ticket for the same travel. Explain the impact of these two methods.

**Lesson:** [[10.02 Traditional Commerce vs E-Commerce]]

### Expected answer
**Online advance booking (£2):**
- E-commerce is **cost-effective**: online booking reduces counter staff and processing costs, and the saving is passed on to customers.
- Early sales let the company **forecast demand**, plan buses and **fill seats** (dynamic pricing).
- Customers who plan ahead save greatly and enjoy **24×7 booking** and e-tickets; it encourages people to move online.

**Station purchase (£20):**
- Serves walk-in and last-minute travellers who need flexibility, at a premium.
- Covers the higher cost of counter service.

**Wider impact:** a **fairness and digital-divide** issue: people without Internet access or cards, and those with emergencies, pay **ten times more**. Online tickets may be **non-refundable or inflexible**. Overall, the pricing pushes customers to e-commerce and benefits the company.

## 2019/20 · Q8(c) (4 marks)
> **Question:** It is advised not to save credit card details for online payment in future. Describe the consequences of saving your credit card details that way.

**Lesson:** [[10.11 PCI DSS and Consumer Rights]] · [[13.06 Spyware]]

### Expected answer
- If the merchant's **database is breached**, stored card numbers can be **stolen and misused**. (PCI DSS requirement 3, "protect stored cardholder data", exists because stored data is a target.)
- **Anyone using your account or device** (a shared computer, a stolen phone, a hijacked session) can make purchases.
- **Spyware, data harvesters and phishing** can capture account credentials and then use the saved card.
- You **depend on the merchant's security** standards, and fraudulent charges lead to **time-consuming disputes**.
- *Balance:* saving is convenient (faster checkout), but it is safer to use tokenised wallets and 2FA/OTP for each payment.

## 2018/19 · Q3(c)
> **Question:** Suppose that there is a travel agent who provides you cheap air tickets. You need to send him the photocopy of your credit card for making the payment. Describe the advantages and disadvantages clearly in this method.

**Lesson:** [[10.11 PCI DSS and Consumer Rights]] · [[13.04 Phishing]]

### Expected answer
**Advantages:** cheaper tickets; convenient, with no need to visit; the agent books quickly on your behalf.
**Disadvantages:** the photocopy exposes the **card number, name and expiry date (and the CVV if the back is copied)**, so anyone who handles it can commit **card-not-present fraud**. It goes against **PCI DSS** principles (protect cardholder data, restrict access on a need-to-know basis); there is no audit trail; it may be a **phishing scam**; and disputes are hard to prove.
**Safer alternative:** pay through a **secure payment gateway (HTTPS)** with OTP, or use a virtual card.

## Features of a good e-commerce website & selling on eBay (2018/19 Q4(b),(d) = 2017/18 Q2(b),(d))

## 2017/18 · Q3(d)
> **Question:** Suppose that you have paid a bill at a shop using your debit card. Next day only you noticed that your card number was printed in the bill. i) Describe the issues with this situation. ii) Explain the actions to be taken at this situation.

**Lesson:** [[10.11 PCI DSS and Consumer Rights]]

### Expected answer
**(i) Issues:** anyone who sees the bill (staff, someone who finds it in a bin, a lost receipt) gets the **full card number** to use in fraud; the merchant is **breaking PCI DSS** (receipts should mask all but the last 4 digits; requirement 3, protect stored cardholder data); there is an identity-theft risk.
**(ii) Actions:** keep or destroy the bill securely; **monitor transactions**; **inform the bank** (block or reissue the card if needed) and **the merchant**; report to the consumer authority (right to be heard, right to redress).
