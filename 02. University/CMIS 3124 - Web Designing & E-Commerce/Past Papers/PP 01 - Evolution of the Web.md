## 2023/24 · Q1(d) (8 marks)

> **Question:** Describe how the nature and structure of the Web, as well as the way we use it, have been continuously evolving.

**Lesson:** [[01.02 Web 1.0 - The Static Web]] · [[01.03 Web 2.0 - The Participatory Web]] · [[01.09 Web 2.5 and Web 3.0]] · [[01.10 Web 4.0 and the Future Internet]]

### Expected answer
The Web has moved through several stages. At each stage, users gained more control and the technology underneath became more distributed and more intelligent.

**Web 1.0, the static web (early 1990s to early 2000s).** Websites were static information portals that offered content without interactivity. Users could only read. Bandwidth limits meant few images or videos, and search used simple directory-like engines such as AltaVista.

**Web 2.0, the participatory web.** Users actively create and share content (user-generated content) through blogs, wikis and social media. The focus is on collaboration, usability and interoperability. Rich web applications (Gmail, YouTube), service integration, sharing tools, multi-device support and easy subscriptions (RSS) appeared. This fuelled e-commerce (personalised shopping, peer reviews), business marketing and online education, built on cloud applications and APIs.

**Web 2.5, a transitional phase.** It keeps Web 2.0's social, user-generated content but adds some decentralisation: blockchain, tokenised ecosystems (NFTs) and better data privacy. It is a hybrid stepping stone towards Web 3.0.

**Web 3.0, the decentralised web.** It emphasises user control, privacy and peer-to-peer interaction without central authorities. Advanced encryption, smart contracts, DeFi, tokenised assets and user-owned data mean that users can own and monetise their data and digital assets.

**Web 4.0, the symbiotic web.** AI, machine learning and IoT devices converge so that the web becomes predictive, context-aware and able to make real-time decisions. It offers hyper-personalisation and voice, gesture and brain-computer interfaces, with uses such as smart cities, healthcare monitoring and self-driving vehicles.

**How our use has changed:** from reading pages on a desktop → creating and sharing on mobile devices → owning digital assets → interacting with intelligent, connected systems. The future points to hyper-personalisation, ubiquitous IoT, an intelligent/emotional web, decentralisation at scale, AR/VR and the metaverse, a quantum internet, and sustainable, ethical technology.

### Key points to include
- All five eras, each with **name, period/idea and an example**
- The **user-role trend**: read → write → own → interact
- At least 2–3 future directions

### How to approach this question
Write one short paragraph per era (about 1.5 marks each) and finish with a sentence on the trend. Use the lecture's labels: "static web", "participatory", "decentralised", "symbiotic".

## 2019/20 · Q6(b): Short notes (4 marks each, "4×5 marks")

> **Question:** Write short notes on the following terms: i. Blogs ii. Wikipedia iii. Mashups iv. Web2.0 v. Social Bookmarking

**Lesson:** [[01.04 Blogs]] · [[01.05 Wikis]] · [[01.06 Mashups]] · [[01.03 Web 2.0 - The Participatory Web]] · [[01.08 Social Bookmarking]]

### Expected answers

**(i) Blogs.** Short for "web log". A blog is a website where people enter thoughts, ideas, suggestions and comments. It is a powerful two-way web-based communication tool: the author posts and readers comment, so the author gets feedback. Blogs can be private (internal to an organisation) or public. Individuals use them for opinions and stories; businesses use them for news, marketing and customer engagement. Posts usually appear newest first and can be syndicated with RSS.

**(ii) Wikipedia.** Wikipedia is the best-known example of a wiki, a web-based collaborative authoring (content management) system. Anyone can create a new article or revise and edit existing ones using just a web browser. It is simple, easy to use and powerful for collaborative work: a free online encyclopedia written by volunteers. A revision history allows errors to be reverted, but because anyone can edit, information should be verified.

**(iii) Mashups.** A mashup is a web page or site that combines information and services from multiple sources on the Web. A mashup server connects, collects and mashes up web data and back-end data, typically through APIs/web services (e.g. PHP `file()`). Examples: HousingMaps.com (craigslist listings on Google Maps) and Fishing Solutions (Google Maps + anglers' information). Uses: new business applications, employee-built value-added apps, better decision making, CRM, payroll, logistics and e-commerce.

**(iv) Web 2.0.** Web 2.0 is the participatory (read-write) web, where users actively create and share content (user-generated content) on dynamic platforms such as blogs, wikis and social media. It emphasises collaboration, usability and interoperability. Features: rich web apps (Gmail, YouTube), service integration and APIs, sharing tools, multi-device support and subscriptions (RSS). Impact: personalised e-commerce with peer reviews, business marketing, online education, and cloud-based applications.

**(v) Social Bookmarking.** A browser-based function to save, sort and share webpage links digitally, through a dedicated platform or a browser extension. Users organise bookmarks with folders, categories and tags. Because bookmarks are stored online, they are available on any device. Social features include re-posting, commenting and following users or tags, and algorithms suggest related tags and users. Tracking popular sources reveals trends, which boosts traffic and SERP visibility.

### How to approach this question
Each note needs **about 4 points**: definition → key feature → how it is used → example.

## 2018/19 · Q5(a)

> **Question:** i) Compare and contrast Blog and Wikipedia. ii) Describe the advantages of Social Bookmarking. iii) What is meant by Mashup and what are the uses of it?

**Lesson:** [[01.04 Blogs]] · [[01.05 Wikis]] · [[01.08 Social Bookmarking]] · [[01.06 Mashups]]

### Expected answer

**(i) Blog vs Wikipedia**

| | Blog | Wikipedia (wiki) |
|---|---|---|
| Authorship | One author or a small team | Written collaboratively by anyone |
| Reader's role | Can only **comment** | Can **edit** the article itself |
| Purpose/style | Personal opinions, news, dated posts; two-way communication | Neutral encyclopedic articles organised by topic |
| Access | Private or public | Public, with revision history |

*Similarities:* both are Web 2.0 tools with user-generated content, are published and read through a browser, and are free to use.

**(ii) Advantages of social bookmarking:** access bookmarks from anywhere on any device (stored online); organise them with tags, folders and categories; share links and discover sources through other users; real-time trends and algorithm suggestions; site owners get more traffic and better SERP visibility.

**(iii) Mashup:** a page or site that combines data and services from several web sources into a new application (e.g. HousingMaps). **Uses:** create new core business apps; knit services together with a rich UI; let employees build apps quickly without waiting for IT; mix data in unexpected ways; support internal decisions and give executives insight into customers and sales; payroll, CRM, logistics, procurement, marketing, e-commerce.

### How to approach this question
For "compare and contrast", use a **table plus one line of similarities**.

## 2017/18 · Q8(d)

> **Question:** You may find several free web services at present. Describe a suitable service / method, with justification, for publishing following articles:
> i) "Scientific facts followed in Hinduism". You wish to get this article edited by people who have good knowledge on this topic.
> ii) "Ghost and me" which is a fictitious story. You wish to get more comments from readers to enhance your writing skill.
> iii) "Specific Absorption Rate (SAR) Distribution by the Human Eye Due to Mobile Base Station Antennas in Sri Lanka". This is a pure scientific article with the results of some standard experiments. You wish other researchers to do more researches on this topic.

**Lesson:** [[01.05 Wikis]] · [[01.04 Blogs]] · [[01.08 Social Bookmarking]] · [[01.07 RSS Feeds]]

### Expected answer
- **(i) Wiki.** Knowledgeable people can add to, correct and edit the article collaboratively using only a browser, and the revision history keeps track of changes.
- **(ii) Blog.** A two-way communication tool: readers leave comments that give feedback and help improve writing skill.
- **(iii) Social bookmarking and/or RSS** (or a wiki). Researchers can save, tag, share and follow updates. Bookmark trends raise visibility on search results, so more researchers find and build on the work.

### How to approach this question
State the **service + the specific feature that matches the stated goal** (edit / comment / further research).
